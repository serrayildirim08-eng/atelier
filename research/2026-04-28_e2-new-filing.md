## 1. The "In Process of Investing" Doctrine — Walsh & Pollard

### 1.1 The case (FACT, high confidence)

**Citation.** *Matter of Walsh and Pollard*, 20 I&N Dec. 60 (BIA 1988) (Interim Decision #3111). Justice.gov EOIR Volume 20 publishes the full opinion at `justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2012/08/14/3111.pdf` (retrieved 2026-04-28; PDF binary, text not parsed by agent — see methodology note).

**Facts the BIA credited as qualifying as "in the process of investing"** (corroborated by Berardi Immigration Law, Visa Business Plans, and AILA secondary; retrieved 2026-04-28):

- IAD Ltd. (a UK automotive design company) was contracted by GM to redesign GM's car line.
- IAD created a U.S. subsidiary specifically to perform the contract.
- **Concrete pre-revenue acts the Board credited:** rented office space; purchased office furniture; hired two U.S. citizens to assist UK designers in their relocation; established a corporate bank account funded with **USD 15,000**.
- The BIA held this satisfied the "actively in the process of investing" prong, and that the design engineers — though not in supervisory or managerial roles — were essential employees and qualified for E-2.

**Walsh & Pollard holdings relevant to E-2 NEW filings:**
1. There is **no fixed dollar minimum** for E-2 substantiality. The "amount normally considered necessary to establish a viable enterprise of the nature contemplated" is the test. (FACT, high confidence — restated identically across all secondary sources retrieved 2026-04-28.)
2. A foreign treaty national must have **either** invested **or** be **actively in the process of investing**. The "process of investing" prong is alive and well — but it requires more than intention. (FACT, high confidence.)
3. The Board treated **lease + furniture + initial hires + funded operating account** as qualifying acts, even though the U.S. subsidiary had earned no revenue at the time of the visa adjudication. (FACT, high confidence.)

### 1.2 What "irrevocably committed" requires (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(B))

**Citation.** 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) (`fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html`) (retrieved 2026-04-28; live URL returned a TLS error from the agent's fetch — quoted language confirmed across U.S. Embassy Chile's republication, e2visalawyer.net's PDF mirror, and Beyond Border Global summary, all retrieved 2026-04-28). FAM-quoted passages flagged `[primary text not parsed — corroborated by ≥3 secondary]`:

- "An investment conditioned upon the issuance of the E-2 visa **may still qualify** if the assets are held in escrow." Despite the condition, the purchase constitutes a "solid commitment" if the assets are held in escrow for release or transfer once the condition is met. (FACT, high confidence; corroborated by 3 secondary sources.)
- The funds must be (i) the investor's possession, (ii) committed, (iii) at risk in the commercial sense, with possibility of partial or total loss. (FACT, high confidence.)
- **"Mere intent to invest, or possession of uncommitted funds in a bank account, is not enough."** This is the bright line — and is the language that defeats most weak E-2 NEW filings. (FACT, high confidence per all FAM republications retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 1.3 How the doctrine reads in 2025-26 (PATTERN, medium confidence)

Practitioners (Visa Business Plans, U.S. Immigration Plan, Beyond Border Global, all retrieved 2026-04-28) converge on three operational tests for "irrevocably committed" in 2025-26 consular practice:

1. **Spent.** Cancelled checks, wire confirmations, paid invoices for premises build-out, equipment, inventory, franchise fees, retainers. *(Strongest.)*
2. **Escrowed with restricted release.** Funds in a written escrow that release **only** on visa issuance and which (a) commit to a specific named transaction (lease assumption, asset purchase, share purchase) and (b) limit any clawback strictly to a denial event — **not** "any reason" withdrawal. *(Acceptable if written tightly; see §4.)*
3. **Letter of Intent (LOI) / pending purchase agreement.** Weakest. Generally insufficient on its own per consular practice 2025-26.

**FAM-anchored takeaway for E-2 NEW.** Walsh & Pollard authorizes a pre-revenue posture, but only if the file shows the kind of concrete deployment that put the *Walsh* applicants over the line: a leased premises, paid-for tangible setup, and at least one operating-account act. A bank balance plus a business plan, with nothing else, will not survive 9 FAM 402.9-6(B).

## 2. Pre-Launch Evidence Package (No Revenue Yet)

This is the central E-2 NEW scenario the renewal manual does not handle.

### 2.1 What substitutes for "operating history"

When the business is formed, funds are deployed (lease signed and paid, equipment purchased, build-out underway), but no customers exist yet, the file must reconstruct an "operational" picture **out of deployment evidence**:

| Renewal exhibit (Kacar-Salih shape) | E-2 NEW substitute (pre-launch) |
|---|---|
| 12+ months of business bank statements | Petitioner operating account opened + initial capital deposits + first vendor outflows (≥3 months ideal, ≥1 month minimum) |
| Federal tax return (G.6, APS 5) | EIN assignment letter (CP-575) **+** state sales-tax / employer registration **+** if the business has been operating <12 months at filing, partial-year P&L or unaudited interim financials |
| Payroll records / W-2s (G.7) | Offer letters with start dates, payroll-provider contract (e.g., Gusto / ADP setup confirmation), I-9-ready new-hire packet, **hiring-timetable schedule** in the business plan |
| Photos of operating premises (G.1) | Photos of leased premises during build-out: dated, with progress (demo → rough-in → finish), plus general-contractor agreement and progress invoices |
| Vendor invoices showing recurring procurement (G.5) | One-time setup invoices: equipment purchase orders, POS / merchant-processor onboarding, insurance binder, opening inventory order |
| Merchant processing statements | Merchant-account application + approval letter (Stripe / Square / bank merchant) and processor agreement |

(PATTERN, high confidence — converges across Pollak PLLC, Gozel Law, Pass Right, Alaz Law, Lighthouse HQ E-2 guides, all retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 2.2 Mandatory pre-launch exhibits (FACT, high confidence)

The minimum pre-launch evidence package consistently described across practitioner sources (≥3-source rule met; retrieved 2026-04-28):

1. **Articles of Organization / Incorporation** (state-filed; same as renewal Tab D.1).
2. **Operating Agreement** — naming Beneficiary and showing executive role from day one.
3. **EIN assignment letter (IRS CP-575)** — anchors federal recognition of the entity.
4. **Commercial lease, fully executed** — with rent paid through filing date, plus paid invoices for security deposit + first month + any prepaid CAM. The lease, **plus proof of payment**, is the single strongest non-revenue at-risk exhibit.
5. **Build-out contract or franchise development agreement** with **progress payments shown** — invoiced, paid, and dated.
6. **Equipment purchase orders + paid invoices + delivery proof** (bills of lading or delivery receipts).
7. **Insurance binder** — general liability, property, workers' comp once first hire is set.
8. **State / local licensing applications and permits** — where issuance precedes opening (food service, liquor, professional licenses, daycare, etc.).
9. **Merchant account onboarding documents** — signed application, approval letter.
10. **Hiring file** — at least one signed offer letter with concrete start date prior to or shortly after admission, plus payroll provider contract.
11. **Business plan** (5-year, Matter-of-Ho-compliant — see §3).
12. **Source-of-funds memo + chain table** — same discipline as Tab E in renewal manual, but generally higher-volume because the deployment is the only at-risk proof.
13. **Photographs** — premises in current state, with date-stamps; equipment in place.
14. **Beneficiary CV + executive role appointment** — same as renewal Tab H, but emphasizes industry track record substituting for absent operating history.

### 2.3 Practitioner playbook — the "open-on-day-of-admission" posture (PATTERN, medium-high confidence)

Across U.S. Immigration Plan, Pollak PLLC, and Pass Right (retrieved 2026-04-28), the recommended posture for an initial filing where launch has not yet occurred is:

- File should describe the business as **"immediately capable of commencing operations upon Beneficiary's admission."**
- Cover-letter language should pair this phrase with citation to 9 FAM 402.9-6(D) (bona fide enterprise / real and operating) and Walsh & Pollard's "in the process of investing" prong.
- Hiring timetable (item 10 above) must be specific: month, role, FT/PT, target wage band — **NOT** "as needed."

**Confidence note.** Practitioner sources are unanimous here — but this is a doctrine of practice, not regulation. Marked PATTERN.

## 3. The 5-Year Business Plan for E-2 NEW

### 3.1 The Matter of Ho standard (FACT, high confidence)

**Citation.** *Matter of Ho*, 22 I&N Dec. 206 (Assoc. Comm'r 1998) (justice.gov EOIR Volume 22, retrieved 2026-04-28; primary text not parsed by agent — see methodology note). Quoted standard reproduced consistently across Joorney, Visa Business Plans, Plan Writers, EB5 Insights (all retrieved 2026-04-28):

> A petitioner must submit "a comprehensive business plan" that is "**comprehensive, detailed, and credible**." A "comprehensive business plan as contemplated by the regulations should contain, at a minimum, a description of the business's products and/or services, its objectives, market analysis, including the names of competing businesses and their relative strengths and weaknesses, a comparison of the competition's products and pricing structures, a description of the target market/prospective customers of the new commercial enterprise, a description of any manufacturing or production processes, materials required, and supply sources, details of any contracts executed for the supply of materials and/or the distribution of products, a description of marketing strategy including pricing, advertising, and servicing, a description of the business's organizational structure and its personnel's experience, **a staffing timetable**, and **job descriptions for all positions**, including a specification of those positions that will be held by qualifying employees… as well as **sales, cost, and income projections** and details of the **bases therefor**." (Quoted via Joorney's reproduction of *Ho*, retrieved 2026-04-28.)

Although *Ho* is an EB-5 case, the standard is **applied by analogy to E-2 NEW** because the business plan plays the same evidentiary role: it is the document from which the adjudicator infers viability, capacity to support more than the investor's family (E-2 marginality), and the develop-and-direct strategy. Practitioner consensus is unanimous on this analogical use (Visa Business Plans, Joorney, Pass Right, Alaz Law, retrieved 2026-04-28). FACT, high confidence on the analogical use; FACT, high confidence on the *Ho* elements.

### 3.2 Sections that must absolutely be in an E-2 NEW plan

Synthesized minimum (PATTERN, high confidence; ≥3-source rule met, retrieved 2026-04-28):

1. **Executive summary** — investor, role, investment, business model, 5-year hiring count, 5-year revenue.
2. **Industry analysis** — capital intensity, growth rate, regulatory environment. Footnote every assumption to a primary source.
3. **Market analysis** — TAM/SAM/SOM with footnoted demographics. For local-services businesses, county-level Census data + BLS QCEW + drive-time analysis.
4. **Competitive analysis** — named competitors, SWOT.
5. **Operations** — premises, suppliers, hours, processes, technology stack.
6. **Marketing & sales strategy** — channels with cost.
7. **Organizational chart + staffing timetable + job descriptions** — see §3.4.
8. **Capital deployment table** — line by line, tied to the SOF chain.
9. **5-year financial projections** — P&L, cash flow, balance sheet. Year 1 monthly, Year 2 quarterly, Years 3-5 annual (PATTERN, U.S. Immigration Plan + Alaz Law + Pass Right, retrieved 2026-04-28).
10. **Assumption appendix** — every revenue, COGS, and labor assumption footnoted.

### 3.3 Common failure patterns ("Matter of Ho paper plan") — what defeats them

The "paper plan" failure mode — a glossy deck with no underlying support — fails *Ho*'s "credible" prong. Concrete failure patterns:

| Failure pattern | What kills the plan | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| Hockey-stick revenue without footnoted assumptions | Adjudicator marks "speculative" | Footnote each assumption to IBISWorld / BLS QCEW / FRED / Statista / FDD Item 19 with retrieval date |
| "We will hire as needed" or "5 employees by Year 5" with no schedule | Marginality denial | Month-by-month staffing timetable Year 1; quarterly Y2; annual Y3-5 — each line tied to a job description |
| Stock industry ratios (e.g., "restaurant labor 30%") with no source | Loss of credibility | Cite IBISWorld report number, FDD Item 19 if franchise, or industry trade-association data |
| Identical FDD-replicated language for franchise plans | "Stock business plan" rejection — see §6 | Customize with locale-specific demographics, drive-time analysis, named local competitors |
| No customer-acquisition cost model | Plan can't support revenue ramp | CAC × volume = marketing spend; tied to channel mix |
| Reliance on independent contractors / "1099 only" | 2026 marginality red flag — see §3.4 | At least 3-5 W-2 hires by Y5; first within 12-18 months |
| No reinvestment line | Looks like personal-living plan | Show retained-earnings reinvestment — explicit line in cash-flow |

(PATTERN, high confidence; converges across U.S. Immigration Plan, Alaz Law, Pass Right, Pandev, ALG Lawyers — all retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 3.4 W-2 hiring timetable — 2025-26 RFE language

**FACT (medium-high confidence; PATTERN at consular post level).** Practitioner reports for 2025-26 (U.S. Immigration Plan, Pass Right, Alaz Law, retrieved 2026-04-28):

- Adjudicators expect **first W-2 hire within 12–18 months** of operations.
- Plans relying on gig-workers / 1099s are flagged.
- A defensible target band is **3–5 W-2 FTEs by Year 5**, more for capital-intensive industries.
- Each hire must have: title, FT/PT, anticipated wage band, month of hire, sourcing channel, prerequisite credentials.

> Cover-letter snippet pattern (drafter-ready, derived from Akalan voice corpus — to be added to the manual):
> "The Petitioner's five-year staffing plan, set forth in the enclosed business plan, projects an initial W-2 hire of `<<role>>` in `<<Month YY>>` at a wage band of `$<<low>>–<<high>>` per annum, with subsequent additions of `<<role>>` in `<<Month YY>>` and `<<role>>` in `<<Month YY>>`. Year-five projected headcount is `<<N>>` W-2 employees, demonstrating that the enterprise is not — and will not be — a marginal operation supporting only the Beneficiary and his family. (Exhibit: Business Plan §`<<X>>`; Exhibit: Job Descriptions Schedule.)"

### 3.5 Industry assumption sourcing (FACT, high confidence)

Sources practitioners cite as primary for E-2 NEW assumption footnoting:

- **IBISWorld** — sector revenue, growth, profitability ratios. Cite report number and date.
- **BLS QCEW (Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages)** — county-level wage benchmarks for staffing-cost defense.
- **FRED (St. Louis Fed)** — macro indicators, regional labor cost indices.
- **Statista** — supplementary, only for consumer-facing metrics not in BLS.
- **FDD Item 19 (Financial Performance Representations)** — for franchise cases only; cite Item 19 page and FDD year.
- **U.S. Census Bureau ACS** — demographics for market analysis.
- **State and county economic development authorities** — sometimes provide free trade-area studies.

Footnote format the agent should enforce: `IBISWorld Report 72211, "Limited-Service Restaurants in the U.S.," September 2025, p. 14 (retrieved 2026-04-28)`.

## 4. Funds Escrow vs. Funds Spent — The At-Risk Distinction

### 4.1 The hierarchy practitioners use (PATTERN, high confidence)

From U.S. Immigration Plan (Feb 2026), Beyond Border Global, Visa Business Plans, e2visalawyer.net (all retrieved 2026-04-28). Three-source rule met:

```
SPENT  >  ESCROWED (denial-only release)  >  ESCROWED (general clawback)  >  LOI
```

- **SPENT** is always strongest. Cancelled checks, wires, paid invoices.
- **ESCROW with denial-only clawback** is acceptable per FAM (see §1.2) when the agreement (a) names the specific transaction, (b) restricts release to visa-issuance / admission, and (c) restricts clawback to denial.
- **ESCROW with "any-reason clawback"** is increasingly rejected as speculative (PATTERN, multiple practitioner sources, 2025-26).
- **LOI / unsigned PSA** is generally insufficient.

### 4.2 Escrow language: acceptable vs. unacceptable (PATTERN, high confidence)

**Acceptable language pattern** (synthesized from FAM-aligned practitioner descriptions, retrieved 2026-04-28):

> "Funds shall be released from escrow to Seller upon issuance of an E-2 visa to Buyer **OR** upon Buyer's admission to the United States in E-2 status, whichever first occurs. Funds shall be returned to Buyer **only if** an E-2 visa application filed within `<<X>>` days of this Agreement is denied by the U.S. Department of State, after exhaustion of any motion to reconsider. No other event triggers a refund."

**Unacceptable / risky** (recent rejection pattern):

- "Buyer may rescind for any reason."
- "Refund upon failure to close for any cause."
- "Termination at Buyer's sole discretion."

(PATTERN, medium-high confidence; flagged by U.S. Immigration Plan and several escrow-focused practitioner blogs in 2025-26.)

### 4.3 The "spent > escrowed > LOI" defensive paragraph

Drafter snippet to add to the manual when not all funds are spent:

> "Of the total committed investment of `$<<Total>>`, `$<<Spent>>` has been irrevocably deployed and is documented by paid invoices, executed lease, and equipment delivery (Exhibit: …). The balance of `$<<Escrowed>>` is held in a written escrow whose release is restricted to issuance of an E-2 visa or admission of the Beneficiary in E-2 status, with refund permitted only upon denial of the visa application — i.e., the limited contingency expressly contemplated by 9 FAM 402.9-6(B). No portion of the funds may be withdrawn for any other reason."

## 5. Source-of-Funds Memorandum for E-2 NEW

### 5.1 What the SOF memo looks like (PATTERN, high confidence)

The renewal manual already covers SOF with rigor (Tab E reconstruction). For E-2 NEW, the same discipline applies, **plus** these additions specific to no-prior-history:

1. **Chronological narrative.** A 1-3 page memo, written for a non-financial reader (the consular officer), narrating: "Beneficiary accumulated funds from `<<source 1>>` between `<<dates>>`; then `<<source 2>>`; consolidated into account `<<>>` on `<<date>>`; converted to USD on `<<date>>`; wired to U.S. on `<<date>>`; deployed to Petitioner on `<<date>>`."
2. **Exhibit-citation discipline.** Every dollar movement in the narrative ends with `(Exhibit `<<E.X.x>>`)`.
3. **Tax-return support.** For income-source claims, attach the home-country tax returns covering the accumulation years. Where tax returns are not standard (some treaty countries), attach a CPA certification letter explaining the local norm.
4. **Lawful-origin certification.** A signed Beneficiary affidavit attesting to lawful origin with subjects to penalty of perjury under 28 USC 1746.

### 5.2 Tier scaling (PATTERN, medium confidence)

Practitioner consensus on scrutiny tiers (Colombo Hurd, Scott Legal, U.S. Immigration Plan, Alaz Law, retrieved 2026-04-28):

| Tier | Investment | Expected SOF rigor | Expected substantiality posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | ≤ $100,000 | Heightened — every dollar traced; smaller accumulation pool means less room for unexplained gaps | Argue qualitatively (Walsh & Pollard) — match enterprise need; service-only businesses under ~$80K flagged as thin |
| Mid | $100,000 – $500,000 | Standard — full chain; ≥75–90% expected as a percentage of enterprise cost | Standard inverted-sliding-scale narrative |
| High | $500,000+ | Standard rigor on origin, but proportionality threshold drops (large absolute dollar) | Substantiality argued primarily in absolute terms |

This is a 2025-26 practitioner taxonomy. The FAM does not codify these tiers; they are PATTERN.

### 5.3 Cryptocurrency as source — 2025-26 consular requirements (FACT/PATTERN mix, high confidence on documentation; medium on outcomes)

From Tondini, Oguz, Alaz Law, e2visalawyer.net (all retrieved 2026-04-28):

Required documentary chain:

1. **Initial fiat purchase records** — bank statement showing USD/EUR/TRY → exchange.
2. **Exchange KYC pack** — onboarding KYC records (ID verification, address, source-of-funds questionnaire submitted to the exchange).
3. **Full transaction logs** — every trade, every hop. Exchange-issued CSV / API export.
4. **Wallet-to-wallet blockchain IDs** — block explorer screenshots tying transactions to the named addresses.
5. **Liquidation records** — exchange records of crypto → fiat conversion immediately preceding deployment.
6. **U.S. bank-side ingestion records** — wire from exchange / off-ramp → applicant's U.S. bank → Petitioner.
7. **Tax compliance** — home-country and (if applicable) U.S. tax return showing crypto reporting.

> "If this exhaustive ledger cannot be provided, the application will likely be denied." (PATTERN, multiple sources retrieved 2026-04-28.)

**Drafter snippet (firm voice, to be added):**
> "The Beneficiary's investment funds derive in part from lawful gains realized through the trading of digital assets on the regulated exchange `<<Exchange>>`, which performs Know-Your-Customer verification on all account holders. The complete trade ledger, the corresponding blockchain transaction identifiers, and the final fiat-conversion records are enclosed (Exhibits `<<E.x.x>>` through `<<E.x.y>>`). Each transaction has been reported on the Beneficiary's `<<year>>` `<<jurisdiction>>` tax return."

## 6. Franchise E-2 NEW Scenario

### 6.1 FDD Item 7 vs. actual deployed (PATTERN, high confidence)

**Item 7 of the FDD** is the franchisor's estimate of total initial investment cost. Item 7 is a **range** (often wide). Practitioner discipline (Pollak PLLC, Joorney, Internicola, Franchisee Lawyer, all retrieved 2026-04-28):

- The E-2 file's deployed-funds total must **align with the high end of Item 7** for a same-territory unit, **or** the cover letter must explain the deviation (e.g., "Petitioner has elected the higher-amenities buildout").
- A deployed amount **below** the FDD low end is a substantiality red flag.
- An LOI for a franchise unit, without paid franchise fee + paid build-out draws, is the weakest possible posture.

### 6.2 The "stock business plan" rejection pattern (PATTERN, medium-high confidence)

Sources do not use the literal phrase "stock business plan rejection" as a doctrinal term, but the rejection pattern is consistently described:

- A plan that reproduces verbatim the franchisor's marketing deck or the FDD Item 19 numbers without locale-specific adjustment is criticized as failing *Ho*'s "credible" prong.
- The defense is to commission an independent business plan (Joorney / Visa Business Plans / Wise Business Plans / similar) that **uses** the FDD Item 19 numbers as one input but layers in: locale demographics, drive-time analysis, named local competitors, locale-specific labor rates from BLS QCEW, and a CAC model.

(PATTERN, medium-high confidence; converges across Joorney, Pollak PLLC, Internicola, Visa Business Plans, retrieved 2026-04-28. No primary BIA/AAO precedent retrieved using the specific phrase; rejection is described at the practitioner / RFE level.)

### 6.3 Anti-restrictive-franchise paragraph — the "develop and direct" defense

The threat: a franchise agreement specifying centralized marketing, mandated suppliers, mandatory territories, fixed pricing, fixed signage — all of which can be argued by USCIS as defeating develop-and-direct.

The defense (PATTERN, high confidence; Pollak, Joorney, Internicola, Franchiselawsolutions.com, retrieved 2026-04-28):

> Cover-letter paragraph (drafter snippet, to be added):
> "While the Petitioner operates as a franchisee under the Franchise Agreement enclosed herewith (Exhibit: …), the Beneficiary retains and exercises full executive control over those functions that constitute developing and directing the enterprise within the meaning of 9 FAM 402.9-7. Specifically, the Beneficiary determines and controls: local hiring and termination decisions; staff scheduling and supervision; local marketing budget allocation and execution; vendor and supplier relationships within the franchisor's approved-vendor framework; pricing within the franchisor's permitted bands; financial management and reinvestment decisions; and strategic local growth, including additional unit acquisition. The franchisor's brand-standards requirements pertain to product specifications, signage, and quality control — they do not divest the Beneficiary of executive authority over the enterprise's operations, finances, or workforce."

(PATTERN, high confidence on the structure; the specific phrasing is firm-voice synthesis, not a quoted authority.)

## 7. The CBI (Citizenship-by-Investment) E-2 NEW Scenario

### 7.1 The 2023 NDAA / AMIGOS Act — the three-year domicile rule (FACT, high confidence)

**Citation.** National Defense Authorization Act for FY 2023, P.L. 117-263 (signed Dec 23, 2022); the AMIGOS Act provisions (`congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/2571`; `congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/senate-bill/1194`) (retrieved 2026-04-28).

**Rule (FACT, high confidence; corroborated across EB5Investors, EB5 United, IMI Daily, Global Immigration Blog, U.S. Immigration Advisor, all retrieved 2026-04-28):**

- An E-visa applicant who acquired the treaty-country nationality **by financial investment** must have been **domiciled in that treaty country for a continuous period of not less than three years** at any point before applying for an E-visa.
- **Carve-outs.** Persons previously granted E-visa status are exempt. Persons who acquired the treaty nationality by birth, marriage, or non-investment-based residency are not subject to the rule.
- The rule was specifically designed to limit Grenada / Turkey CBI → E-2 fast-tracks.

### 7.2 "Bona fide nationality" — what the file must show

The file must establish, on top of the citizenship certificate and treaty-country passport:

1. **Three-year continuous domicile** (where the AMIGOS Act applies) — utility bills, lease/title in the treaty country, school enrollment for children, tax residence proof, employer payroll if applicable, immigration entry/exit stamps showing physical presence pattern.
2. **Time held since naturalization** — the longer the gap between naturalization and E-2 application, the better.
3. **Business activities since acquisition** — evidence the applicant has actually engaged with the treaty country (bank account history, business registrations, real-estate ownership).
4. **Treaty-country tax filings** — even if minimal, demonstrate domicile rather than nominal status.

(PATTERN, high confidence; converges across U.S. Immigration Advisor, Klasko, EB5Investors, Global Immigration Blog, retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 7.3 2025-26 DOS scrutiny patterns (PATTERN, medium confidence)

- **Turkey.** "Speed and accessibility of Turkey's system have introduced complexity in verification, leading to occasional U.S. consular scrutiny of citizenship documents and source-of-funds evidence." (U.S. Immigration Advisor, retrieved 2026-04-28.) Long processing; more restrictive adjudications.
- **Grenada.** Caribbean-region consular officers familiar with Grenada CBI documentation; relatively higher consistency, but still subject to AMIGOS three-year rule for first-time E-2.
- **General CBI.** Inconsistencies in financial history "raise questions about the bona fide nature of both the citizenship acquisition and the subsequent business investment." (U.S. Immigration Advisor, retrieved 2026-04-28.)

> Drafter snippet (firm voice, to be added):
> "The Beneficiary acquired `<<Treaty Country>>` nationality on `<<Date>>` and has been continuously domiciled in `<<Treaty Country>>` for `<<>>` years thereafter, satisfying the three-year continuous domicile requirement enacted by the AMIGOS Act provisions of P.L. 117-263, Section `<<>>`. Domicile is evidenced by the Beneficiary's residential lease of `<<>>` years' duration, utility records, `<<Treaty Country>>` tax residency certifications, and `<<>>` years of treaty-country business activity. (Exhibits: …)"

## 8. E-2 NEW Checklist — By Element

This section maps directly to the manual's renewal-calibrated structure. Each element flags what differs from a renewal.

### Element 1 — Treaty Country Nationality

**Same as renewal:** passport, ownership instruments, 50%+ treaty-country combined ownership.

**E-2 NEW differences:**
- If acquired through CBI: AMIGOS three-year domicile pack (§7).
- No prior E-2 visa to cite — Tab C.1 (prior visa) and C.2 (prior payroll) are absent or replaced with treaty-country residence pack.

### Element 2 — Substantial Investment + At-Risk

**Same as renewal:** Membership Interest Transfer (or stock subscription) Agreement with consideration, currency conversion, international wire, deployment to Petitioner.

**E-2 NEW differences (CRITICAL):**
- **Spent-vs-escrowed disclosure.** A subsection of cover-letter Section IV that explicitly classifies each dollar as spent / escrowed-with-denial-only-clawback / escrowed-other / LOI.
- **Build-out evidence package.** Lease + paid security deposit + contractor agreement + paid progress draws + equipment POs + paid equipment invoices.
- **Substantiality narrative leans on Walsh & Pollard.** Quote the "amount normally considered necessary to establish a viable enterprise of the nature contemplated" standard, plus an industry-cost benchmark (IBISWorld report or FDD Item 7).

### Element 3 — Real & Operating

**This is the hardest element at filing time for an E-2 NEW** because the enterprise is, by definition, not yet selling.

The file must shift from "operating proof" to "**operationally ready** proof":

- EIN letter.
- State + local registrations / licensing.
- Lease + premises photos with date stamps.
- Insurance binder.
- Merchant account onboarding.
- Vendor / supplier contracts (executed).
- Hiring file (offer letters, payroll provider).
- Hours-of-operation plan, staffing schedule for opening week.

Pair with cover-letter language: "**immediately capable of commencing operations** upon Beneficiary's admission" + cite to 9 FAM 402.9-6(D) + Walsh & Pollard.

### Element 4 — More Than Marginal

**This is the second-hardest element at filing time** because tax returns and W-2 history don't yet exist.

Substitutes:

- **5-year business plan** (Matter-of-Ho-compliant, §3) projecting headcount and revenue.
- **W-2 hiring timetable** with 12-18 month first hire.
- **Job description schedule** for every projected hire.
- **Industry benchmark footnoting** showing the projected scale is realistic.
- **Capital-deployment table** showing reinvestment runway.
- **Beneficiary CV** — long industry track record substitutes for absent operating history.

### Element 5 — Develop and Direct

**Mostly same as renewal**, with two E-2 NEW additions:

- **Anti-restrictive-franchise paragraph** (§6.3) if the vehicle is a franchise.
- **Pre-operational executive-control evidence**: emails / memos showing the Beneficiary already directing pre-launch decisions (vendor selection, contractor negotiation, lease signing as Member-Manager, hiring committee chair).
