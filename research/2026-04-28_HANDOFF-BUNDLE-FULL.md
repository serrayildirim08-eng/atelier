## 1. THE FIVE E-2 ELEMENTS

The five elements are derived from **INA § 101(a)(15)(E)(ii)**, **8 CFR § 214.2(e)(12)–(15)**, and **9 FAM 402.9-6**. They are conjunctive — failure on any one is fatal.

| # | Element | Primary authority |
|---|---------|------------------|
| 1 | Treaty country nationality | 9 FAM 402.9-4(B); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(3) |
| 2 | Substantial investment | 9 FAM 402.9-6(C)–(D); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(14) |
| 3 | Real & operating enterprise (not speculative/idle) | 9 FAM 402.9-6(B); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(12)–(13) |
| 4 | More than marginal | 9 FAM 402.9-6(E); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(15) |
| 5 | Develop and direct | 9 FAM 402.9-6(F); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(16); *Matter of Walsh and Pollard*, 20 I&N Dec. 60 (BIA 1988) |

### 1.1 Treaty country / nationality

**Standard.** The applicant must hold the nationality of a country that maintains a qualifying treaty of commerce and navigation, BIT, or equivalent with the United States. **Dual nationals** may use whichever nationality qualifies, but for principal investors, the *nationality of the enterprise* must match — meaning **at least 50% of the enterprise must be owned by nationals of the same treaty country**, and those owners must either (a) be maintaining E-2 status in the U.S. or (b) if abroad, would qualify for E-2 if they applied. See [9 FAM 402.9-4(B)](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(3)(ii).

**Treaty list — current as of April 2026.** Maintained by DOS at [travel.state.gov Treaty Countries](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/treaty.html). ~80+ countries. Notable points the agent should encode:

- **Portugal — added effective March 15, 2024** (E-1 and E-2 eligible). [usimmigrationadvisor.com — Active E-2 Treaty Countries](https://www.usimmigrationadvisor.com/active-e-2-treaty-countries).
- **France — validity period extended from 25 to 48 months** under a bilateral mobility-equivalency agreement (linked to the Passeport Talent). [Consulate General of France in Boston — US/France Period of Validity Extended](https://boston.consulfrance.org/united-states-france-period-of-validity-extended-for-treaty-investor-and-treaty); [National Law Review — Reciprocity Now Effective for French Nationals](https://natlawreview.com/article/reciprocity-now-effective-french-nationals).
- **Non-treaty countries** the agent must immediately flag as *ineligible by nationality*: India, China (PRC), Russia, Brazil, Vietnam, South Africa, Nigeria, most of the Gulf except **Bahrain** and **Oman**. [Manifest Law — E-2 Treaty Countries 2026](https://manifestlaw.com/blog/e2-visa-treat-countries/).
- **Workaround paradigm** the agent should know about (and only mention to qualified leads): **citizenship-by-investment programs** in **Grenada, Turkey, Montenegro (defunct 2022), and St. Lucia** are widely used by Indian, Chinese, and other non-treaty nationals to acquire treaty nationality before filing. Grenada and Turkey are the two dominant routes. The CBI nationality must be **bona fide** — DOS scrutinizes recently acquired passports for evidence of substantive ties.

**Reciprocity** (validity period and visa fee) is set by country, not by USCIS, and lives at [travel.state.gov reciprocity tables](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country.html). Country-specific examples (verify per case): UK 5 years, Japan 5 years, Germany 5 years, France 48 months (post-extension), Italy 24 months, Spain 60 months, Turkey 60 months, Grenada 60 months, South Korea 60 months. **The reciprocity period caps the visa stamp validity, not the period of authorized stay; each entry grants a 2-year I-94.**

### 1.2 Substantial investment — the proportionality test

**Standard.** No statutory minimum. The investment must be (i) **substantial in proportion to the total cost of either purchasing an established enterprise or creating a new one**; (ii) **sufficient to ensure the treaty investor's financial commitment to the successful operation of the enterprise**; (iii) **of a magnitude to support the likelihood that the treaty investor will successfully develop and direct the enterprise**. [9 FAM 402.9-6(D)](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(14).

**The inverted sliding scale.** The lower the total cost of the business, the higher the percentage of investment required. There is no published formula, but practitioner consensus from FAM training materials and approved cases produces this rough scale:

| Total cost of enterprise | Required % to be "substantial" |
|---|---|
| Up to $100,000 | 75–100% |
| $100,000–$500,000 | 60–75% |
| $500,000–$3,000,000 | 30–60% |
| $3,000,000+ | 30% or less may suffice |

[Colombo Hurd — Substantial Capital Requirement](https://www.colombohurdlaw.com/visas/e2-visas/understanding-the-e-2-substantial-capital-requirement/); [Lighthouse HQ — E-2 Minimum Investment](https://www.lighthousehq.com/blog/e-2-visa-minimum-investment).

**2025–2026 actual numbers** (from practitioner blogs aggregating approved/denied cases — these are *patterns*, not citable rules):

- **Service businesses / consulting:** $80,000–$150,000 typical. Sub-$80k flagged as "insufficient." [BeyondBorder Global — E-2 Investment Amount 2025](https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/e-2-visa-investment-amount----how-much-is-enough-in-2025).
- **Tech startups / SaaS:** $100,000–$250,000. Below $100k requires very strong proportionality + irrevocable commitment narrative. One reported approval: **$120,000 with detailed product-dev contracts and hired CTO**. [usimmigrationplan — E-2 for Tech Startups 2026](https://www.usimmigrationplan.com/post/e-2-visas-for-tech-startups-and-saas-in-2026).
- **Restaurant / café / retail:** $150,000–$500,000+ (build-out, equipment, permits, inventory, lease deposit, working capital).
- **Franchises:** $200,000–$500,000+ depending on franchisor (anchor with the FDD's Item 7 estimate).
- **E-commerce:** $80,000–$200,000 — vulnerable to "marginal/passive" attacks if no employees and no physical presence.
- **Real estate:** **non-passive only** — must be active development/operation (e.g., short-term rental management with staff, real estate brokerage). Pure buy-and-hold is disqualified as passive.

[Pandev Law — E-2 Denials Common Reasons](https://www.pandevlaw.com/blog/e2-visa-denials-common-reasons-and-tips-to-strengthen-your-case/); [usimmigrationplan — The Substantial Investment Mystery 2026](https://www.usimmigrationplan.com/post/the-2026-e-2-visa-the-substantial-investment-mystery).

**Counted vs. not counted toward "investment":**
- Counted: equipment, inventory, leasehold improvements, lease deposits and prepaid rent (when paid), legal/professional fees tied to the business launch, marketing spend committed, payroll obligations, working capital irrevocably committed.
- Generally **not** counted: personal expenses, the value of the investor's own future labor ("sweat equity"), uncommitted cash sitting in a personal account, residential real estate the investor lives in, intangible "ideas" without development spend.
- Loans: **only loans collateralized by the investor's personal, non-business assets count** — loans secured by the assets of the U.S. enterprise itself do **not** count because they shift the risk to the business, not the investor. 9 FAM 402.9-6(C); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(12).

### 1.3 Real & operating enterprise

**Standard.** A "bona fide enterprise" — a real, active, and operating commercial or entrepreneurial undertaking producing services or goods for profit. **Excludes** speculative/idle investment, paper organizations, uncommitted funds, and passive investment (e.g., holding undeveloped land, passive securities, a residential rental held for appreciation). 9 FAM 402.9-6(B); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(13).

**Proof:** business license, EIN/CP-575, articles of incorporation/organization, lease, bank account, employer registrations (state UI, IRS payroll), inventory or equipment purchases, contracts with suppliers/customers, marketing materials, website, point-of-sale activity, payroll already started. **The agent should privilege evidence of actual transactions** (invoices, deposits, paid bills) over plans/intentions.

**Speculative-investment trap:** an LLC formed but with funds only "earmarked" in a personal account is *not* a real and operating enterprise. The "in the process of investing" doctrine from *Matter of Walsh and Pollard*, 20 I&N Dec. 60 (BIA 1988), allows pre-launch filings only if funds are **irrevocably committed**. [Matter of Walsh and Pollard — Interim Decision #3111](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2012/08/14/3111.pdf).

### 1.4 More than marginal — the marginality test

**Standard.** A marginal enterprise is one that does not have the present or future capacity to generate **more than enough income to provide a minimal living for the treaty investor and his or her family**. An enterprise lacking that capacity may still pass marginality if it has **the present or future capacity to make a significant economic contribution**. The five-year horizon is the standard from FAM. [9 FAM 402.9-6(E)](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(15).

**Two prongs (alternatives — pass either):**
1. **Income prong:** projected income comfortably exceeds a "minimal living" within five years. Practitioners benchmark "minimal living" against the area's median household income or HHS poverty guidelines for family size + cost of living — there is no published USCIS number. Showing net income substantially above this for the principal's family is the safest path.
2. **Job creation / economic contribution prong:** the enterprise will hire U.S. workers (W-2 employees) and create measurable economic contribution. **2026 trend:** adjudicators are *rejecting plans relying heavily on 1099 contractors* and require a **specific W-2 hiring timetable**. [Joorney — E-2 Marginality 2025](https://www.joorney.com/news/e2-visa-success-2025-business-plans-marginality-requirement-tips/).

**How marginality is actually proven** (practitioner playbook):
- 5-year financial projections with month-by-month detail in Year 1, quarterly in Year 2, annual Years 3–5, **with assumptions footnoted to industry data** (IBISWorld, BLS, FDD Item 19, FRED).
- An organizational chart showing W-2 hires by date.
- Letters of intent / signed offers from initial hires.
- Comparable-business revenue benchmarks.
- Marketing pipeline (signed customer agreements, LOIs, paid pilots).

**Key denial pattern:** "solo consultant" cases — single-owner, no employees, working from home — are the highest marginality-denial risk. [Scott Legal — Marginality](https://legalservicesincorporated.com/immigration/e-2-visa/what-does-marginality-mean-for-an-e-2-visa-how-do-i-prove-that-my-e-2-business-is-not-marginal-do-i-have-to-hire-u-s-workers-if-i-get-an-e-2-visa/).

### 1.5 Develop and direct

**Standard.** The treaty investor must be coming to the U.S. **solely to develop and direct** the enterprise. Established by either:
- **Ownership of at least 50% of the enterprise**, OR
- **Operational control** through a managerial position or other corporate device (e.g., voting trust, GP role in an LP, manager of a manager-managed LLC).

[9 FAM 402.9-6(F)](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(16).

**Notes:**
- 50/50 partnerships between two treaty nationals of the same country can both qualify for E-2 because each holds "develop and direct."
- Minority owners with negative control via a board seat / supermajority voting / shareholders' agreement can qualify, but require careful drafting.
- *Matter of Walsh and Pollard* clarified the develop-and-direct requirement applies to E-2 *investors*, not E-2 *employees* (who are evaluated under the executive/supervisory or essential-skills tests).
- **Franchise pitfall:** if the franchise agreement is so restrictive the franchisee cannot make hiring, pricing, or operational decisions, "develop and direct" is challenged. [Pollak PLLC — E-2 Franchise Guide](https://www.pollakimmigration.com/blog/e2-visa-franchise-program-how-to-do-it-right).

## 2. SOURCE OF FUNDS — TRACING & LAWFUL ORIGIN

**Standard.** All invested capital must be (i) **lawfully obtained**, (ii) **fully traceable**, (iii) **under the investor's possession and control**, and (iv) **subject to loss** (at risk). 9 FAM 402.9-6(C); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(12). [Berardi — E-2 Money Trail](https://berardiimmigrationlaw.com/e-2-visas-the-money-trail/).

**The chronological-annotated-file approach.** Every dollar must be tied to its origin via documentary chain — *origin → custody → transfer → deployment*.

### 2.1 Acceptable origin categories and required documents

| Origin | Required documents |
|---|---|
| **Salary / employment income** | Multi-year payslips; employment contract; tax returns (foreign + IRS where applicable); bank statements showing salary deposits aligning to payslips. |
| **Business profits / dividends** | Audited financials; corporate tax filings; dividend declarations; minute-book resolutions; capital account statements; bank statements showing distributions. |
| **Sale of real estate** | Deed; sale-purchase agreement; closing statement / HUD-1 equivalent; title report; bank statement showing sale proceeds deposit; original acquisition documents (proving lawful acquisition). |
| **Sale of business / shares** | Stock purchase agreement / asset purchase agreement; closing flow-of-funds memorandum; valuation; tax filings on the gain; original acquisition documents. |
| **Inheritance** | Death certificate; will/probate order; estate accounting; transfer documents; **show the decedent's lawful source** if the inheritance is large. |
| **Gift** | **Notarized gift letter** stating: amount, donor, recipient, date, that the gift is irrevocable, and that no repayment is required. **Donor's** source-of-funds documentation (same standard as if the donor were the applicant). Wire/transfer record. |
| **Loan** | Loan agreement; collateral schedule; **proof the collateral is the investor's personal/non-business asset** (e.g., home equity, securities, second property); bank record of disbursement. **Loans secured by the U.S. enterprise's own assets do NOT count.** 9 FAM 402.9-6(C). |
| **Personal savings** | Multi-year bank statements; corresponding tax returns establishing income that produced the savings. |
| **Cryptocurrency** | Exchange records; wallet transaction history; KYC documents from the exchange; conversion-to-fiat records; tax treatment. **Heightened scrutiny — DOS particularly skeptical of unverifiable crypto sources.** |

[Aga Asbury Immigration — Clean E-2 Source of Funds](https://agaimmigration.com/e-2-visa-source-of-funds/); [Scott Legal — Source of Funds Requirement](https://legalservicesincorporated.com/immigration/e-2-visa/understanding-the-e-2-visa-source-of-funds-requirement/).

### 2.2 Documentation depth by investment size

- **≤ $100,000:** 6–12 months of bank statements often suffice plus origin docs.
- **$100,000–$500,000:** typically 1–3 years of records; tax returns matching deposits.
- **$500,000+:** 3–5 years of records, with a written source-of-funds narrative tying every transaction.
- **Large lump-sum windfalls** (e.g., a $400k crypto windfall, a property sale) require *deep* origin proof — adjudicators' chief concern is unexplained wealth.

[Lauren Landed — E-2 Source of Funds](https://www.laurenlanded.com/blog/e2-visa-source-of-funds).

### 2.3 USCIS vs. Consular scrutiny

- **USCIS (I-129)** applies the strictest paper-trail standard — adjudicator reads only what is in the file. Build a **source-of-funds memo** with exhibit citations.
- **Consular** officers can ask follow-up questions live; the practitioner consensus is that 3–6 months of records often suffice plus the deal flow. **2025–2026 trend:** consular interviews are getting longer and tougher — handled by rotating officer pools rather than dedicated E visa units. [AILA Practice Alert (March 2025)](https://www.aila.org/library); summarized at [usvisahelp.com — E-2](https://www.usvisahelp.com/nonimmigrant-e2.php).

### 2.4 At-risk / irrevocably committed (related but distinct from "lawful")

**Funds must be at risk of partial or total loss if business fortunes reverse.** Funds sitting in a personal account labeled "for the business" are not at risk. The doctrinal anchor is *Matter of Walsh and Pollard* — funds are "in the process of being invested" only if **commitment is real and irrevocable**. Conditional escrow tied to *visa issuance only* is acceptable per 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) and Walsh/Pollard.

**2026 caution:** consulates are increasingly skeptical of "contingent" escrow agreements — if the escrow allows the investor to claw back funds for *any* reason other than visa denial, adjudicators are calling it speculative. [usimmigrationplan — At Risk Rule 2026](https://www.usimmigrationplan.com/post/the-2026-e-2-visa-the-at-risk-rule-escrow-vs-spent); [BeyondBorder Global — At Risk Capital](https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/e-2-at-risk-capital-what-counts-as-irrevocably-committed-vs-merely-planned).

**Practical rule:** the agent should prefer evidence of **actually-spent** funds over **escrowed** funds where possible. Lease executed and rent paid > lease signed and deposit held in escrow > LOI to lease.

## 3. COMMON RFE / DENIAL PATTERNS

Pulled from practitioner aggregations: [Pandev Law — E-2 Denials](https://www.pandevlaw.com/blog/e2-visa-denials-common-reasons-and-tips-to-strengthen-your-case/), [VisaBusinessPlans — Good Bad Ugly of RFEs](https://www.visabusinessplans.com/blog/2024/september/the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly-of-rfes-what-you-n/), [Colombo Hurd — RFE Trends 2025](https://www.colombohurdlaw.com/strategies-to-overcome-rfe-challenges-to-employment-based-petitions-under-the-trump-administration/), [Stelmakh — RFEs in 2025](https://stelmakhlaw.com/blog/rfes-in-2025/).

### 3.1 Top RFE / denial categories — language and counter-strategies

| RFE/denial pattern | Typical USCIS language | What officer is actually testing | Counter-strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marginality** | "The petitioner has not established that the enterprise has the present or future capacity to generate more than a minimal living within 5 years." | Whether the business is just self-employment with extra steps. | Detailed 5-yr projections + W-2 hiring timetable + signed offer letters + market sizing. |
| **Substantial investment / proportionality** | "The record does not establish that the investment is substantial in relation to the total cost of the enterprise." | Whether the investor has skin in the game proportional to costs. | Itemized cost table; receipts/invoices vs. plan; explicitly state proportionality % and cite 9 FAM 402.9-6(D). |
| **At-risk / irrevocably committed** | "Funds remain in the petitioner's personal account and have not been irrevocably committed to the enterprise." | Whether funds are actually deployed. | Move funds into business account, spend on lease/equipment/payroll *before* filing. Provide bank statements + receipts. |
| **Source of funds** | "The petitioner has not sufficiently established the lawful source of the invested capital. Provide [tax returns / sale agreements / bank statements] for the period [X–Y]." | Whether origin is documented and legal. | Source-of-funds memo + chronological exhibit set. |
| **Real and operating** | "Evidence of operations is insufficient. The record contains business plans but no evidence of [customers/employees/revenue]." | Whether the business is actually running. | Receipts, customer contracts, payroll register, point-of-sale records, photos of premises. |
| **Develop and direct** | "The record does not establish that the petitioner will develop and direct the enterprise." | Whether the investor has real control. | Cap table; operating agreement; corporate resolutions appointing investor as manager/officer; signature authority. |
| **Treaty nationality of enterprise** | "The petitioner has not established that the enterprise is at least 50% owned by nationals of the treaty country." | Cap table chain. | Color-coded cap table tracing every owner up to a treaty national; passports for each beneficial owner. |
| **Nonimmigrant intent** | "The applicant has not established intent to depart upon termination of E-2 status." | Whether they are using E-2 as immigrant pathway. | Attestation; ties abroad; emphasize statutory rule that E-2s need not maintain a foreign residence (a unique feature) — but should still articulate intent to depart. |
| **Essential-employee specialization** (E-2 employee cases) | "The record does not establish that the position requires specialized skills not readily available in the U.S. labor market." | Whether the role is genuinely essential. | Job analysis; LCA-style market data; describe proprietary knowledge; training plan for U.S. workers. |

### 3.2 Trump-era RFE patterns (2025–2026)

Practitioners report broader, more demanding RFEs across employment-based categories since January 2025, with USCIS issuing more RFEs/NOIDs and citing wider discretionary grounds. [Ballard Spahr — DHS Ends Automatic EAD Extension](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2025/10/dhs-ends-automatic-extension-of-certain-employment-authorization-documents); [NAFSA — Executive and Regulatory Actions Trump 2 Administration](https://www.nafsa.org/executive-and-regulatory-actions-trump2admin); [Maynard Nexsen — Trump 2.0 EOs Impacting Employment-Based Visas](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-trump-2-0-executive-orders-that-impact-employment-based-visas).

The agent should anticipate, for E-2 specifically:
- More aggressive *marginality* attacks on solo/home businesses.
- More aggressive *source-of-funds* tracing requests.
- New "discretionary" denials citing **PA-2025-16** USCIS Policy Alert (Aug 19, 2025) on discretionary factors in adjudication. [USCIS PA-2025-16](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/policy-manual-updates/20250819-DiscretionaryFactors.pdf).

## 4. E-2 EMPLOYEES — ESSENTIAL & EXECUTIVE/SUPERVISORY

E-2 employees are evaluated under **separate** prongs from the principal investor — they need *not* meet "develop and direct," but must meet **either** the **executive/supervisory** prong **or** the **essential-skills** prong, **plus** the same nationality requirement (must share the treaty nationality of the qualifying employer).

### 4.1 Executive or supervisory

**9 FAM 402.9-7(C); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(17).** Position must be **principally and primarily** executive or supervisory (not heavily collateral or nonsupervisory). Factors:

- Title of position
- Place in firm's organizational structure
- Duties of the position
- Degree of ultimate control and responsibility for overall operations or a major component
- Number and skill levels of employees supervised
- Level of compensation
- Whether the applicant possesses qualifying executive/supervisory experience

A "first-line supervisor" of low-skill workers generally fails. Position should manage other professional/managerial staff or a major function/component. [Inventimm — E-2 Managers/Executives/Essential](https://www.inventimm.com/practice-areas/e-1-e-2-visa-managers-executives-essential-employees/).

### 4.2 Essential-skills employee

**9 FAM 402.9-7(D); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(18).** Specialized knowledge or skills essential to the efficient operation of the enterprise. Factors:

- Degree of proven expertise in the area of operations
- Whether other workers possess the same skills
- Whether U.S. workers have the same skills/qualifications
- Length of experience and training
- Period of training/experience required to perform the job
- Relationship of the skill/knowledge to the enterprise's specific processes/applications
- Salary that the special qualifications can command
- Whether the skills and qualifications are readily available in the U.S. labor market

**Foreign-language or culture knowledge alone is NOT essentiality.** [Vazquez & Poudat — Essential Employee](https://www.vpimmigration.com/essential-employee-e1-e2-visa.html).

**Long-term vs. short-term need.** Short-term essentiality (start-up, new technology, training) typically ~1–2 years with a U.S.-worker training plan. Long-term essentiality (ongoing proprietary knowledge) must be specifically explained.

### 4.3 Distinguishing from L-1

| Feature | E-2 (employee) | L-1A/B |
|---|---|---|
| Nationality requirement | Must match treaty country of employer | Any nationality |
| Qualifying employment abroad | Not required | 1 of past 3 years with related entity |
| Specialized knowledge / executive standard | Essentiality OR exec/supervisory | Specialized knowledge (L-1B) OR managerial/executive (L-1A) |
| Spouse work auth | Yes (E-2S, automatic per I-94) | Yes (L-2S, automatic per I-94) |
| Dual intent | Limited — nonimmigrant intent presumed but not required to maintain foreign residence | Recognized dual intent |
| Path to GC | Difficult (no direct path; usually via EB-1C/EB-2/EB-5) | Strong (L-1A → EB-1C is the canonical path) |
| Validity | 2-yr admission, indefinite renewals | 1-yr (new office) or 3-yr; max 7 (L-1A), 5 (L-1B) |

The agent should run an L-1 vs. E-2 comparison whenever the prospect has a foreign parent company.

### 5.1 Specifically affecting E-2

- **France reciprocity extended to 48 months** (post-2024; in effect 2025). Big practical win for French clients.
- **Portugal added** as a treaty country effective **March 15, 2024**.

- **End of automatic EAD extension (Oct 30, 2025)** — affects E-2D spouses who *had* obtained an EAD and are renewing. - **PA-2025-16 (Aug 19, 2025)** — USCIS expanded use of "discretionary factors" in adjudication. Even technically eligible petitions can be denied on discretion. The agent should add a "favorable discretionary factors" section to every petition memo (community ties, tax compliance, no immigration violations, U.S. job creation already underway).

- **Expanded social-media vetting** (Aug 2025) — applicants flagged for "anti-American" or "antisemitic" content. The agent should warn clients about social-media review.
- **Biometrics rule** — proposed to require biometrics from "any individual filing or associated with" an immigration benefit request.

### 5.3 No new AAO precedent on E-2 in this window

A targeted check of the [AAO Non-Precedent Decisions](https://www.uscis.gov/administrative-appeals/aao-decisions/aao-non-precedent-decisions) database (filter: Form I-129, Subject: Petition for a Nonimmigrant Treaty Investor (E-2), Regulation: 8 CFR 214.2(e)) is the agent's standing instruction. ## 6. FILING POSTURE — I-129 vs. CONSULAR

| Dimension | I-129 (USCIS Change of Status) | Consular Processing (DS-160 + DS-156E) |
|---|---|---|
| Who's eligible | Applicant **already in U.S.** in lawful NIV status (other than C, D, K, S, TWOV) | Applicant abroad, or in U.S. but planning to consular-process |
| Forms | Form **I-129** + **E-1/E-2 Classification Supplement** + **G-28** | Form **DS-160** + Form **DS-156E** (executives/managers/essentials) + DS-160 confirmation |
| Filing fee (FY2026) | I-129 $1,015 + Asylum Program Fee (varies $300/$600/$0 based on employer size/type) | DS-160 fee $315 (+ reciprocity issuance fee per country) |
| Premium processing | **Yes** ($2,805) — 15 business days | N/A |
| What you get | E-2 *status* (I-94 stamped on approval); **no visa stamp** — must consular-process to travel | E-2 *visa stamp* (multi-entry); admission grants 2-yr I-94 each entry |
| Validity | 2 years per period of authorized stay; renew via I-129 extension | Visa stamp = reciprocity period (e.g., 5 yrs UK/Japan/Germany); I-94 = 2 yrs per entry |
| Adjudication forum | USCIS Service Center | U.S. consulate abroad |
| Tone | Paper-only; very document-heavy; binary | Live interview; document set + officer interaction; **dominant** in E-2 practice |
| Premium-processing strategy | If client urgently needs to start work; risk: USCIS often issues RFEs | Most E-2 cases go consular for the multi-year stamp |
| RFE/follow-up risk | RFE common; binary review | "221(g)" administrative processing for additional documents |

[USCIS — I-129](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129); [travel.state.gov — DS-160 FAQs](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/forms/ds-160-online-nonimmigrant-visa-application/ds-160-faqs.html); [Alaz Law — E-2 Change of Status 2026](https://www.alazlaw.com/blog/e-2-visa-change-of-status-2026-apply-without-leaving-us/); [tryalma — E-2 Processing Time 2026](https://www.tryalma.com/learn/e2-processing-time).

### 6.1 Document differences

**I-129 (USCIS) — added:**
- Form I-129 + E supplement
- Form G-28
- Cover letter / legal memorandum
- Heavy paper exhibit set (USCIS sees only what's in the file)
- Filing receipts and certified mail tracking
- For change of status from a status with limited duration (e.g., B-1/B-2): explicit statement of *change-of-purpose timeline* to defeat preconceived-intent allegations

**Consular — added:**
- DS-160 confirmation page
- DS-156E (separate form per applicant for executives/managers/essentials)
- Visa application fee (MRV) receipt + reciprocity fee
- Appointment confirmation
- Original passport
- 2x2 photo per State spec
- Interview prep memo (oral testimony is part of adjudication)

### 6.2 Spouse / children — E-2D

**E-2D dependents** are the spouse and unmarried children under 21. They follow the principal.

- **Spouse work authorization (E-2S):** **automatic per I-94 annotation** — no EAD required since the November 2021 USCIS policy update memorialized in [Vol. 10, Part B, Ch. 2 of the Policy Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-10-part-b-chapter-2). The I-94 must be annotated "E-2S" (or "E-1S/E-3S" as applicable). [Maggio Kattar — Work Auth for H-4/L-2/E-2 Spouses](https://maggio-kattar.com/employer-compliance/work-authorization-for-h-4-l-2-and-e-2-spouses/).
  - **Trap:** if the I-94 lacks the "S" annotation (older I-94s), the spouse must **either** request a corrected I-94 from CBP/USCIS **or** file Form I-765 for an EAD.
  - **2025–2026 caveat:** the Oct 30, 2025 end of automatic EAD extension affects spouses who *had* filed an I-765 and are renewing. For new admissions, the I-94-only path is unaffected.
- **Children** under 21: dependent status, no work authorization, can attend school (K–12 and university).
- **Children turn 21:** age-out — must independently qualify (F-1, OPT, H-1B, etc.). The agent must flag age-out within 6 months.

## 8. THE 5-YEAR BUSINESS PLAN STANDARD

**E-2 has no codified Matter-of-Ho equivalent**, but practitioners and FAM-trained consular officers apply *Matter of Ho*-style factors by analogy. *Matter of Ho*, 22 I&N Dec. 206 (Assoc. Comm'r 1998), is technically EB-5 doctrine, but its **"comprehensive, credible, and verifiable"** test for business plans is the de facto E-2 yardstick. [USCIS Policy Manual references to Matter of Ho factors](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-g) (in Vol. 2 Part G, Ch. 2 for E-2).

### 8.1 Required components — the "Ho-by-analogy" plan

1. **Executive summary** — what the business does, where, who runs it, total investment, jobs created.
2. **Company description** — entity structure, ownership, treaty-nationality compliance.
3. **Products / services** — concrete description, pricing, unit economics.
4. **Market analysis** — TAM/SAM/SOM, competitive positioning, customer demographics, geography. **Sources cited** (IBISWorld, BLS, U.S. Census, FDD Item 19).
5. **Marketing plan** — channels, customer acquisition cost, expected conversion, marketing spend by quarter.
6. **Operations plan** — supply chain, location, equipment, hours, fulfillment.
7. **Management & organization** — current staff + 5-year **W-2** hiring timetable with titles, salaries, and start dates.
8. **Financial projections** — **monthly Year 1, quarterly Year 2, annual Years 3–5**. Include P&L, cash flow, balance sheet. **Footnoted assumptions** for every line (revenue per customer, COGS%, labor %, marketing %, etc.).
9. **Sensitivity analysis / contingencies** — what happens at 70% / 85% / 100% / 115% of base case.
10. **Job creation summary** — total W-2 jobs by Year 5, quantified economic contribution.
11. **Risks & mitigation** — honest risk register.
12. **Appendices** — quotes, letters of intent, market data sources.

[Joorney — Franchise E-2 Business Plans Deep Dive](https://www.joorney.com/news/franchise-e2-visa-business-plans-deep-dive-what-works/); [BeyondBorder Global — Business Plan E-2 2025](https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/business-plan-e2-visa-complete-guide-for-investors-2025); [Mayo Law — E-2 Business Plan Requirements](https://mayo.law/e-2-visa-business-plan-requirements/); [VisaBusinessPlans — Franchise Plan Won't Cut It](https://www.visabusinessplans.com/blog/2025/june/buying-a-franchise-for-your-e-2-visa-here-s-why-/).

### 8.2 Plan failure modes the agent should screen for

- **The "paper plan" pattern from *Matter of Ho*** — generic plan, no supplier relationships, no customer development, no funds-deployment narrative. Auto-fail.
- **Franchise's own boilerplate plan** — almost always rejected. Franchisor's plan is for the brand, not for E-2 adjudication. Must be replaced with a custom plan that addresses E-2 elements.
- **1099-heavy projections** — adjudicators in 2026 reject these. Agent must convert to W-2.
- **Year 1 projected at break-even or worse, with no growth pathway** — marginality red flag.
- **Footnote-free assumptions** — adjudicator can't verify ⇒ "speculative."
- **Mismatch between investment table and plan deployment narrative** — credibility hit.

## 10. PITFALLS — TOP-OF-MIND TRAPS

### 10.1 The marginal home-based business
Solo consultant, no employees, working from a home office, projecting income that just covers the family's living costs ⇒ near-certain marginality denial. [Scott Legal — Marginality](https://legalservicesincorporated.com/immigration/e-2-visa/what-does-marginality-mean-for-an-e-2-visa-how-do-i-prove-that-my-e-2-business-is-not-marginal-do-i-have-to-hire-u-s-workers-if-i-get-an-e-2-visa/); [RJ Immigration — Marginal Requirement](https://rjimmigrationlaw.com/resources/what-is-the-marginal-requirement-for-an-e-2-investor-visa/).

**Cure:** office lease (even shared/coworking), W-2 hires within 12 months, marketing pipeline, signed customer contracts, projections passing the income prong *and* job-creation prong.

### 10.2 Franchise vs. original
Neither is inherently better. **Franchise pros:** faster "real and operating," proven model, easier marginality narrative. **Franchise cons:** restrictive franchise agreements can defeat "develop and direct"; **franchisor's stock business plan is almost always inadequate** — must build a custom E-2 plan. [Pollak — E-2 Franchise Guide](https://www.pollakimmigration.com/blog/e2-visa-franchise-program-how-to-do-it-right); [Colombo Hurd — Franchise Investment Guide 2026](https://www.colombohurdlaw.com/visas/e2-visas/advantages-disadvantages-investment-franchise-obtain-e-2-visa/).

### 10.3 In-progress investment ("at risk" / irrevocably committed)
Funds sitting in a personal account, an LLC formed but not funded, a lease "negotiated but not signed" — none of these meet the "in the process of investing" threshold from *Matter of Walsh and Pollard*. **Cure:** spend the money or escrow it under a visa-denial-only-clawback agreement; provide receipts. [usimmigrationplan — At Risk Rule 2026](https://www.usimmigrationplan.com/post/the-2026-e-2-visa-the-at-risk-rule-escrow-vs-spent).

### 10.4 Passive investment trap
Real estate buy-and-hold, undeveloped land, minority position in someone else's business, brokerage account ⇒ disqualified. The investor must *actively engage* in management. [USCIS — E-2 Treaty Investors](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/e-2-treaty-investors).

### 10.5 Loan secured by enterprise assets
Loan collateralized by the U.S. business's own assets does NOT count toward investment because risk shifts to the business, not the investor. Personal-asset-collateralized loans count. 9 FAM 402.9-6(C).

### 10.6 Cap-table contamination
Adding a non-treaty-national co-founder above 50% kills the entire E-2 nationality of the enterprise. The agent must screen **every** capital event (new investor, vesting, option exercise, conversion) for nationality impact.

### 10.7 Source-of-funds opacity
Cash businesses, undocumented gifts, large unexplained deposits, crypto without exchange records, "loans from family" without paper. Each of these triggers source-of-funds RFE/221(g).

### 10.8 Preconceived intent (B-1/B-2 → E-2 change of status)
Entering on B-1/B-2 with preformed intent to file an E-2 change of status is a misrepresentation risk. Must either (a) build the case in compliance with 30/60-day rule and timeline genuinely supports a *change* of purpose, or (b) consular-process from abroad.

### 10.9 E-2 → green card path is hard
E-2 has no direct adjustment-of-status path. Common bridges: **EB-5** (if scaling investment + jobs), **EB-1C** (if international transferee with multinational structure), **EB-2 NIW** (if individual qualifies), **family-based**. Agent should run a green-card-path analysis up front.

### 10.10 Children aging out
Dependents lose status at 21. **No CSPA protection** for E-2D (CSPA only applies to immigrant categories). Agent must flag age-out 12+ months in advance and plan F-1/H-1B/etc.

## 11. MANUAL SKELETON — STRUCTURE FOR THE AGENT

This is the proposed structure for an E-2 case-type manual the agent reads/writes from. Each section has *required slots* the agent populates from client documents, *decision points* it routes on, and *output artifacts* it generates.

### 11.1 Top-level case state machine

```
INTAKE → ELIGIBILITY-SCREEN → STRATEGY-SELECT → DOC-COLLECT → ANALYSIS → DRAFT → REVIEW → FILE → POST-FILE
```

### 11.2 INTAKE — slots to fill
- `principal.fullName`, `principal.nationality`, `principal.dualNationalities[]`, `principal.dob`, `principal.currentLocation`, `principal.currentImmigrationStatus`, `principal.priorUSImmigrationHistory`
- `dependents[].relationship`, `.fullName`, `.dob`, `.nationality`
- `enterprise.name`, `.entityType` (LLC/Corp/LP), `.stateOfFormation`, `.industry`, `.naicsCode`, `.address`, `.formationDate`, `.ein`
- `enterprise.captable[]` — owner, % ownership, nationality, direct/indirect path
- `investment.totalCommitted`, `.totalSpent`, `.totalInEscrow`, `.byCategoryItemized[]`
- `enterprise.totalCostOfEstablishment`
- `sourceOfFunds.categories[]` — origin, amount, date, supporting documents available
- `priorAttempts.priorE2Filings[]`, `.priorDenials`, `.priorRefusals[]`
- `clientGoals.targetEntryDate`, `.familyMembersAccompanying`, `.greenCardInterest`

### 11.3 ELIGIBILITY-SCREEN — decision points
1. **Treaty nationality?**
   - Branch A: Direct treaty national ⇒ proceed.
   - Branch B: Not treaty national ⇒ flag CBI options (Grenada/Turkey) or pivot to L-1/EB-5/O-1.
   - Branch C: Travel-ban list ⇒ flag and assess waiver feasibility.
2. **Cap table 50%+ treaty-national?** If no, structuring required.
3. **Investment substantial under proportionality test?** Run the math. If marginal, recommend additional spend or structural change.
4. **Real & operating?** Score against a checklist (license, lease, EIN, transactions). If "speculative," recommend pre-filing milestones.
5. **Marginality?** Run 5-yr projection screen and W-2 hiring screen. If solo + home + sub-$100k ⇒ red flag.
6. **Develop and direct?** Verify ownership ≥50% or operational control via governance docs.

Output: **Eligibility Memo** with go/no-go and conditions.

### 11.4 STRATEGY-SELECT — decision points
- **Filing posture:** I-129 (COS) vs. consular processing.
  - Inputs: client current location, urgency, need to travel, status validity.
  - Default: consular if abroad or planning to travel; I-129 only if compelling reason to stay in U.S. and start work fast.
- **Premium processing:** yes if I-129 and urgent; no for consular (N/A).
- **Dependents path:** I-539 + I-539A (COS) vs. DS-160 each (consular).
- **Nationality strategy:** principal nationality, ownership-chain structuring, CBI if needed.

Output: **Strategy Memo** with rationale + cost/timeline estimate.

### 11.5 DOC-COLLECT — slot-driven checklist
Iterate Section 7 above. For each unchecked exhibit, the agent generates a client request with deadline. Status-tracked.

Output: **Document Tracker** with red/yellow/green per exhibit.

### 11.6 ANALYSIS — element-by-element memo
For each of the five elements + source of funds, the agent writes a short memo:
- Standard cited (FAM + CFR + PM + precedent)
- Facts of this case
- Application of standard to facts
- Strength rating (strong / adequate / weak)
- Mitigation if weak

Output: **Element-by-Element Analysis Memo**.

### 11.7 DRAFT — artifacts
- Cover letter / legal brief
- Source-of-funds memorandum (with chronological exhibit chain)
- 5-year business plan (Section 8 structure) — typically authored by a business plan vendor (Joorney, Visa Business Plans, etc.) but the agent reviews against the E-2 element checklist
- Itemized investment schedule
- Cap-table chain diagram
- Organizational chart (current + Year 5)
- W-2 hiring timetable
- Exhibit index
- Forms package: I-129+E supplement+G-28 OR DS-160+DS-156E

### 11.8 REVIEW — quality gates
- Element-by-element coverage check
- Exhibit cross-reference check

# E-2 Sub-Type Taxonomy (Authoritative)

> Synthesizes 9 FAM 402.9, 8 CFR 214.2(e), USCIS Policy Manual, and the two
> real Akalan filings (Kacar-Salih + Camural) into a single decision tree for
> the agent's Phase-0 case-subtype detector. The agent uses this to pick which
> Tab map, which cover-letter template, which evidence schema, and which voice
> to apply per case.

## 1. The full E-2 universe

```
E-2 Treaty Investor / Treaty Trader (INA §101(a)(15)(E))
│
├── E-1 Treaty Trader   (8 CFR 214.2(e); 9 FAM 402.9-3)         [out of scope here]
│
└── E-2 Treaty Investor (8 CFR 214.2(e); 9 FAM 402.9-3)
    │
    ├── PRINCIPAL — the person who develops and directs
    │   │
    │   ├── 1. Individual Treaty Investor
    │   │      • Person directly invests substantial capital
    │   │      • Owns ≥50% OR has operational control
    │   │      • Develops & directs the enterprise
    │   │      • Pattern: Kacar-Salih (small LLC, $120K, sole/co-owner)
    │   │      • Authority: 9 FAM 402.9-4 to -6, 8 CFR 214.2(e)(2)
    │   │
    │   └── 2. Corporate-Owned E-2 (rare)
    │          • Treaty-country corporation is the investor
    │          • Individual principal still must qualify as treaty national
    │          • Authority: 9 FAM 402.9-4(B), 8 CFR 214.2(e)(3)
    │
    ├── EMPLOYEE — sponsored by a qualifying treaty enterprise
    │   │
    │   ├── 3. Executive / Supervisory Employee  (E-2 Manager)
    │   │      • "Great authority to determine the policy and direction"
    │   │      • Supervises significant portion of operations
    │   │      • Authority: 8 CFR 214.2(e)(17), 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a)
    │   │
    │   └── 4. Essential Skills / Specialized Knowledge Employee
    │          • Special qualifications essential to enterprise
    │          • Skills not readily available in US labor market
    │          • Salary-differential proof
    │          • Pattern: Camural (Pomega Energy — Medium Voltage Sales Specialist)
    │          • Authority: 8 CFR 214.2(e)(18), 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b)
    │
    └── DEPENDENT — derivative status under principal
        │
        ├── 5. E-2 Spouse (I-539, with EAD eligibility)
        │      • Authority: INA §214(e)(6); 9 FAM 402.9-8
        │
        └── 6. E-2 Child (I-539A, under 21, unmarried)
               • Authority: 9 FAM 402.9-8
```

The agent's Phase-0 detector must classify each filing into exactly **one
principal subtype (1–4)** plus **zero or more dependents (5–6)**.

## 2. Procedural variants (orthogonal to subtype)

Every subtype above can present as one of these procedural postures:

| Procedural variant | What it is | Form mix |
|---|---|---|
| **New (Consular)** | First-time E-2 issued at US consulate abroad | DS-160 + DS-156E (no I-129) |
| **New (USCIS COS)** | Change of Status to E-2 from inside US | I-129 + I-129E |
| **Extension (USCIS)** | Extending E-2 status without leaving US | I-129 + I-129E (renewal context) |
| **Visa Renewal (Consular)** | Re-issuance of stamp at consulate | DS-160 + DS-156E |
| **Re-Validation** | Limited domestic stamp renewal (where available) | Varies |

Detection signals:
- I-129 + I-129E present → USCIS path (COS or Extension)
- DS-160 + DS-156E only → consular path (new or renewal)
- I-94 admit-until date in past + I-129E filed → COS
- I-94 admit-until date in future + I-129E filed → Extension
- "Renewal" or "Extension" stated explicitly in cover letter → renewal posture

## 3. Element checklist by subtype

What the agent must prove varies by subtype. Cover letter sections ARE the
element list.

### 3.1 Subtype 1 — Individual Treaty Investor (Kacar-Salih)

| Element | Authority | Cover-letter section |
|---|---|---|
| Treaty country nationality | 9 FAM 402.9-4(A) | II |
| Petitioner ≥ 50% treaty-national ownership | 9 FAM 402.9-4(B) | II–III |
| Substantial investment, irrevocably committed, at risk | 9 FAM 402.9-6(B), -6(D); Matter of Walsh & Pollard | IV–V |
| Real and operating bona-fide enterprise | 9 FAM 402.9-6(A) | VI |
| More than marginal | 9 FAM 402.9-6(E); Matter of Ho (5-yr plan, by analogy) | VI |
| Develop and direct | 9 FAM 402.9-7(1) | VII |
| Nonimmigrant intent | 9 FAM 402.9-4(F) | NOID Tab |

### 3.2 Subtype 2 — Corporate-Owned E-2

Same as Subtype 1 PLUS:
- Corporate parent's treaty-country nationality and ≥50% treaty ownership of the parent itself (9 FAM 402.9-4(B)).

### 3.3 Subtype 3 — Executive/Supervisory Employee

The treaty enterprise itself must already qualify (Subtype 1 or 2). Then the
employee-specific elements:

| Element | Authority |
|---|---|
| Employee's treaty-country nationality (must match the enterprise's qualifying nationality) | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) |
| Position is genuinely executive or supervisory | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(17) |
| Title + organizational placement | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) |
| Duties = ultimate control or supervision of major component | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) |
| Number / skill level of supervised employees | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) |
| Salary commensurate with executive/supervisory role + industry + geography | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) |
| Prior executive/supervisory experience | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) |

**Important nuance:** An exec/supervisor that primarily does "routine
substantive staff work" with only incidental supervision FAILS this category.
The drafter must include the manual's **anti-routine paragraph**.

### 3.4 Subtype 4 — Essential Skills Employee (Camural)

Same enterprise-qualifying foundation as 3.3 PLUS:

| Element | Authority |
|---|---|
| Employee's treaty-country nationality | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b); 8 CFR 214.2(e)(18) |
| Special qualifications essential to operation | 8 CFR 214.2(e)(18) |
| Degree of proven expertise in the area | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Whether others possess the same skill | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Length of experience / training with the enterprise | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Training period required to do the job | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Skill's relationship to enterprise's specific processes / applications | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Salary the special qualifications command | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Skills NOT readily available in US labor market | 8 CFR 214.2(e)(18); 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |
| Foreign-language-and-culture alone is NOT enough | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) |

**Time-limited nature (CRITICAL drafting consideration):**
- Start-up skills may no longer be essential after initial operations.
- Some skills are essential only short-term for training local employees.
- Long-term essentiality requires showing product improvement / quality control / unique-service activities.

The drafter must ARGUE the projected duration and frame the skill as either
durably essential OR justify the limited-duration request honestly.

### 3.5 Subtype 5 — E-2 Spouse (I-539)

| Element | Authority |
|---|---|
| Marital relationship documented | 9 FAM 402.9-8 |
| Concurrent or subsequent to principal's filing | 9 FAM 402.9-8 |
| Intent to depart with principal | 9 FAM 402.9-4(F), 8 CFR 214.2(e)(5) |
| EAD eligibility (incident to status, no separate I-765 since 2022) | INA §214(e)(6) |

### 3.6 Subtype 6 — E-2 Child (I-539A)

| Element | Authority |
|---|---|
| Parent-child relationship documented (vital records + certified translation) | 9 FAM 402.9-8 |
| Under 21, unmarried | 9 FAM 402.9-8 |
| Concurrent with principal | 9 FAM 402.9-8 |

```typescript
type E2CaseSubtype = {

    | 'executive_supervisory_employee' // 3

  dependent_count: number;
```

`HIGH` — at least 3 strong signals align (e.g., letterhead + classification statement + role evidence).

- **Cover letter snippet:** none.
- **PII rule:** routing + account → never persist beyond active case. Drop from final memory pass.

## 2. Tab B — Cover Letter (Drafter Output, Not Input)

This Tab is **produced** by the Phase 3 drafter, not extracted. Its
structure must follow the firm's Roman-numeral section convention,
each section mirroring the Tab letter it relies on (II↔C, III↔D,
IV↔E, V↔F, VI↔G, VII↔H — note: Akalan has used this II-III-IV-V-VI-VII
ordering with VII for develop-and-direct and a final VIII for intent
to depart in some renewals; preserve the order from `01-COVER-LETTER-TEMPLATE.md`).

**Drafter anchors (must include in every cover letter):**

1. Statutory cite at first reference: `INA §101(a)(15)(E)(ii)`.
2. Regulatory cite: `8 CFR §214.2(e)`.
3. FAM cite per section: `9 FAM 402.9-4` (nationality), `-6(B)` (at-risk),
   `-6(C)` (substantiality), `-6(E)` (marginality), `-7` (develop & direct).
4. Inline exhibit citation format: `(Exhibit: <Title> dated <Date>)`.
5. Each section ends with: `Accordingly, <element> is satisfied under
   INA §… and 9 FAM 402.9-…`
6. Defined terms: `the Beneficiary` and `the Petitioner` — never first
   name, never "Applicant", never "Investor" alone.
7. Currency: `<ISO> <amount>.<cents>` always (e.g., `TRY 4,000,000.00`,
   `$120,000.00`).

**Defensive-drafting rule:** when an exhibit reflects foreign-jurisdiction
practice unfamiliar to USCIS, **insert the cultural/legal explanation
BEFORE the exhibit citation**. Example pattern (real Akalan phrasing):

> "Under [Treaty Country] law and customary practice, real property
> transfers are effected directly through the [official body], and a
> separate written sales contract is not issued in standard title deed
> transfers. Accordingly, the lawful sale of the Beneficiary's real
> property is evidenced through the official title deed records.
> (Exhibit: …)"

## 3. Tab C — Qualification Under a Treaty of Commerce and Navigation

- **Agent extraction schema:**
  ```json
  {

    "full_name_ascii": "<ASCII transliteration>",
    "passport_number": "<>",
    "country_of_issue": "<>",

    "date_of_expiration": "<YYYY-MM-DD>",

  }
  ```

- **Skill:** `passport-extractor` (vision pass, MRZ-aware).
- **Defensive drafting:** if expiration < 6 months from filing → flag (visa rules require ≥ 6 months validity).
- **Cover letter snippet:**

### 3.2 Prior E-2 Visa (Renewal Only)
- **What it is:** Visa stamp + I-797 approval notice from prior E-2 cycle.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** C.1. **APS:** 5. **Required for:** Renewal.
- **Element proven:** E1 (continuity) + supports E5 develop-and-direct.
- **Firm uses it for:** Demonstrate prior E-2 status, prior compliance, continuity.
- **Why it matters:** Renewal narrative requires prior status proof. Also evidences pattern of prior compliance for discretionary considerations under PA-2025-16.
- **Agent extraction:** prior visa number, validity dates, classification, port of entry, prior I-94 admit-until date.
- **Skill:** `visa-stamp-extractor` (vision).
- **Cover letter snippet (renewal):**

  > Visa.)"

### 3.3 Payroll Records for Prior E-2 Employment (Renewal Only)
- **What it is:** W-2s, pay stubs, payroll provider reports for the prior E-2 period.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** C.2. **APS:** 4. **Required for:** Renewal.
- **Element proven:** E1 + E5 (the Beneficiary actually worked the prior E-2 role) + E3/E4 (the Petitioner was actually paying employees).
- **Firm uses it for:** Three-fer — proves the Beneficiary worked, proves the Petitioner operated, proves prior compliance.
- **Why it matters:** USCIS scrutinizes whether the prior E-2 was actually used per its terms.
- **Agent extraction:** employer name+EIN, employee name, gross wages by quarter, withholdings, dates of employment.
- **Skill:** `payroll-extractor`.
- **Defensive drafting:** if any quarter shows zero wages while in E-2 → severity 3 conflict, attorney review.

### 3.4 Form I-94 Arrival/Departure Record
- **What it is:** CBP record of admission with class of admission (e.g., "E-2") and admit-until date.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** C.3. **APS:** 5.
- **Element proven:** Procedural / E1 continuity.
- **Firm uses it for:** Prove lawful status at time of filing (especially for COS or renewal).
- **Why it matters:** I-539 / COS eligibility depends on lawful status maintained.
- **Agent extraction:** admission number, class of admission, admit-until date, port of entry.
- **Skill:** `i94-extractor`.
- **Quality gate:** admit-until date must be ≥ filing date. If < filing date → status violation, escalate severity 5.

### 3.5 Co-Owner's EAD Card / Passport Showing Nationality
- **What it is:** Nationality proof for the OTHER member(s) whose nationality counts toward 50%+ treaty ownership.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** C.7. **APS:** 5.
- **Element proven:** E1 (50%+ treaty ownership).
- **Firm uses it for:** Show that the combined treaty-country ownership reaches the 50% threshold.
- **Why it matters:** 9 FAM 402.9-4(B). Without this, the nationality element fails even if the Beneficiary alone is a treaty national.
- **Agent extraction:** same passport schema as 3.1, plus EAD category if applicable.
- **Skill:** `passport-extractor` or `ead-extractor`.
- **Cover letter snippet:**
  > "`<<XX>>%` of the membership interest is held by `<<Beneficiary>>`,
  > a national of `<<Treaty Country>>`, and `<<XX>>%` is held by
  > `<<Co-Owner>>`, also a national of `<<Treaty Country>>`. Combined
  > treaty-country ownership equals `<<XX>>%`. (Exhibit: …)"

### 3.6 Membership Interest Transfer Agreement (cross-listed in C, D, E, F)
- See section 4.5 (Tab D) for full manual entry — this exhibit appears in multiple Tabs because it proves multiple elements simultaneously.

### 3.7 Member Resolutions
- **What it is:** Corporate resolutions adopted by the LLC members (consent / vote document).
- **Tab/Exhibit:** C.6 (also referenced in D.3, H.3). **APS:** 4.
- **Element proven:** E1 (formal acknowledgment of treaty owners) + E5 (appointment to executive role).
- **Firm uses it for:** Show that the ownership and management structure was formally adopted, not just contractual.
- **Agent extraction:** resolution date, resolutions adopted (verbatim), members present, signatures.
- **Skill:** `corporate-doc-extractor`.

## 5. Tab E — Investment: Source, Transfer, and At-Risk Commitment of Funds
**E-2 Element 2 + Source-of-Funds chain**

> **Why this Tab is the most rigorous:** SOF gaps are the #1 reason for E-2
> RFEs and denials. The agent must reconstruct a continuous trail from
> lawful origin → personal account → currency conversion → international
> wire → US business deployment, with **zero unexplained gaps over
> $10,000**. Per Master OS, any unexplained gap > $10K = attorney
> escalation.

### 5.1 Source / Origin Documents

#### 5.1.1 Prior Title Deed
- **What it is:** Government-issued land registry document showing the Beneficiary's PRIOR ownership of real property in the treaty country.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** E.1.a. **APS:** 5.
- **Element proven:** E2 — source of funds is lawful (own real property).
- **Firm uses it for:** Establish that the Beneficiary owned the property before sale.
- **Agent extraction:** parcel ID, property type, registration date, owner name (ASCII + native), registry office, location.
- **Skill:** `government-doc-extractor` + `foreign-language-translator` (capture original quote, transliterate to ASCII).

#### 5.1.2 Current Title Deed
- **What it is:** Land registry document confirming the property has been transferred to a new owner.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** E.1.b. **APS:** 5.
- **Element proven:** E2 — sale actually closed.
- **Firm uses it for:** Confirm the property transfer happened on `<<Date>>` and proceeds are lawful.
- **Agent extraction:** transfer date, new owner name, transfer registry number.
- **Defensive drafting:**
  > **MANDATORY** — when the treaty country effects transfers without a
  > separate sale contract (e.g., Turkey's Tapu Müdürlüğü), the cover
  > letter must explain this BEFORE introducing the exhibit. Real Akalan
  > phrasing:
  >
  > "Under `<<Treaty Country>>` law and customary practice, real
  > property transfers are effected directly through the `<<official
  > body — e.g., Land Registry Office (Tapu Mudurlugu)>>`, and a
  > separate written sales contract is not issued in standard title
  > deed transfers. Accordingly, the lawful sale of the Beneficiary's
  > real property is evidenced through the official title deed records."

#### 5.1.3 Bank Receipts Confirming Property Sale Proceeds
- **What it is:** Wire/transfer confirmations showing the buyer paid the Beneficiary.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** E.1.c. **APS:** 5.
- **Element proven:** E2 — funds actually received from named buyer.
- **Firm uses it for:** Build the per-installment table of receipts (multiple deposits totaling sale price).
- **Agent extraction:**
  ```json
  {
    "receipts": [
      {
        "date": "<YYYY-MM-DD>",
        "amount": "<currency + amount>",
        "from_name": "<buyer>",
        "from_account_last4": "<>",
        "to_name": "<Beneficiary>",
        "to_account_last4": "<>",
        "bank": "<>",
        "memo_or_remittance": "<>"
      }
    ]
  }
  ```
- **Skill:** `bank-receipt-extractor` (table extraction).
- **Why it matters:** Each receipt becomes a row in the SOF chain table. Sum of receipts must equal sale price stated in title deed transfer.
- **Cover letter narration pattern (real Akalan phrasing):**
  > "The initial transfer of `<<TRY 30,000.00>>` was made on
  > `<<Date>>`. The remaining portion of the deposit `<<TRY
  > 40,000.00>>` was made by the buyer, `<<Buyer Name>>` on
  > `<<Date>>`. On the sale day, `<<Date>>` the amount of `<<TRY
  > 2,900,000.00>>`, `<<TRY 1,000,000.00>>`, and `<<TRY 30,000.00>>`,
  > all credited to the same personal account of `<<Beneficiary>>` by
  > the buyer `<<Buyer Name>>`. (Exhibit: …)"

#### 5.1.4 Bank Statement (Sale Proceeds Period)
- **What it is:** Multi-month bank statement covering the period when sale proceeds were received.
- **Tab/Exhibit:** E.1.d. **APS:** 4.
- **Element proven:** E2 — receipts are not isolated, they exist within an authentic banking history.
- **Firm uses it for:** Corroborate the receipts and show no unexplained outflows.

#### 5.1.5 Residential Lease Agreement (Rental Income Source)

- **Tab/Exhibit:** E.1.e. **APS:** 4.
- **Element proven:** E2 — additional lawful income source.
- **Firm uses it for:** Anchor the rental income narrative (monthly amount, tenant, term).
- **Agent extraction:**
  ```json
  {
    "landlord_name": "<>",
    "landlord_relationship": "<Beneficiary | spouse | other>",
    "tenant_name": "<>",
    "tenant_account_last4": "<>",
    "monthly_rent_initial": "<currency + amount>",
    "monthly_rent_current": "<currency + amount>",
    "term": "<from / to>",
    "escalation_clause": "<verbatim>"
  }
  ```
- **Skill:** `contract-extractor`.
- **Defensive drafting cases:**
  - **Spouse-named lease:** If landlord is spouse not Beneficiary → MUST insert this real Akalan defense:
    > "Under U.S. immigration law, lawful income earned by a spouse
    > forms part of the family's household financial resources and may
    > be lawfully applied toward an E-2 investment. Accordingly, the
    > documented rental income received through the marital household
    > constitutes legitimate capital available to the Beneficiary for
    > investment purposes."
  - **Indexed escalation (TEFE/TUFE / CPI):** explain in footnote that the escalation is tied to officially published inflation indices issued by the national statistics authority. Real Akalan footnote pattern:
    > "In `<<Country>>`, residential lease agreements commonly
    > incorporate annual rent adjustments that are indexed to officially

    > market."

- **Firm uses it for:** Build the recurring-deposit table corroborating monthly rent claims.

### 6.3 Drafter Logic for Section V Substantiality

**Real Akalan approach (CRITICAL):** Akalan does **NOT** compute a strict
percentage proportionality in the cover letter. Instead, the firm argues
substantiality **qualitatively** in three moves:

1. **Industry context** — name the industry and describe its capital
   intensity (real Akalan: "a longstanding food-service establishment
   within the restaurant and delicatessen industry, a sector that
   requires continuous capital outlay for inventory procurement,
   equipment maintenance, vendor payments, staffing…").

2. **Absolute and proportional substantial** — assert both, without
   computing a ratio: "the Beneficiary's total investment commitment of
   `$<<Total>>`, of which `$<<At Risk>>` has already been irrevocably
   placed at risk … constitutes a substantial investment both in
   absolute terms and in proportion to the overall value and operational
   requirements of the enterprise."

3. **Loss-if-fails** — quantify economic motivation: "the amount
   invested … reflects a meaningful financial commitment that would
   result in significant financial loss to the Beneficiary should the
   enterprise fail."

Cite 9 FAM 402.9-6(C). Cite Membership Interest Transfer Agreement +
Petitioner's Balance Sheet.

**Reviewer's note:** This is a deliberate firm style. The reviewer
(Phase 4) should NOT flag the absence of a strict % ratio as a defect,
unless the case profile (high enterprise cost) suggests RFE risk on
proportionality. In that case, the reviewer suggests adding a ratio
table to Section V as an enhancement, but does not block.

**Defensive note for AI drafter:** Matter of Walsh and Pollard (Interim
Decision #3111) — not cited in the real Akalan filing for at-risk, but
SHOULD be cited if the case has any thin-margin facts (e.g., not all
funds yet expended, contingent on future earnings). The drafter should
add Walsh and Pollard when at-risk has any softness.

### 2.2 Mandatory content per section

#### INVESTMENT, ESTABLISHMENT, OWNERSHIP AND CONTROL
- Treaty-country nationality of enterprise's owners (≥ 50%)
- Parent → subsidiary corporate relationship
- Date of US subsidiary formation
- Initial investment amount + date + wire route
- Shareholder breakdown (Camural: Kontrolmatik TR 50%, Pomega TR 10%, Kontrolmatik US 7%, individual TR shareholders 32%, German 1% = ≥92% Turkish)
- Names + titles of executive leadership
- *Authority cite:* `INA §101(a)(15)(E)(ii)` and `9 FAM 402.9-4(B)`

#### DOING BUSINESS
- Industry, products, services
- Specific product lines
- Customer commitments / offtake agreements
- Capital equipment ownership / acquisition
- Inline `(Exhibit: …)` for each claim

#### PREMISES
- Operating address
- Lease agreement (commercial)
- For plant/facility builds: site selection narrative, land purchase

#### OPERATING PERSONNEL
- Current headcount (W-2 vs 1099 breakdown)
- Number of executives/managers vs total
- Reporting structure summary
- Projected headcount with growth narrative

#### FINANCIAL SITUATION AND FUTURE PLANS
- Capital deployment to date (verbatim figures with date stamps)
- Liquid assets, fixed assets, total worth
- Income statement summary
- 5-year strategic narrative (industry tailwinds, expansion plans, IRR/DSCR if known)
- Government incentives (PTC, IRA, state credits) where applicable
- *This section is the substantiality + marginality combined argument*

#### THE BENEFICIARY'S PROSPECTIVE ROLE
- Why the company NEEDS this role (commercial necessity narrative)
- Specific responsibilities (verbatim from Job Offer Letter)
- Reports-to structure (cite Org Chart)
- Required qualifications for the role
- Foreign-language requirements where applicable (Camural: English + Turkish for Pomega coordination)
- *This is where the "essential to operations" argument gets staged*

#### BENEFICIARY'S EMPLOYMENT HISTORY AND QUALIFICATIONS
- Education credentials
- Years of experience (≥ 10 typical)
- Specific employers + roles + responsibilities
- Geographic territories covered
- Quantified achievements (Camural: "doubled annual sales target in 2022 at SIEMENS")
- Key certifications + training
- LoR direct quote (italicized)
- Salary differential vs domestic peers — **CRITICAL** per 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b)
- Domestic salary in foreign country (TRY 140K vs TRY 45K average peer)
- US salary offered + benefits
- *Authority cite:* `9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b)` and `8 CFR §214.2(e)(18)`

#### CONCLUSION
- One-sentence summary tying labor force + income potential to public interest
- Specific validity dates requested
- Officer signature block

| Feature | Pattern |
|---|---|

## 5. Time-limited essentiality (drafter judgment)

Per 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b), some skills are essential only for short-term
training of locally hired employees, and some for the start-up phase only.
The drafter must position the case honestly:

| Pattern | Drafter framing | Validity to request |
|---|---|---|
| Plant build-out + employee training of US workforce | "essential during construction and ramp-up phase" | 2 years (typical Camural) |
| New product launch / quality control | "essential through commercial-scale production" | 2-3 years |
| Long-term ongoing role | "durable expertise unique to the enterprise's specific processes" | up to 5 years |

**Avoid:** characterizing the role as permanently essential when the facts
suggest training-then-handoff. Adjudicators flag overstated permanence.

## 1. DS-160 (Online Nonimmigrant Visa Application) for E-2 cases

**Form basics.** DS-160 is the universal NIV electronic application; every E-1, E-2, E-2S spouse, and E-2D child applicant files their own. It is hosted on the Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC). Confirmation page barcode (CEAC ID) is taken to interview. (`travel.state.gov / DS-160 — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)

### 1.1 E-2-specific DS-160 fields applicants commonly fumble

- **"Purpose of Trip" / Visa Class.** Must select the precise sub-class: `E1 - TREATY TRADER`, `E2 - TREATY INVESTOR`, `E2 - EXEC/MGR/ESSENTIAL EMP`, or the dependent variants `E2 - CHILD OF TREATY INVESTOR` / `E2 - SPOUSE OF TREATY INVESTOR`. Picking the wrong sub-class (e.g. principal investor selecting "Exec/Mgr/Essential Employee") routes the file to the wrong DS-156E rule path. (PATTERN; multiple practitioner sites — medium)
- **"Have you been issued a U.S. visa in the last ten years?"** plus prior-visa number. For renewals this MUST match the prior E-2 visa foil exactly; transposed digits are a documented 221(g) trigger. (`rnlawgroup.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, PATTERN, medium)
- **Present/Previous Employer block.** Salary, title, address, and dates must reconcile with the DS-156E Part III and any I-129 record. Material divergence ("CEO" on DS-156E vs "Director" on DS-160) is itself a 221(g) flag. (`rnlawgroup.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, PATTERN, medium)
- **"Have you ever been refused a U.S. visa, been refused admission, or had a visa cancelled?"** Any prior 221(g) — including the present one if previously issued — counts as a refusal under DOS practice. False "No" answers are the single most cited DS-160 defect. (`thecassellfirm.com / kasturilaw.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, PATTERN, high)
- **Unlawful presence / overstay question.** A common 2025 221(g) source per Reddy Neumann Brown. (`rnlawgroup.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, PATTERN, medium)
- **Travel companions / Travel paid by.** For E-2 employees, the "paid by" must name the U.S. enterprise, not the principal investor personally — mismatches cue source-of-funds questions. (PATTERN; practitioner — low)

### 1.2 2025-26 DS-160 updates

- **Public-social-media requirement (DS-160 social handles).** Effective **18 June 2025** State announced that F, M, J applicants must set social profiles to "public"; expansion to **H-1B / H-4 effective 15 Dec 2025**. E-category is NOT yet listed in the public-profile mandate, but the 5-year handle disclosure on DS-160 has applied to all NIV applicants since 2019 and is unchanged. (`colorado.edu/isss/2025/07/16 — retrieved 2026-04-28`; `tandslaw.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high) **[NEEDS VERIFICATION whether DOS extended the public-profile rule to E-category by April 2026.]**
- **In-person rule.** Effective **2 September 2025** all NIV applicants regardless of age must appear in person; the prior under-14 / over-79 exemption is gone. Applies fully to E-2D children. (`travel.state.gov / news; multiple practitioner aggregators — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)
- **"Visa Integrity Fee" — $250.** Enacted in HR-1 ("One Big Beautiful Bill Act") on **4 July 2025**, charged at issuance (not application). As of late March 2026 the payment mechanism is still not operational and Bureau of Consular Affairs has not pushed implementation guidance to posts. (`cnbc.com / 2025-07-18`; `gtlaw-insidebusinessimmigration.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT for the law / PATTERN for "not yet operational", medium) **[NEEDS VERIFICATION when DOS turns the fee on.]**

## 2. DS-156E (Nonimmigrant Treaty Trader / Investor Application)

### 2.1 Form metadata
- OMB Control Number **1405-0101**, current OMB approval **expires 30 April 2026**, latest 30-day Federal Register notice 28 Aug 2024 (extension and revision). (`federalregister.gov 2024-19259; omb.report 1405-0101 — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)

### 2.2 Who must file DS-156E (FACT, high — `9 FAM 402.9-9` and `travel.state.gov/treaty-trader-investor` — retrieved 2026-04-28)

- **Required:**
  - All E-1 treaty trader applicants (Parts I, II, III).
  - E-2 **Executive / Manager / Essential Employee** applicants (Parts I, II, III) — Parts I and II are completed by the qualifying U.S. enterprise; Part III by each individual employee applicant.
- **NOT required:**
  - **Principal E-2 investors** applying as the owner of the enterprise (DOS treats the substantive showing as built into the consular package + DS-160 + supporting binder, not a separate DS-156E). This is the most-missed piece for U.S. counsel transitioning from I-129 practice.
  - **All E-1 / E-2 derivatives** (spouse, children under 21).

> Practical note: when a single treaty enterprise sends multiple essential-employee applicants over a 5-year window, **Parts I and II can be re-used** if no material change has occurred and the enterprise is registered with the post's E-Visa Unit; only Part III is re-filed per applicant. (`9 FAM 402.9-9` "company registration" guidance, expanded 2023 — `ogletree.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)

### 2.3 Section-by-section content

**Part I — Petitioner / Employer (signed by an authorized officer of the U.S. enterprise; not the applicant)**
- Legal name, d/b/a, U.S. address, FEIN, date and state of incorporation
- Type of business (NAICS-style category) and whether sole proprietorship / partnership / corporation / LLC / branch of foreign parent
- Treaty country of nationality of the enterprise (≥50% ownership test)
- Names and nationalities of owners holding ≥50%
- (`ds156_e.PDF — eforms.state.gov — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high; field-level details corroborated by `boundless.com/form-ds-156e-explained — retrieved 2026-04-28`, medium)

**Part II — Investment / Trade / Staffing (signed by the same enterprise officer)**
- Total amount invested + breakdown by category (real property, equipment, inventory, working capital, lease deposits, fees, etc.) — this is the **substantiality** showing
- Source of investment funds (personal savings / loan / sale of asset / gift) and whether funds are "at risk" and committed
- Annual gross / net income, US-source revenue
- Total U.S. employees by category (US workers, treaty-country nationals in E status, principal investor), broken out as essential vs ordinarily skilled
- Future hiring plan (typical 5-year horizon)
- (`ds156_e.PDF — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)

**Part III — Applicant / Employee (signed by the individual applicant)**
- Position title, salary, duration sought, % time in supervisory vs hands-on duties
- Education, prior experience, language skills, and **why the role is "essential"** (for essential-employee track) — i.e. specialized skills not readily available in the U.S. labor market
- Prior U.S. status / prior E visas
- (`ds156_e.PDF — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)

### 2.4 What attaches to DS-156E (PATTERN aggregated from consulate post checklists, medium)
- Company registration / good-standing certificate, articles of incorporation, ownership chart proving treaty-nationality
- Business plan with 5-year pro forma (especially for new enterprises — marginality rebuttal)
- Tax returns (federal + state), audited financials or CPA-prepared statements, bank statements
- Lease, equipment receipts, payroll records (I-9s, W-2s, quarterly 941)
- For source of funds: bank wires, gift letters, sale-of-asset proof traced to the U.S. business account
- For Part III applicant: CV, diplomas (with translations), professional licenses, prior employment verification, salary letter

### 2.5 How DS-156E differs substantively from the I-129 E supplement

| Dimension | I-129 + E supplement | DS-156E |
|---|---|---|
| Adjudicator | USCIS service center (CSC for E-2) | Consular officer + post's E-Visa Unit (E-VU) / Treaty Visa Unit |
| Standard of review | Preponderance, deference to prior approvals codified | Same substantive law (INA 101(a)(15)(E), 8 CFR 214.2(e), 9 FAM 402.9) but officer reviews **de novo** at first-time consular case (`9 FAM 402.9-7` — high) |
| Form field granularity | I-129 Supplement E asks for fewer line items; cover letter carries the load | DS-156E itself **contains** the substantiality table — Part II line-by-line breakdown of investment is mandatory on the form, not just in the memo |
| Dependents | Filed on I-539 separately | No DS-156E required; covered by their own DS-160 only |
| Re-use across applicants | None — each I-129 stands alone | Parts I & II re-usable across employees within the registration window (5-year customary review) |

### 2.6 Common DS-156E errors (PATTERN, medium)
- Listing the **foreign parent** as the U.S. employer in Part I (Part I asks for the U.S. enterprise that is the qualifying business)
- Investment "at risk" total in Part II that double-counts uncommitted funds
- Part III "essential" narrative recycled from I-129 cover letter without tightening to the consular standard ("specialized" + "for a finite period to train U.S. workers" or "qualifications not readily available")
- Mismatched signature dates across Parts I/II/III (post will sometimes refuse to accept)
- Old form revision (the eforms.state.gov master is the only authoritative version)

## 3. Pre-interview document package — the "interview binder"

### 3.1 Tabbed binder structure (PATTERN — corroborated by U.S. Embassy France required-format page and the multi-post template hosted on `common.usembassy.gov/.../E2-Requirements.pdf — retrieved 2026-04-28`; medium-high)

A typical post-published structure (applicant brings two identical binders — one for the post to retain, one to keep):

- **Section A — Cover Letter (legal memorandum) + Table of Contents**
- **Section B — Forms:** DS-160 confirmation; DS-156E Parts I, II, III; MRV fee receipt
- **Section C — Applicant-personal:** passport bio page, prior visas, photos, CV, diplomas, marriage / birth certificates for derivatives
- **Section D — Treaty nationality of business:** ownership chart, share certificates / cap table, passports of ≥50% owners
- **Section E — Real & Operating enterprise:** articles of incorporation, certificate of good standing, business licenses, lease, utility bills
- **Section F — Substantial investment:** source-of-funds tracing, wire receipts, escrow / closing statements, equipment invoices, capital-expenditure ledger
- **Section G — Not marginal:** business plan with 5-year financial projections, current P&L, payroll showing U.S. workers, tax returns
- **Section H — Develop & Direct (principal) OR Essential / Executive role (employee):** ownership %, board minutes, organizational chart with applicant boxed; for employees the Part III qualifications proof
- **Section I — Intent to depart / non-immigrant intent:** statement of intent, foreign ties evidence (real estate, family, foreign tax residency)

### 3.2 Order consular officers actually consume (PATTERN, medium — synthesized from consulate FAQ pages and practitioner guides incl. Scott Legal, Reddy Neumann Brown — retrieved 2026-04-28)

1. **Cover letter** — first 2 pages skimmed
2. **DS-156E Part II** — substantiality table
3. **Source-of-funds tab** — they pull random wires and ask "where did this come from"
4. **Treaty-nationality proof** — ownership chart
5. **Business plan first page + financial projections summary**
6. Everything else only on follow-up question.

### 3.3 The consular analog of the "I-129 cover letter"

It is a **legal memorandum to the consulate** — usually 8–15 pages — addressed `Dear Consular Officer` (not `Director` as in I-129). Practitioner conventions (PATTERN, medium):
- Element-by-element walk-through of `9 FAM 402.9-3` through `402.9-7` (treaty-country, substantial investment, real & operating, not marginal, develop & direct, source of funds)
- For each element, parenthetical pinpoint to the exact tab letter and exhibit number ("see Tab F-3, wire receipt of 12 Mar 2025")
- Closing paragraph stating **unequivocal intent to depart** when E status ends — the consular standard differs from USCIS in that nonimmigrant intent is litigated more closely at the post (`9 FAM 402.9-4` and Australia E-3 guidance carry this through — `ogletree.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, high)

### 3.4 Originals + copies; translations
- **Originals** at the interview window for inspection of: passport, marriage / birth certificates, diplomas. (`travel.state.gov` — high)
- **Copies** in the binder (NEVER staple originals into the binder).
- **Translations:** any non-English document needs a **certified English translation** under 22 CFR 41.103. The translator certifies competence; notarization is not required by DOS but several posts request it. (FACT, high — `22 CFR 41.103`; PATTERN at post level — medium)

## 4. Photograph specifications

### 4.1 Specs (FACT, `travel.state.gov/photos.html — retrieved 2026-04-28`, high)
- **Square**, 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) printed; digital upload 600 × 600 px, JPEG, ≤ 240 kB
- **Head size:** 1 to 1 ⅜ inches (22–35 mm) from chin to top of head, OR 50%–69% of total image height
- **Background:** plain white or off-white
- **Recency:** taken within the **last 6 months**
- **No eyeglasses** (since 1 Nov 2016) absent rare medical exception with documentation
- **No headwear** unless religious daily wear; full face visible, no shadows

### 4.2 Common rejection reasons (FACT, `travel.state.gov/photos.html — retrieved 2026-04-28`, high)
- Wrong head size (most frequent)
- Tinted or colored background (cream, gray, blue)
- Eyeglasses worn
- Shadow on face or behind head
- Digitally altered / "beautified" / smartphone "portrait mode" depth-of-field artifact
- Old photo (>6 months) — caught by visible appearance change vs DS-160 file
- Print quality (vending machine, low-DPI inkjet)

## 6. Post-issuance / port-of-entry steps

### 6.1 Visa stamp annotations (FACT, `9 FAM 402.9-9` annotation guidance — corroborated by `legalservicesincorporated.com / kpmg.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, high)

The visa foil's **"Annotation"** field carries a coded string the consular officer types in:

| Annotation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `E-2 PRIN` (or `PRINCIPAL`) | Principal treaty investor |
| `E-2 EMPL OF [COMPANY], EXP [date]` | Executive / Manager / Essential Employee — must name the qualifying enterprise and a tie-back date |
| `E-2D SP, [PRINCIPAL NAME]` | E-2 dependent spouse, naming the principal |
| `E-2D CH, [PRINCIPAL NAME]` | E-2 dependent child, naming the principal |

Some posts now include the **petition number / E-VU registration number and expiration**, per the 2023 9 FAM update on company registration (`ogletree.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, high).

### 6.2 I-94 issuance and the 2-year admission rule
- E-2 admissions are **2 years per entry** under **8 CFR 214.2(e)(20)**, regardless of how many years the visa foil itself is valid. Re-entering on a 5-year visa resets the 2-year I-94 each time. (FACT, high — `8 CFR 214.2(e)(20)`)
- The I-94 is generated electronically at POE; CBP issues a paper Form I-94 only at land borders or on request. Always retrieve at **i94.cbp.dhs.gov** post-arrival to confirm Class of Admission and expiration.

### 6.3 The "S" annotation for E spouses (post-Nov 2021 / Jan 2022)
- USCIS / CBP began issuing I-94s with **`E-2S`** Class of Admission for E-2 spouses on **30–31 January 2022**, implementing the November 2021 USCIS Policy Manual change. (`uscis.gov / Policy Manual Vol 10 Pt B Ch 2 — retrieved 2026-04-28`; `ogletree.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)
- An unexpired `E-2S` I-94 is **work authorization incident to status** — accepted as **List C document for Form I-9** (List C #7 acceptable receipt category per USCIS guidance). No EAD (I-765) is required, though some employers still ask for one. (`uscis.gov — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)
- E-2 children remain `E-2D` (no work authorization).
- Pre-Jan-2022 spouses who still hold `E-2` (no S) I-94s received transitional notices treating the I-94 plus the notice as I-9 evidence. (`uscis.gov — retrieved 2026-04-28`, FACT, high)

### 6.4 Common POE issues (PATTERN, medium)
- CBP officer admits the spouse with `E-2` instead of `E-2S` Class of Admission — must request a **deferred-inspection appointment** to correct the I-94, otherwise employer cannot complete I-9 on List C.
- I-94 expiration set to passport expiration (the **lesser-of-passport rule**) when passport has under 2 years validity — common for Turkish, Pakistani applicants — collapses the expected 2-year admission window.
- Annotation foil omits the company name on E-2 EMPL — POE officer may ask for the original visa-application binder; bring it in carry-on.

## 7. 221(g) "administrative processing" patterns — E-2 specific (2025-26)

### 7.1 What 221(g) is (FACT, high — `travel.state.gov/administrative-processing-information — retrieved 2026-04-28`)
A **refusal under INA § 221(g)** is technically a denial that the consulate may overcome upon: (a) further documents from applicant, (b) inter-agency security check (SAO Donkey, Hawk, Mantis, etc.), or (c) policy review. Passport may be held or returned.

### 7.2 E-2-specific 221(g) triggers (PATTERN, medium — synthesized `rnlawgroup.com`, `infoimmigration.com`, `migratemate.co`, `npzlaw.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`)

1. **Source-of-funds gap** — wire from a third party (parent, holding co.) without a gift letter or board resolution
2. **Substantiality near the floor** — investment under ~$100K with thin business-plan numbers
3. **Speculative / not-yet-operational enterprise** — pre-revenue, no employees, lease only signed
4. **Marginality concern** — single-investor consulting LLC with no projected hires and modest profit
5. **Treaty nationality unclear** — multi-tier holding structure with non-treaty intermediate parent
6. **Prior visa file inconsistency** — DS-160 / DS-156E / I-129 record divergence
7. **Security checks** — SAO triggered by name match, dual-use technology business (semiconductors, drones, biotech), or country of birth
8. **Social-media flag** — increasingly cited 2025+ when public profiles show employment that contradicts DS-160

### 7.3 Typical resolution timeline (PATTERN, medium)
- Documents-only 221(g): **2–8 weeks** after submission; some posts auto-close in 90 days if applicant doesn't respond
- Security-check (SAO) 221(g): **30–180 days**, occasional 6+ months for sensitive-technology cases
- Policy-review (E-VU re-registration / re-look at company): **30–120 days**

### 7.4 What to do while pending (PATTERN, medium)
- Track CEAC status; if "Refused" stays > 60 days, send a polite written follow-up to the post via post-specific E-Visa Unit email (most posts publish one)
- If passport returned, the applicant **may travel internationally but cannot enter the U.S.** until the visa is issued
- LegalNet inquiry to State (`LegalNet@state.gov`) if attorney follow-up; Congressional inquiry (Senate / House liaison) past 90–120 days
- Mandamus litigation only after typically 6+ months unreasonable delay

## 8. Consular interview prep — practitioner approach

### 8.1 Mock interview question list — E-2 specific (PATTERN, medium — synthesized `rnlawgroup.com`, `pandevlaw.com`, `nnuimmigration.com`, `e2visalawyer.net — retrieved 2026-04-28`)

**Business questions (most weight)**
- "Tell me about your business in 30 seconds."
- "What does your company do, who are your customers, and how do you make money?"
- "How many U.S. employees do you have today, and what's your hiring plan for the next 2 years?"
- "What's the address of your office? Walk me through what's there."

**Investment / source of funds**
- "How much have you invested, and on what?"
- "Where did the money come from? Walk me through each wire."
- "Are these funds at risk if the business fails?"
- "Was any of the investment financed? By whom, and is it secured by your personal assets?"

**Role**
- "What is your title? What do you do day to day?"
- "Who reports to you? Who do you report to?"
- (Essential employee) "Why can't a U.S. worker do this job? Have you trained anyone? When will the U.S. worker take over?"

**Intent to depart**
- "What will you do when your E-2 ends?"
- "Do you have a home / family / business interests in [home country]?"

**Treaty nationality**
- "Who owns the company? Are they citizens of [treaty country]?"

### 8.2 Model answer structure (PATTERN, medium)

For each question, the practitioner-coached pattern is **short → specific → tab-pointer**:
> *"I have invested $325,000 since opening, primarily in equipment and three months of working capital. The funds came from the sale of my Istanbul apartment in March 2025; the closing statement and the wire to our U.S. business account are in Tab F-2 if you'd like to see them."*

Avoid: monologue, jargon, hedging, memorized scripts. Officers are trained to detect coaching (`pandevlaw.com — retrieved 2026-04-28`, PATTERN, medium).

### 8.3 When the officer pulls in the spouse vs. only interviews principal (PATTERN, medium)
- Most posts **interview principal alone** when family members are derivative; spouses appear at the window for fingerprinting, oath, and document submission only.
- Spouse pulled in for substantive questioning when:
  - Joint property / financial co-mingling on source-of-funds is material
  - Marriage genuineness is queried (rare for E-2 vs K-1)
  - Spouse is an **owner** in their own right (joint investors → both treated as principals)
  - Spouse intends to use `E-2S` work authorization at a sensitive-industry employer

## 1. The "In Process of Investing" Doctrine — Walsh & Pollard

### 1.1 The case (FACT, high confidence)

**Citation.** *Matter of Walsh and Pollard*, 20 I&N Dec. 60 (BIA 1988) (Interim Decision #3111). Justice.gov EOIR Volume 20 publishes the full opinion at `justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2012/08/14/3111.pdf` (retrieved 2026-04-28; PDF binary, text not parsed by agent — see methodology note).

**Facts the BIA credited as qualifying as "in the process of investing"** (corroborated by Berardi Immigration Law, Visa Business Plans, and AILA secondary; retrieved 2026-04-28):

- IAD Ltd. (a UK automotive design company) was contracted by GM to redesign GM's car line.
- IAD created a U.S. subsidiary specifically to perform the contract.
- **Concrete pre-revenue acts the Board credited:** rented office space; purchased office furniture; hired two U.S. citizens to assist UK designers in their relocation; established a corporate bank account funded with **USD 15,000**.
- The BIA held this satisfied the "actively in the process of investing" prong, and that the design engineers — though not in supervisory or managerial roles — were essential employees and qualified for E-2.

**Walsh & Pollard holdings relevant to E-2 NEW filings:**
1. There is **no fixed dollar minimum** for E-2 substantiality. The "amount normally considered necessary to establish a viable enterprise of the nature contemplated" is the test. (FACT, high confidence — restated identically across all secondary sources retrieved 2026-04-28.)
2. A foreign treaty national must have **either** invested **or** be **actively in the process of investing**. The "process of investing" prong is alive and well — but it requires more than intention. (FACT, high confidence.)
3. The Board treated **lease + furniture + initial hires + funded operating account** as qualifying acts, even though the U.S. subsidiary had earned no revenue at the time of the visa adjudication. (FACT, high confidence.)

### 1.2 What "irrevocably committed" requires (per 9 FAM 402.9-6(B))

**Citation.** 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) (`fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040209.html`) (retrieved 2026-04-28; live URL returned a TLS error from the agent's fetch — quoted language confirmed across U.S. Embassy Chile's republication, e2visalawyer.net's PDF mirror, and Beyond Border Global summary, all retrieved 2026-04-28). FAM-quoted passages flagged `[primary text not parsed — corroborated by ≥3 secondary]`:

- "An investment conditioned upon the issuance of the E-2 visa **may still qualify** if the assets are held in escrow." Despite the condition, the purchase constitutes a "solid commitment" if the assets are held in escrow for release or transfer once the condition is met. (FACT, high confidence; corroborated by 3 secondary sources.)
- The funds must be (i) the investor's possession, (ii) committed, (iii) at risk in the commercial sense, with possibility of partial or total loss. (FACT, high confidence.)
- **"Mere intent to invest, or possession of uncommitted funds in a bank account, is not enough."** This is the bright line — and is the language that defeats most weak E-2 NEW filings. (FACT, high confidence per all FAM republications retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 1.3 How the doctrine reads in 2025-26 (PATTERN, medium confidence)

Practitioners (Visa Business Plans, U.S. Immigration Plan, Beyond Border Global, all retrieved 2026-04-28) converge on three operational tests for "irrevocably committed" in 2025-26 consular practice:

1. **Spent.** Cancelled checks, wire confirmations, paid invoices for premises build-out, equipment, inventory, franchise fees, retainers. *(Strongest.)*
2. **Escrowed with restricted release.** Funds in a written escrow that release **only** on visa issuance and which (a) commit to a specific named transaction (lease assumption, asset purchase, share purchase) and (b) limit any clawback strictly to a denial event — **not** "any reason" withdrawal. *(Acceptable if written tightly; see §4.)*
3. **Letter of Intent (LOI) / pending purchase agreement.** Weakest. Generally insufficient on its own per consular practice 2025-26.

**FAM-anchored takeaway for E-2 NEW.** Walsh & Pollard authorizes a pre-revenue posture, but only if the file shows the kind of concrete deployment that put the *Walsh* applicants over the line: a leased premises, paid-for tangible setup, and at least one operating-account act. A bank balance plus a business plan, with nothing else, will not survive 9 FAM 402.9-6(B).

## 2. Pre-Launch Evidence Package (No Revenue Yet)

This is the central E-2 NEW scenario the renewal manual does not handle.

### 2.1 What substitutes for "operating history"

When the business is formed, funds are deployed (lease signed and paid, equipment purchased, build-out underway), but no customers exist yet, the file must reconstruct an "operational" picture **out of deployment evidence**:

| Renewal exhibit (Kacar-Salih shape) | E-2 NEW substitute (pre-launch) |
|---|---|
| 12+ months of business bank statements | Petitioner operating account opened + initial capital deposits + first vendor outflows (≥3 months ideal, ≥1 month minimum) |
| Federal tax return (G.6, APS 5) | EIN assignment letter (CP-575) **+** state sales-tax / employer registration **+** if the business has been operating <12 months at filing, partial-year P&L or unaudited interim financials |
| Payroll records / W-2s (G.7) | Offer letters with start dates, payroll-provider contract (e.g., Gusto / ADP setup confirmation), I-9-ready new-hire packet, **hiring-timetable schedule** in the business plan |
| Photos of operating premises (G.1) | Photos of leased premises during build-out: dated, with progress (demo → rough-in → finish), plus general-contractor agreement and progress invoices |
| Vendor invoices showing recurring procurement (G.5) | One-time setup invoices: equipment purchase orders, POS / merchant-processor onboarding, insurance binder, opening inventory order |
| Merchant processing statements | Merchant-account application + approval letter (Stripe / Square / bank merchant) and processor agreement |

(PATTERN, high confidence — converges across Pollak PLLC, Gozel Law, Pass Right, Alaz Law, Lighthouse HQ E-2 guides, all retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 2.2 Mandatory pre-launch exhibits (FACT, high confidence)

The minimum pre-launch evidence package consistently described across practitioner sources (≥3-source rule met; retrieved 2026-04-28):

1. **Articles of Organization / Incorporation** (state-filed; same as renewal Tab D.1).
2. **Operating Agreement** — naming Beneficiary and showing executive role from day one.
3. **EIN assignment letter (IRS CP-575)** — anchors federal recognition of the entity.
4. **Commercial lease, fully executed** — with rent paid through filing date, plus paid invoices for security deposit + first month + any prepaid CAM. The lease, **plus proof of payment**, is the single strongest non-revenue at-risk exhibit.
5. **Build-out contract or franchise development agreement** with **progress payments shown** — invoiced, paid, and dated.
6. **Equipment purchase orders + paid invoices + delivery proof** (bills of lading or delivery receipts).
7. **Insurance binder** — general liability, property, workers' comp once first hire is set.
8. **State / local licensing applications and permits** — where issuance precedes opening (food service, liquor, professional licenses, daycare, etc.).
9. **Merchant account onboarding documents** — signed application, approval letter.
10. **Hiring file** — at least one signed offer letter with concrete start date prior to or shortly after admission, plus payroll provider contract.
11. **Business plan** (5-year, Matter-of-Ho-compliant — see §3).
12. **Source-of-funds memo + chain table** — same discipline as Tab E in renewal manual, but generally higher-volume because the deployment is the only at-risk proof.
13. **Photographs** — premises in current state, with date-stamps; equipment in place.
14. **Beneficiary CV + executive role appointment** — same as renewal Tab H, but emphasizes industry track record substituting for absent operating history.

### 2.3 Practitioner playbook — the "open-on-day-of-admission" posture (PATTERN, medium-high confidence)

Across U.S. Immigration Plan, Pollak PLLC, and Pass Right (retrieved 2026-04-28), the recommended posture for an initial filing where launch has not yet occurred is:

- File should describe the business as **"immediately capable of commencing operations upon Beneficiary's admission."**
- Cover-letter language should pair this phrase with citation to 9 FAM 402.9-6(D) (bona fide enterprise / real and operating) and Walsh & Pollard's "in the process of investing" prong.
- Hiring timetable (item 10 above) must be specific: month, role, FT/PT, target wage band — **NOT** "as needed."

**Confidence note.** Practitioner sources are unanimous here — but this is a doctrine of practice, not regulation. Marked PATTERN.

## 3. The 5-Year Business Plan for E-2 NEW

### 3.1 The Matter of Ho standard (FACT, high confidence)

**Citation.** *Matter of Ho*, 22 I&N Dec. 206 (Assoc. Comm'r 1998) (justice.gov EOIR Volume 22, retrieved 2026-04-28; primary text not parsed by agent — see methodology note). Quoted standard reproduced consistently across Joorney, Visa Business Plans, Plan Writers, EB5 Insights (all retrieved 2026-04-28):

> A petitioner must submit "a comprehensive business plan" that is "**comprehensive, detailed, and credible**." A "comprehensive business plan as contemplated by the regulations should contain, at a minimum, a description of the business's products and/or services, its objectives, market analysis, including the names of competing businesses and their relative strengths and weaknesses, a comparison of the competition's products and pricing structures, a description of the target market/prospective customers of the new commercial enterprise, a description of any manufacturing or production processes, materials required, and supply sources, details of any contracts executed for the supply of materials and/or the distribution of products, a description of marketing strategy including pricing, advertising, and servicing, a description of the business's organizational structure and its personnel's experience, **a staffing timetable**, and **job descriptions for all positions**, including a specification of those positions that will be held by qualifying employees… as well as **sales, cost, and income projections** and details of the **bases therefor**." (Quoted via Joorney's reproduction of *Ho*, retrieved 2026-04-28.)

Although *Ho* is an EB-5 case, the standard is **applied by analogy to E-2 NEW** because the business plan plays the same evidentiary role: it is the document from which the adjudicator infers viability, capacity to support more than the investor's family (E-2 marginality), and the develop-and-direct strategy. Practitioner consensus is unanimous on this analogical use (Visa Business Plans, Joorney, Pass Right, Alaz Law, retrieved 2026-04-28). FACT, high confidence on the analogical use; FACT, high confidence on the *Ho* elements.

### 3.2 Sections that must absolutely be in an E-2 NEW plan

Synthesized minimum (PATTERN, high confidence; ≥3-source rule met, retrieved 2026-04-28):

1. **Executive summary** — investor, role, investment, business model, 5-year hiring count, 5-year revenue.
2. **Industry analysis** — capital intensity, growth rate, regulatory environment. Footnote every assumption to a primary source.
3. **Market analysis** — TAM/SAM/SOM with footnoted demographics. For local-services businesses, county-level Census data + BLS QCEW + drive-time analysis.
4. **Competitive analysis** — named competitors, SWOT.
5. **Operations** — premises, suppliers, hours, processes, technology stack.
6. **Marketing & sales strategy** — channels with cost.
7. **Organizational chart + staffing timetable + job descriptions** — see §3.4.
8. **Capital deployment table** — line by line, tied to the SOF chain.
9. **5-year financial projections** — P&L, cash flow, balance sheet. Year 1 monthly, Year 2 quarterly, Years 3-5 annual (PATTERN, U.S. Immigration Plan + Alaz Law + Pass Right, retrieved 2026-04-28).
10. **Assumption appendix** — every revenue, COGS, and labor assumption footnoted.

### 3.3 Common failure patterns ("Matter of Ho paper plan") — what defeats them

The "paper plan" failure mode — a glossy deck with no underlying support — fails *Ho*'s "credible" prong. Concrete failure patterns:

| Failure pattern | What kills the plan | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| Hockey-stick revenue without footnoted assumptions | Adjudicator marks "speculative" | Footnote each assumption to IBISWorld / BLS QCEW / FRED / Statista / FDD Item 19 with retrieval date |
| "We will hire as needed" or "5 employees by Year 5" with no schedule | Marginality denial | Month-by-month staffing timetable Year 1; quarterly Y2; annual Y3-5 — each line tied to a job description |
| Stock industry ratios (e.g., "restaurant labor 30%") with no source | Loss of credibility | Cite IBISWorld report number, FDD Item 19 if franchise, or industry trade-association data |
| Identical FDD-replicated language for franchise plans | "Stock business plan" rejection — see §6 | Customize with locale-specific demographics, drive-time analysis, named local competitors |
| No customer-acquisition cost model | Plan can't support revenue ramp | CAC × volume = marketing spend; tied to channel mix |
| Reliance on independent contractors / "1099 only" | 2026 marginality red flag — see §3.4 | At least 3-5 W-2 hires by Y5; first within 12-18 months |
| No reinvestment line | Looks like personal-living plan | Show retained-earnings reinvestment — explicit line in cash-flow |

(PATTERN, high confidence; converges across U.S. Immigration Plan, Alaz Law, Pass Right, Pandev, ALG Lawyers — all retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 3.4 W-2 hiring timetable — 2025-26 RFE language

**FACT (medium-high confidence; PATTERN at consular post level).** Practitioner reports for 2025-26 (U.S. Immigration Plan, Pass Right, Alaz Law, retrieved 2026-04-28):

- Adjudicators expect **first W-2 hire within 12–18 months** of operations.
- Plans relying on gig-workers / 1099s are flagged.
- A defensible target band is **3–5 W-2 FTEs by Year 5**, more for capital-intensive industries.
- Each hire must have: title, FT/PT, anticipated wage band, month of hire, sourcing channel, prerequisite credentials.

> Cover-letter snippet pattern (drafter-ready, derived from Akalan voice corpus — to be added to the manual):
> "The Petitioner's five-year staffing plan, set forth in the enclosed business plan, projects an initial W-2 hire of `<<role>>` in `<<Month YY>>` at a wage band of `$<<low>>–<<high>>` per annum, with subsequent additions of `<<role>>` in `<<Month YY>>` and `<<role>>` in `<<Month YY>>`. Year-five projected headcount is `<<N>>` W-2 employees, demonstrating that the enterprise is not — and will not be — a marginal operation supporting only the Beneficiary and his family. (Exhibit: Business Plan §`<<X>>`; Exhibit: Job Descriptions Schedule.)"

### 3.5 Industry assumption sourcing (FACT, high confidence)

Sources practitioners cite as primary for E-2 NEW assumption footnoting:

- **IBISWorld** — sector revenue, growth, profitability ratios. Cite report number and date.
- **BLS QCEW (Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages)** — county-level wage benchmarks for staffing-cost defense.
- **FRED (St. Louis Fed)** — macro indicators, regional labor cost indices.
- **Statista** — supplementary, only for consumer-facing metrics not in BLS.
- **FDD Item 19 (Financial Performance Representations)** — for franchise cases only; cite Item 19 page and FDD year.
- **U.S. Census Bureau ACS** — demographics for market analysis.
- **State and county economic development authorities** — sometimes provide free trade-area studies.

Footnote format the agent should enforce: `IBISWorld Report 72211, "Limited-Service Restaurants in the U.S.," September 2025, p. 14 (retrieved 2026-04-28)`.

## 4. Funds Escrow vs. Funds Spent — The At-Risk Distinction

### 4.1 The hierarchy practitioners use (PATTERN, high confidence)

From U.S. Immigration Plan (Feb 2026), Beyond Border Global, Visa Business Plans, e2visalawyer.net (all retrieved 2026-04-28). Three-source rule met:

```
SPENT  >  ESCROWED (denial-only release)  >  ESCROWED (general clawback)  >  LOI
```

- **SPENT** is always strongest. Cancelled checks, wires, paid invoices.
- **ESCROW with denial-only clawback** is acceptable per FAM (see §1.2) when the agreement (a) names the specific transaction, (b) restricts release to visa-issuance / admission, and (c) restricts clawback to denial.
- **ESCROW with "any-reason clawback"** is increasingly rejected as speculative (PATTERN, multiple practitioner sources, 2025-26).
- **LOI / unsigned PSA** is generally insufficient.

### 4.2 Escrow language: acceptable vs. unacceptable (PATTERN, high confidence)

**Acceptable language pattern** (synthesized from FAM-aligned practitioner descriptions, retrieved 2026-04-28):

> "Funds shall be released from escrow to Seller upon issuance of an E-2 visa to Buyer **OR** upon Buyer's admission to the United States in E-2 status, whichever first occurs. Funds shall be returned to Buyer **only if** an E-2 visa application filed within `<<X>>` days of this Agreement is denied by the U.S. Department of State, after exhaustion of any motion to reconsider. No other event triggers a refund."

**Unacceptable / risky** (recent rejection pattern):

- "Buyer may rescind for any reason."
- "Refund upon failure to close for any cause."
- "Termination at Buyer's sole discretion."

(PATTERN, medium-high confidence; flagged by U.S. Immigration Plan and several escrow-focused practitioner blogs in 2025-26.)

### 4.3 The "spent > escrowed > LOI" defensive paragraph

Drafter snippet to add to the manual when not all funds are spent:

> "Of the total committed investment of `$<<Total>>`, `$<<Spent>>` has been irrevocably deployed and is documented by paid invoices, executed lease, and equipment delivery (Exhibit: …). The balance of `$<<Escrowed>>` is held in a written escrow whose release is restricted to issuance of an E-2 visa or admission of the Beneficiary in E-2 status, with refund permitted only upon denial of the visa application — i.e., the limited contingency expressly contemplated by 9 FAM 402.9-6(B). No portion of the funds may be withdrawn for any other reason."

## 5. Source-of-Funds Memorandum for E-2 NEW

### 5.1 What the SOF memo looks like (PATTERN, high confidence)

The renewal manual already covers SOF with rigor (Tab E reconstruction). For E-2 NEW, the same discipline applies, **plus** these additions specific to no-prior-history:

1. **Chronological narrative.** A 1-3 page memo, written for a non-financial reader (the consular officer), narrating: "Beneficiary accumulated funds from `<<source 1>>` between `<<dates>>`; then `<<source 2>>`; consolidated into account `<<>>` on `<<date>>`; converted to USD on `<<date>>`; wired to U.S. on `<<date>>`; deployed to Petitioner on `<<date>>`."
2. **Exhibit-citation discipline.** Every dollar movement in the narrative ends with `(Exhibit `<<E.X.x>>`)`.
3. **Tax-return support.** For income-source claims, attach the home-country tax returns covering the accumulation years. Where tax returns are not standard (some treaty countries), attach a CPA certification letter explaining the local norm.
4. **Lawful-origin certification.** A signed Beneficiary affidavit attesting to lawful origin with subjects to penalty of perjury under 28 USC 1746.

### 5.2 Tier scaling (PATTERN, medium confidence)

Practitioner consensus on scrutiny tiers (Colombo Hurd, Scott Legal, U.S. Immigration Plan, Alaz Law, retrieved 2026-04-28):

| Tier | Investment | Expected SOF rigor | Expected substantiality posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | ≤ $100,000 | Heightened — every dollar traced; smaller accumulation pool means less room for unexplained gaps | Argue qualitatively (Walsh & Pollard) — match enterprise need; service-only businesses under ~$80K flagged as thin |
| Mid | $100,000 – $500,000 | Standard — full chain; ≥75–90% expected as a percentage of enterprise cost | Standard inverted-sliding-scale narrative |
| High | $500,000+ | Standard rigor on origin, but proportionality threshold drops (large absolute dollar) | Substantiality argued primarily in absolute terms |

This is a 2025-26 practitioner taxonomy. The FAM does not codify these tiers; they are PATTERN.

### 5.3 Cryptocurrency as source — 2025-26 consular requirements (FACT/PATTERN mix, high confidence on documentation; medium on outcomes)

From Tondini, Oguz, Alaz Law, e2visalawyer.net (all retrieved 2026-04-28):

Required documentary chain:

1. **Initial fiat purchase records** — bank statement showing USD/EUR/TRY → exchange.
2. **Exchange KYC pack** — onboarding KYC records (ID verification, address, source-of-funds questionnaire submitted to the exchange).
3. **Full transaction logs** — every trade, every hop. Exchange-issued CSV / API export.
4. **Wallet-to-wallet blockchain IDs** — block explorer screenshots tying transactions to the named addresses.
5. **Liquidation records** — exchange records of crypto → fiat conversion immediately preceding deployment.
6. **U.S. bank-side ingestion records** — wire from exchange / off-ramp → applicant's U.S. bank → Petitioner.
7. **Tax compliance** — home-country and (if applicable) U.S. tax return showing crypto reporting.

> "If this exhaustive ledger cannot be provided, the application will likely be denied." (PATTERN, multiple sources retrieved 2026-04-28.)

**Drafter snippet (firm voice, to be added):**
> "The Beneficiary's investment funds derive in part from lawful gains realized through the trading of digital assets on the regulated exchange `<<Exchange>>`, which performs Know-Your-Customer verification on all account holders. The complete trade ledger, the corresponding blockchain transaction identifiers, and the final fiat-conversion records are enclosed (Exhibits `<<E.x.x>>` through `<<E.x.y>>`). Each transaction has been reported on the Beneficiary's `<<year>>` `<<jurisdiction>>` tax return."

## 6. Franchise E-2 NEW Scenario

### 6.1 FDD Item 7 vs. actual deployed (PATTERN, high confidence)

**Item 7 of the FDD** is the franchisor's estimate of total initial investment cost. Item 7 is a **range** (often wide). Practitioner discipline (Pollak PLLC, Joorney, Internicola, Franchisee Lawyer, all retrieved 2026-04-28):

- The E-2 file's deployed-funds total must **align with the high end of Item 7** for a same-territory unit, **or** the cover letter must explain the deviation (e.g., "Petitioner has elected the higher-amenities buildout").
- A deployed amount **below** the FDD low end is a substantiality red flag.
- An LOI for a franchise unit, without paid franchise fee + paid build-out draws, is the weakest possible posture.

### 6.2 The "stock business plan" rejection pattern (PATTERN, medium-high confidence)

Sources do not use the literal phrase "stock business plan rejection" as a doctrinal term, but the rejection pattern is consistently described:

- A plan that reproduces verbatim the franchisor's marketing deck or the FDD Item 19 numbers without locale-specific adjustment is criticized as failing *Ho*'s "credible" prong.
- The defense is to commission an independent business plan (Joorney / Visa Business Plans / Wise Business Plans / similar) that **uses** the FDD Item 19 numbers as one input but layers in: locale demographics, drive-time analysis, named local competitors, locale-specific labor rates from BLS QCEW, and a CAC model.

(PATTERN, medium-high confidence; converges across Joorney, Pollak PLLC, Internicola, Visa Business Plans, retrieved 2026-04-28. No primary BIA/AAO precedent retrieved using the specific phrase; rejection is described at the practitioner / RFE level.)

### 6.3 Anti-restrictive-franchise paragraph — the "develop and direct" defense

The threat: a franchise agreement specifying centralized marketing, mandated suppliers, mandatory territories, fixed pricing, fixed signage — all of which can be argued by USCIS as defeating develop-and-direct.

The defense (PATTERN, high confidence; Pollak, Joorney, Internicola, Franchiselawsolutions.com, retrieved 2026-04-28):

> Cover-letter paragraph (drafter snippet, to be added):
> "While the Petitioner operates as a franchisee under the Franchise Agreement enclosed herewith (Exhibit: …), the Beneficiary retains and exercises full executive control over those functions that constitute developing and directing the enterprise within the meaning of 9 FAM 402.9-7. Specifically, the Beneficiary determines and controls: local hiring and termination decisions; staff scheduling and supervision; local marketing budget allocation and execution; vendor and supplier relationships within the franchisor's approved-vendor framework; pricing within the franchisor's permitted bands; financial management and reinvestment decisions; and strategic local growth, including additional unit acquisition. The franchisor's brand-standards requirements pertain to product specifications, signage, and quality control — they do not divest the Beneficiary of executive authority over the enterprise's operations, finances, or workforce."

(PATTERN, high confidence on the structure; the specific phrasing is firm-voice synthesis, not a quoted authority.)

## 7. The CBI (Citizenship-by-Investment) E-2 NEW Scenario

### 7.1 The 2023 NDAA / AMIGOS Act — the three-year domicile rule (FACT, high confidence)

**Citation.** National Defense Authorization Act for FY 2023, P.L. 117-263 (signed Dec 23, 2022); the AMIGOS Act provisions (`congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/2571`; `congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/senate-bill/1194`) (retrieved 2026-04-28).

**Rule (FACT, high confidence; corroborated across EB5Investors, EB5 United, IMI Daily, Global Immigration Blog, U.S. Immigration Advisor, all retrieved 2026-04-28):**

- An E-visa applicant who acquired the treaty-country nationality **by financial investment** must have been **domiciled in that treaty country for a continuous period of not less than three years** at any point before applying for an E-visa.
- **Carve-outs.** Persons previously granted E-visa status are exempt. Persons who acquired the treaty nationality by birth, marriage, or non-investment-based residency are not subject to the rule.
- The rule was specifically designed to limit Grenada / Turkey CBI → E-2 fast-tracks.

### 7.2 "Bona fide nationality" — what the file must show

The file must establish, on top of the citizenship certificate and treaty-country passport:

1. **Three-year continuous domicile** (where the AMIGOS Act applies) — utility bills, lease/title in the treaty country, school enrollment for children, tax residence proof, employer payroll if applicable, immigration entry/exit stamps showing physical presence pattern.
2. **Time held since naturalization** — the longer the gap between naturalization and E-2 application, the better.
3. **Business activities since acquisition** — evidence the applicant has actually engaged with the treaty country (bank account history, business registrations, real-estate ownership).
4. **Treaty-country tax filings** — even if minimal, demonstrate domicile rather than nominal status.

(PATTERN, high confidence; converges across U.S. Immigration Advisor, Klasko, EB5Investors, Global Immigration Blog, retrieved 2026-04-28.)

### 7.3 2025-26 DOS scrutiny patterns (PATTERN, medium confidence)

- **Turkey.** "Speed and accessibility of Turkey's system have introduced complexity in verification, leading to occasional U.S. consular scrutiny of citizenship documents and source-of-funds evidence." (U.S. Immigration Advisor, retrieved 2026-04-28.) Long processing; more restrictive adjudications.
- **Grenada.** Caribbean-region consular officers familiar with Grenada CBI documentation; relatively higher consistency, but still subject to AMIGOS three-year rule for first-time E-2.
- **General CBI.** Inconsistencies in financial history "raise questions about the bona fide nature of both the citizenship acquisition and the subsequent business investment." (U.S. Immigration Advisor, retrieved 2026-04-28.)

> Drafter snippet (firm voice, to be added):
> "The Beneficiary acquired `<<Treaty Country>>` nationality on `<<Date>>` and has been continuously domiciled in `<<Treaty Country>>` for `<<>>` years thereafter, satisfying the three-year continuous domicile requirement enacted by the AMIGOS Act provisions of P.L. 117-263, Section `<<>>`. Domicile is evidenced by the Beneficiary's residential lease of `<<>>` years' duration, utility records, `<<Treaty Country>>` tax residency certifications, and `<<>>` years of treaty-country business activity. (Exhibits: …)"

## 8. E-2 NEW Checklist — By Element

This section maps directly to the manual's renewal-calibrated structure. Each element flags what differs from a renewal.

### Element 1 — Treaty Country Nationality

**Same as renewal:** passport, ownership instruments, 50%+ treaty-country combined ownership.

**E-2 NEW differences:**
- If acquired through CBI: AMIGOS three-year domicile pack (§7).
- No prior E-2 visa to cite — Tab C.1 (prior visa) and C.2 (prior payroll) are absent or replaced with treaty-country residence pack.

### Element 2 — Substantial Investment + At-Risk

**Same as renewal:** Membership Interest Transfer (or stock subscription) Agreement with consideration, currency conversion, international wire, deployment to Petitioner.

**E-2 NEW differences (CRITICAL):**
- **Spent-vs-escrowed disclosure.** A subsection of cover-letter Section IV that explicitly classifies each dollar as spent / escrowed-with-denial-only-clawback / escrowed-other / LOI.
- **Build-out evidence package.** Lease + paid security deposit + contractor agreement + paid progress draws + equipment POs + paid equipment invoices.
- **Substantiality narrative leans on Walsh & Pollard.** Quote the "amount normally considered necessary to establish a viable enterprise of the nature contemplated" standard, plus an industry-cost benchmark (IBISWorld report or FDD Item 7).

### Element 3 — Real & Operating

**This is the hardest element at filing time for an E-2 NEW** because the enterprise is, by definition, not yet selling.

The file must shift from "operating proof" to "**operationally ready** proof":

- EIN letter.
- State + local registrations / licensing.
- Lease + premises photos with date stamps.
- Insurance binder.
- Merchant account onboarding.
- Vendor / supplier contracts (executed).
- Hiring file (offer letters, payroll provider).
- Hours-of-operation plan, staffing schedule for opening week.

Pair with cover-letter language: "**immediately capable of commencing operations** upon Beneficiary's admission" + cite to 9 FAM 402.9-6(D) + Walsh & Pollard.

### Element 4 — More Than Marginal

**This is the second-hardest element at filing time** because tax returns and W-2 history don't yet exist.

Substitutes:

- **5-year business plan** (Matter-of-Ho-compliant, §3) projecting headcount and revenue.
- **W-2 hiring timetable** with 12-18 month first hire.
- **Job description schedule** for every projected hire.
- **Industry benchmark footnoting** showing the projected scale is realistic.
- **Capital-deployment table** showing reinvestment runway.
- **Beneficiary CV** — long industry track record substitutes for absent operating history.

### Element 5 — Develop and Direct

**Mostly same as renewal**, with two E-2 NEW additions:

- **Anti-restrictive-franchise paragraph** (§6.3) if the vehicle is a franchise.
- **Pre-operational executive-control evidence**: emails / memos showing the Beneficiary already directing pre-launch decisions (vendor selection, contractor negotiation, lease signing as Member-Manager, hiring committee chair).
