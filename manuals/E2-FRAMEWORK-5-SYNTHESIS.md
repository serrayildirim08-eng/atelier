## 1. Cross-axis agreement (the firm position)

Where all four lenses converge, the firm adopts the unified position
without further deliberation. These are the load-bearing
recommendations of the entire framework.

### 1.1 The five elements have an asymmetric burden

All four parts agree: E1 (nationality + ≥ 50 % treaty ownership) and
E5 (develop-and-direct or qualifying employee) are **threshold**
elements — they either are or are not satisfied, and a single
documentary defect collapses them. E2 (substantial investment, at-risk),
E3 (real and operating), and E4 (more than marginal) are
**probabilistic** elements — they are evaluated on the totality of
evidence, and a strong showing on adjacent elements compensates for
modest weakness on one. Suárez-Lopez locates the asymmetry in the
statutory text (E1 and E5 are textually fixed; E2/E3/E4 turn on
discretionary terms — "substantial," "bona fide," "more than marginal").
Yılmaz's empirical data confirms that AAO denials cluster on E2 (32 %)
and E4 (19 %), with E1 (8 %) and E5 (11 %) trailing. The practical
consequence (Whitfield) and the defensive-framing consequence (Demir)
follow: spend documentary effort proportional to the empirical denial
distribution.

### 1.2 The packet is read in adjudicator-time, not regulatory-time

All four parts agree that an officer reads the file in 20–40 minutes,
not 4 hours. Suárez-Lopez frames this as an interpretive constraint on
how much regulatory text the cover letter can usefully cite. Yılmaz's
empirical study shows that overlong cover letters (>30 pages) correlate
with elevated RFE issuance — the officer asks for what the cover letter
buried. Whitfield prescribes a 12–18 page cover-letter target with
heavy use of headers and a one-page substantiality table. Demir's
defensive paragraphs are explicitly written for inline placement
**before** the unfamiliar exhibit, so the officer's confusion is
resolved at the point of confusion.

### 1.3 Sub-type calibration is the single largest source of preventable error

All four parts treat sub-type misclassification as the dominant
upstream failure mode. Suárez-Lopez's regulatory anchors (§§ 9.1–9.4
of Part 1) are mutually exclusive: a case is classified into exactly
one sub-type, and the cover-letter authority allowlist follows. Yılmaz's
denial data shows ~14 % of denials trace to a sub-type-misalignment
defect (the cover letter argues Subtype 1 elements while the facts
support Subtype 4, or vice versa). Whitfield's intake protocol (Part 3
§ 1) front-loads the sub-type determination as Q26–Q30. Demir's
misclassification-hazard catalog (Part 4 § 2) supplies the diagnostic
tests for the four most common boundary errors.

### 1.4 The defensive-paragraph discipline

All four parts agree: when a non-US legal form (Tapu, Esas Sözleşme,
Bordro, Yönetim Kurulu Kararı, Sicil) appears in the file, a defensive
paragraph in the cover letter is **mandatory, not discretionary**.
Suárez-Lopez recognizes this as a comity / interpretive humility move —
the agency adjudicator is not presumed to know foreign law, and the
practitioner closes the gap. Yılmaz's denial data shows that filings
with foreign-jurisdiction documents and no defensive framing receive
RFEs at roughly 2× the base rate. Whitfield places the defensive
paragraph immediately before the exhibit citation. Demir owns the
catalog (Part 4 § 1).

### 1.5 The substantiality math is reconciled, not asserted

All four parts agree: the substantiality argument is not "$X is
substantial." It is "$X investment / $Y total cost of enterprise =
Z%, and Z% lies inside the inverted-sliding-scale range for an
enterprise of cost $Y, supported by [bank confirmations + invoices
totaling $W with $|W − X| < 15 %]." The investment table (Preparation
Manual Part IX; Practical Part 3 § 8) is the unifying artifact. The
firm's bot computes the reconciliation deterministically (manual
§ 4.5 and § F gates).

### 1.6 At-risk doctrine is checked before the file leaves the firm

All four parts agree: 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) and *Matter of Hira* together
foreclose any investment where the funds were borrowed against
**enterprise** assets. Suárez-Lopez articulates the textual rule.
Yılmaz catalogs the AAO denial pattern (loan secured by the LLC's
own equipment / lease / inventory — denied). Whitfield prescribes
the intake question (Q15: "is the investment a loan, and if so, what
is the security?"). Demir handles the gift-vs-loan ambiguity in
Part 4 § 4.

## 2. Cross-axis disagreement (Supervisor decision points)

Two genuine disagreements among the four attorneys did not resolve
during cross-review. They are presented to the Supervisor for
adoption.

### 2.1 The 12-month bank-statement window vs the 24-month window

**Whitfield (practitioner, dominant view).** File 12 months of
business bank statements. More creates an officer-time burden and
correlates with marginal-content RFEs (the officer asks why month 17
shows reduced activity).

**Yılmaz (case-law, minority view).** Service-center priors at NSC
and CSC trend toward asking for 24 months on cases with substantiality
arguments above $300K. A 12-month default exposes the file to an
"insufficient operating history" RFE about 9 % of the time; a 24-month
default eliminates that RFE category.

**Suárez-Lopez (regulatory, neutral).** Neither 12 nor 24 is regulatorily
required; it is a discretionary completeness judgment.

**Demir (edge cases, minority-aligned).** For Subtype 4 cases with
parent-funded structures and US-subsidiary operating history < 24
months, the 24-month window of the foreign parent must accompany.

**Recommended Supervisor posture.** Default to **12 months of bank
statements** with the following triggers for **24 months**: (a)
substantiality argument > $300K, (b) Subtype 4 with US sub < 24
months operating, (c) any prior renewal where the variance analysis
requires earlier baseline.

### 2.2 Premium-processing default for renewals

**Whitfield.** Default to **standard processing** for renewals,
because the I-94 admit-until typically gives a 240-day post-expiration
work-authorization grace period and PP costs $1,685 for marginal
schedule benefit.

**Yılmaz.** For renewals with **any** RFE risk indicator (substantiality
under-documented, marginality thin, prior cycle had an RFE), elect PP
because the 15-day clock forces the adjudicator to engage the file
quickly while supporting evidence is fresh.

**Suárez-Lopez (neutral).** PP is statutory at 8 USC § 1356(u); the
choice is purely strategic.

**Demir.** PP is contraindicated where the file has a Demir-graded
edge-case score ≥ 3 (gift-vs-loan ambiguity, layered ownership, or
foreign-document hygiene gaps), because the 15-day clock pauses
during RFE response and the firm needs the post-RFE drafting window
on substantive deliberation, not on rush-clock pressure.

**Recommended Supervisor posture.** Default to **standard processing**.
Elect PP only when (a) the Beneficiary's prior status expires within
60 days of filing AND no risk indicators are present, OR (b) the
client expressly requests it after disclosure of cost and pause-clock
mechanics.

## 3. The 50-point complete-file diagnostic

Triangulated from all four lenses. Any attorney in the firm should be
able to walk a case folder against this list in 20 minutes and predict
approval probability within ± 10 %. A "no" on any starred (★) item
means do not file.

### 3.1 E1 — Nationality + ownership (10 points)

- [ ] ★ Beneficiary passport (bio page) on file, treaty country confirmed against current DOS treaty list, expiry > 6 months from filing
- [ ] ★ Combined treaty-national ownership of Petitioner ≥ 50 % (sum from Operating Agreement amendment / shareholder register)
- [ ] All co-owner nationality proofs on file (passport bio page or EAD card) where their nationality contributes to the ≥ 50 % threshold
- [ ] Ownership math reconciled across Articles, Operating Agreement, MITA, and Cap Table — entity-coherence gate clean
- [ ] If Subtype 2, foreign parent shareholder register shows ≥ 50 % treaty-national ownership of the parent (per Subtype 2 anchor at Part 1 § 9.2)
- [ ] Beneficiary nationality matches the qualifying treaty country of the enterprise (no cross-country mismatch)
- [ ] No dual-nationality concealment on DS-160 (if consular)
- [ ] Member resolution effective date precedes or coincides with filing date
- [ ] If Subtype 4 employee, Beneficiary nationality matches qualifying treaty country of the petitioning enterprise
- [ ] I-94 admit-until > filing date (no status violation at filing) — § 3.4 gate clean

### 3.2 E2 — Investment + at-risk + SOF (12 points)

- [ ] ★ MITA total consideration / I-129E investment amount drift < $100 — § 4.5 gate clean
- [ ] Investment counted only on includable categories per Preparation Manual Part VIII § 8.2
- [ ] At-risk doctrine satisfied — investment is **not** loaned against enterprise assets
- [ ] Five-link SOF chain documented end-to-end with named numbers (origin → conversion → wire → US → deployment)
- [ ] FX conversion math reconciled — § 5.2.1 gate clean (within 1 %)
- [ ] All foreign-jurisdiction SOF documents accompanied by certified translation (8 CFR § 103.2(b)(3))
- [ ] Tapu / TÜFE / Bordro / Esas Sözleşme defensive paragraphs inserted in cover letter Section IV where triggered
- [ ] Substantiality coverage ratio between 0.85 and 1.15 — § F gate clean
- [ ] Substantiality proportionality (investment / total cost of enterprise) within the inverted-sliding-scale band for the enterprise's cost level
- [ ] If Subtype 2/3/4: parent → subsidiary wire confirmation on file; board resolution authorized amount drift < 10 % vs I-129E — § 6 gate clean
- [ ] Loan-vs-gift ambiguity resolved (per Part 4 § 4) and documented
- [ ] Investment table populated as filed exhibit

### 3.3 E3 — Real and operating (10 points)

- [ ] ★ Commercial lease in the Petitioner's legal name, term ≥ requested visa validity
- [ ] Premises photographs (interior + exterior + signage) in Tab G
- [ ] ≥ 12 months business bank statements (24 months if § 2.1 trigger)
- [ ] Vendor invoices + customer transactions evidencing ongoing commerce
- [ ] Premises licenses (food, alcohol, retail, professional, as applicable)
- [ ] Workers' compensation insurance certificate
- [ ] General liability insurance certificate
- [ ] Utility bills (most recent month)
- [ ] Operating frequency (cadence of bank inflows / outflows) consistent with claimed business model
- [ ] No evidence of speculative or holding-only posture (ruling out 9 FAM 402.9-6(D) bona-fides risk)

### 3.4 E4 — More than marginal (8 points)

- [ ] ★ ≥ 1 W-2 employee (excluding Beneficiary) at filing — Walsh & Pollard satisfied prima facie
- [ ] Payroll register for trailing 4 quarters
- [ ] Form 941 for trailing 4 quarters
- [ ] State unemployment insurance returns (where applicable)
- [ ] Form 940 (FUTA) most recent year
- [ ] I-9 + W-4 on file for each W-2 employee
- [ ] Hiring schedule in business plan projects continued W-2 growth Years 1–5
- [ ] If 1099 contractors used, properly documented (1099-NEC, IC agreements) — but not a substitute for W-2 baseline

### 3.5 E5 — Develop and direct (or qualifying employee) (5 points)

- [ ] ★ Member resolution appointing Beneficiary to a role with **enumerated operational authority** (not bare title)
- [ ] If Subtype 1: ownership ≥ 50 % OR governance control proven via OA / board structure
- [ ] If Subtype 3: CV documents prior P&L responsibility, signing authority, supervised teams; comp at executive industry/geography benchmarks
- [ ] If Subtype 4: diploma + manufacturer/industry certifications + service record showing salary differential vs domestic peer median; specialized knowledge **not** reducible to bilingualism
- [ ] Org chart on file showing reporting structure

### 3.6 Procedural + dependents (5 points)

- [ ] G-28 signed by attorney AND each applicant, dated within 30 days of filing
- [ ] G-1145 attached to front of packet (USCIS routes)
- [ ] G-1650 ACH authorizations covering all fee categories with correct totals
- [ ] If dependents: I-539 (spouse) + I-539A (each child) with marriage / birth certs in Tab L; certified translations attached
- [ ] If consular: DS-160 confirmation + DS-156E completed for each applicant; appointment scheduled

**Approval-probability heuristic.** All ★ items satisfied + ≥ 42 of 50
non-starred → high probability. ≥ 38 / 50 → medium with curable gaps.
< 38 / 50 → escalate to attorney before filing.

## 4. Sub-type synthesis

The four lenses apply differently across the four sub-types. The
following matrix consolidates the per-axis adjustments.

| Axis | Subtype 1 | Subtype 2 | Subtype 3 | Subtype 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulatory anchor** | 9 FAM 402.9-7(1) + 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(2) | 9 FAM 402.9-4(B) + 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(3) | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(a) + 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(17) | 9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) + 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(18) |
| **Dominant denial pattern (per Yılmaz)** | E2 substantiality + E4 marginality (35 % combined) | E1 ownership chain disclosure (28 %) | E5 executive-vs-functional (22 %) | E5 specialized-knowledge sufficiency (31 %) |
| **Practical packet emphasis (Whitfield)** | SOF chain visibility + W-2 marginality + defensive paragraphs | Foreign parent audited financials currency + board-resolution amount-match | Executive role enumeration + comp benchmarking + prior P&L docs | Specialized-knowledge demo + salary-differential service record + offer-letter sequencing |
| **Edge-case dominant risk (Demir)** | Tapu defensive + 49 % ownership boundary + gift-vs-loan | Foreign parent recency + multi-tier holding chains | Title-vs-function gap + comp-below-market | Bilingualism-only collapse + foreign-credential evaluation absence |
| **Recommended PP posture (per § 2.2)** | Standard | Standard | Standard | Standard, escalate only if status-cliff |
| **Bank-statement window (per § 2.1)** | 12 months | 24 months (parent + sub) | 12 months sub + 24 months parent if claimed | 24 months sub + 24 months parent |
| **Cover letter section weighting** | Heavy on IV (SOF) and VI (marginality) | Heavy on III (ownership) and V (substantiality) | Heavy on VII (develop-and-direct) and III (ownership) | Heavy on VII (specialized employee) and II (treaty country) |

## 5. Open questions registry

The following questions are surfaced to the Supervisor for decision.
None of them blocks publication of the framework module; they are
items the team flags for ongoing development.

1. **Amici / dissenting BIA postures.** Yılmaz notes that *Matter of Ho*
   (1988) is asymmetrically applied — agency cites it for denial but
   rarely concedes that a "credible" plan satisfies it. Should the
   firm draft a model amicus brief preserving the affirmative reading
   for cases that go to federal appeal?
2. **Loper Bright recalibration.** Suárez-Lopez (Part 1 § 7) flags that
   post-2024 deference shifts may open daylight between FAM
   constructions and the underlying statute on E2 substantial-amount
   and E5 develop-and-direct. Should the firm develop a position paper?
3. **NDAA 2023 residency requirement.** The 2022 statutory tightening
   (Inhofe NDAA § 5502) requires a specified minimum residency in the
   treaty country for some applicants. The firm has not yet had a
   case turn on this; should we issue an advisory to clients with
   recent naturalization patterns?
4. **Sub-type 2 vs Sub-type 3 boundary cases.** Demir (Part 4 § 2)
   and Whitfield (Part 3 § 11) both flag the "is the human qualifying
   via ownership or via role?" boundary. The firm's bot Phase 0.6
   classifier currently treats them as mutually exclusive. Should we
   develop a hybrid analytical posture for borderline files?
5. **Premium-processing pause-clock dynamics.** The 15-day clock pauses
   during RFE response. Demir's edge-case-score ≥ 3 contraindication
   (§ 2.2) should be tested empirically; the firm has not yet
   validated the threshold.
6. **AAO non-precedent dataset publication.** Yılmaz maintains the
   internal dataset; the firm could publish a redacted analytical
   summary as a thought-leadership piece, with privacy-review
   discipline. Strategic question for the Supervisor.
