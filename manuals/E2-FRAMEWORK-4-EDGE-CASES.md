## 0. How this Part is meant to be used

The sibling Parts describe the modal E-2 case (regulation, adjudicator
priors, practitioner playbook). This Part addresses the deviations.

Three operating principles govern the catalog below.

**First — pre-empt the adjudicator's confusion.** Every drop-in paragraph
in § 1 is written for inline citation in the cover-letter Section under
which the unfamiliar exhibit is offered. Native diacritics ("Tapu
Müdürlüğü", "Esas Sözleşme", "Yönetim Kurulu Kararı") are preserved so
that adjudicators who Google the term reach the same authority the
drafter relied on; ASCII transliterations degrade googleability.

**Second — defensive paragraphs are not optional once triggered.** A
Turkish title deed in the file means the Tapu paragraph in Section IV.
A TÜFE-indexed lease means the TÜFE paragraph. The defensive paragraph
is the firm's standing answer to a foreseeable RFE; it costs nothing in
the initial filing and eliminates a category of post-filing risk.

**Third — edge cases compound.** A 49 % ownership case plus a
gift-versus-loan SOF ambiguity plus a Subtype 1 / Subtype 4 boundary
question is one large problem, not three small ones; rebuild on every
axis or refer out. § 7 records the compounding-risk thresholds.

## 1. Defensive-paragraph catalog

Each paragraph below is drop-in ready. Insert verbatim into the cover
letter at the cited Section, immediately before the relevant exhibit
citation. Adjust only the bracketed placeholders.

### 1.1 Tapu (Turkish title-deed) explanation
> Under Turkish law and customary practice, real-property transfers are
> effected directly through the Tapu Müdürlüğü (Land Registry
> Directorate of the Republic of Türkiye), and a separate written sales
> contract is not issued in standard title-deed transfers. The Tapu
> registry record is itself the conveyance instrument: it is signed by
> both transferor and transferee in the presence of a registry officer,
> records the parcel identifier, the consideration declared, and the
> effective transfer date, and is dispositive as a matter of Turkish
> property law. Accordingly, the lawful sale of the Beneficiary's real
> property at [address] is evidenced through the certified Tapu
> records produced at Exhibit [E-x], and no separate sales contract
> exists or is required. (Exhibit: certified copy of Tapu, dated
> YYYY-MM-DD, parcel ID [pafta/ada/parsel].)

### 1.2 TÜFE / TEFE rent-escalation explanation
> Under Turkish landlord-tenant law (Türk Borçlar Kanunu Article 344),
> residential lease rents are indexed to the Tüketici Fiyat Endeksi
> (TÜFE — Consumer Price Index), and commercial lease rents are
> indexed to the Üretici Fiyat Endeksi (ÜFE) or, by agreement, to the
> superseded Toptan Eşya Fiyat Endeksi (TEFE — Wholesale Price Index)
> for legacy contracts. The annual rent escalation of [percentage]
> reflected in the rental receipts at Exhibit [E-x] is therefore not
> an unexplained increase but a statutorily framed adjustment indexed
> to the Türkiye İstatistik Kurumu (TÜİK) twelve-month moving average,
> capped where a residential lease is involved. The Beneficiary's
> rental income chain across the [N]-year period accordingly reflects
> a lawful, regulator-published index, not discretionary repricing.

## 2. Sub-type misclassification hazards

The Phase-0 detector is reliable on the modal case; it is fragile at the
boundaries. The four hazards below are the firm's running list of the
fact patterns where the file appears to fit one subtype but, on careful
reading, belongs to another. Each hazard carries a diagnostic test.

### 2.1 Looks Subtype 1 but is Subtype 4 — the salary-differential test

**Pattern.** A Beneficiary holds founder shares in a small US LLC, holds
a foreign passport from a treaty country, and was the natural-person
investor of record. On its face this looks like Subtype 1. The
salary-differential test asks: **does the Beneficiary actually develop
and direct, or is the Beneficiary functionally an essential-skills
employee whose ownership stake is incidental to their compensation
package?** If the Beneficiary's ownership stake is below 25 %, was
acquired in lieu of cash compensation, and the actual managerial
authority sits with a different majority owner, the file is Subtype 4
dressed as Subtype 1 and will fail develop-and-direct review.

**Diagnostic.** Compare three salary points: (i) the Beneficiary's
proposed US compensation, (ii) the Beneficiary's foreign-job salary in
the same role, and (iii) the prevailing US wage for the role at the
relevant geography. Where (i) > (ii) by a multiple consistent with
specialized-skills premium, and (iii) is materially below (i), the file
is Subtype 4 and must be rebuilt under the Subtype 4 cover-letter voice.

### 2.2 Looks Subtype 2 but the human qualifies via Subtype 3 — the role-not-ownership test

**Pattern.** Parent-subsidiary structure: a Turkish A.Ş. owns the US
LLC. The drafter reaches for Subtype 2 because of the corporate
ownership chain. **But Subtype 2 describes the petitioning enterprise's
ownership; the human Beneficiary's classification is independent.** If
the human is a senior executive of the parent or the subsidiary, with
P&L authority and span of control — but is not the natural-person
investor — the human is Subtype 3, not Subtype 2.

**Diagnostic.** Ask: who is the I-129E "Investor"? If the answer is the
corporate parent, the petitioner-side classification is Subtype 2 (or
treaty-enterprise-qualifying for an employee filing); the human
Beneficiary is Subtype 3 (executive/supervisory) or Subtype 4
(essential skills). Subtype 1 only applies when the natural-person
Beneficiary is themself the named investor. The Imm-464-2023 RFE in
the firm archive is the standing reminder of this hazard.

### 2.3 Subtype 4 collapses to bilingualism-only — the specialized-knowledge red line

**Pattern.** The drafter argues essential skills under 8 CFR § 214.2(e)
(18) but, on the cover letter's plain reading, the only specialization
is that the Beneficiary speaks Turkish (or the parent's home language)
and English. **9 FAM 402.9-7(2)(b) is explicit that foreign language
and culture alone do not constitute specialized knowledge.** A file
that argues only bilingualism + cultural fluency will draw an RFE that
the firm cannot cure on the existing record.

**Diagnostic.** Strike every reference to language, culture, and
"understanding the parent's home market" from the cover letter. If
what remains still articulates the specialized skill, the file is
sound. If the remaining text is thin, return to intake and obtain
proof of process-specific or technology-specific expertise — supplier
relationships, proprietary product training, certification stacks not
available in US labor markets.

### 2.4 Subtype 1 ownership is borderline

**Pattern.** The Beneficiary holds 49 % (fail), exactly 50 % (qualifies
but bulletproof proof required), 50 % economically with 60 % voting via
class-A/class-B share split, or 50 % nominally with proxy authority over
another's stake. See § 3 below for the resolution matrix.

## 3. Borderline ownership scenarios

| Scenario | Threshold | Firm position | Defensive framing required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 49 % treaty-national + 51 % non-treaty | < 50 % treaty | **Fail** under 9 FAM 402.9-4(B); do not file as Subtype 1 | Re-architect ownership before filing, or re-classify as Subtype 3/4 employee filing if the Beneficiary qualifies on role |
| 50 % exactly | At threshold | **Qualifies** but proof must be bulletproof — Operating Agreement, Membership Interest Transfer Agreement, share-ledger printout, capital-account schedule, all consistent | Defensive paragraph reciting that the Beneficiary "owns fifty per cent (50 %) of the outstanding membership interests" with cite to 9 FAM 402.9-4(B) and exhibit pin-cites |
| 51 %–99 % treaty (single) | Above threshold | Routine Subtype 1 | Standard ownership paragraph; no special framing |
| Loaned-up minority position — Beneficiary 30 %, two co-treaty-nationals 25 % each = 80 % combined treaty | Aggregate above threshold | **Qualifies** at the entity level, but the Beneficiary must independently establish develop-and-direct under 9 FAM 402.9-7(1) | § 1.8 paragraph adapted; plus a develop-and-direct paragraph showing the Beneficiary, not the co-owners, holds operational authority |
| Voting / economic split — 50 % economic but 60 % voting | Above threshold on voting; at threshold on economic | **Qualifies** under 9 FAM 402.9-4(B), which is read for "ownership and control" | Defensive paragraph distinguishing voting and economic interest, with cite to the Operating Agreement clauses creating each, and an explicit recital that the treaty-national side commands both majority voting and at least 50 % economic interest |
| Proxy or trust-held interests | Variable | Treat with caution: a proxy that can be revoked is not "ownership" — file only where the proxy is irrevocable, registered, and treaty-national-controlled | Long-form paragraph reciting proxy/trust mechanics, irrevocability clause, and cite to the trust deed or proxy instrument; recommend pre-filing supervisor review |
| Beneficiary holds dual nationality (one treaty, one not) | Treaty side controls | **Qualifies** if the Beneficiary asserts the treaty nationality on the I-129E and DS-156E; the FAM permits election | Cover-letter paragraph electing treaty nationality, with passport copies of both nationalities at exhibit, and cite to 9 FAM 402.9-4(A) |

**Subtype 1 exemplar (US LLC, Q1 2026 filing)** used the 50 %-exact
framing on cover-letter Section II with a Membership Interest Transfer
Agreement, an Operating Agreement amended on the same date, and a
capital-account ledger reconciled to the wire confirmation. The file
approved without RFE.

## 4. SOF chain edge cases

### 4.1 Cash inheritance from informal economies

**Pattern.** The Beneficiary inherited cash from a parent in a
jurisdiction with no probate court, no formal estate administration,
and no inheritance-tax filing. The funds sat in the family home or in
a non-interest-bearing personal account for several years before being
deployed.

**Resolution.** Build a five-document substitute file:
(i) the decedent's death certificate, certified-translated;
(ii) a notarized affidavit from the surviving family members reciting
the chain of inheritance, with cross-signatures from each putative
heir disclaiming interest in favour of the Beneficiary;
(iii) any tax filing the decedent did make (income tax returns, real
estate tax receipts, vehicle title) to evidence the decedent's lawful
income generation during their lifetime;
(iv) a Hizmet Dökümü (or local equivalent) for the decedent
documenting career history;
(v) a defensive paragraph in cover-letter Section IV-A explaining the
absence of probate as a feature of the legal regime, not as evidentiary
weakness.

### 4.2 Real-estate-rental income with TÜFE/TEFE escalation

**Pattern.** The Beneficiary's SOF includes [N] years of residential or
commercial rental income from a Turkish property, with annual rent
escalations following TÜFE or, for legacy commercial leases, TEFE.

**Resolution.** Insert § 1.2 defensive paragraph. Build the rental
schedule with three columns: contractual rent, TÜFE/TEFE-indexed rent,
and actually-received rent. Reconcile to bank deposits at Is Bankası /
Akbank / equivalent. Where the actually-received rent diverges from the
indexed rent, explain (e.g., voluntary forbearance during the 2023
post-quake hardship; partial payment month with subsequent catch-up).

### 4.3 Business-of-business proceeds (SPV-to-SPV chains)

**Pattern.** The Beneficiary owns a Turkish A.Ş. that owns a Turkish
Ltd. Şti. that owns a Cypriot or Maltese SPV that holds the cash
ultimately wired to the US LLC. Each layer was lawful at each step,
but the chain crosses three jurisdictions and two layers of
intra-group lending.

**Resolution.** Map the corporate chain on a single-page diagram with
each entity's full legal name, jurisdiction, formation date, and the
percentage owned at each layer. For each intra-group transfer, produce
the Yönetim Kurulu Kararı (or local equivalent) authorizing the
transfer, the wire confirmation, and the recipient bank receipt.
Insert § 1.5 defensive paragraph for the Yönetim Kurulu Kararı, plus
a cover-letter recital that "the funds at issue were earned by [top
SPV] in [year] from [revenue source], retained as accumulated
earnings, and on-distributed to [next SPV] pursuant to a lawfully
adopted board resolution dated [date]." A multi-tier chain without
this scaffolding will draw a substantial-investment / source-of-funds
RFE.

### 4.4 Bridging gap between unrelated foreign accounts

**Pattern.** Funds rest temporarily in a spouse's account, a parent's
account, or an attorney trust account before reaching the Beneficiary's
own account.

**Resolution.** Where the funds rested in the spouse's account, produce
a notarized intra-spousal acknowledgement clarifying that the funds
were and remained the Beneficiary's beneficial property; where the
funds rested in a parent's or sibling's account, produce a notarized
gift-or-loan instrument (see § 4.5) characterizing the transfer; where
the funds rested in an attorney trust account, produce the engagement
letter and the trust-account ledger. Insert a cover-letter paragraph
identifying every account in the chain by IBAN, bank, holder, and the
date range during which the funds resided there, so the adjudicator
can audit the chain end-to-end without leaving the cover letter.

### 4.5 Gift-vs-loan ambiguity

**Pattern.** A "loan" from a parent or sibling is undocumented, has no
repayment schedule, no interest rate, and no enforcement intent. Or a
"gift" is documented with a promissory note that the parties never
intended to enforce.

**Resolution.** **A loan that lacks repayment intent is a gift.** A
gift documented as a loan misstates the substance and creates an
at-risk problem (because if it is a real loan secured by the
enterprise, it fails 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(12)). The firm's rule: at
intake, ask the client what the parties actually intend. If the
intent is "this is family money, nobody expects it back," document as
a gift with a notarized gift deed. If the intent is "this will be
repaid on schedule," document as a loan with a real schedule, real
interest, and a separate (non-enterprise) collateral source. A
Subtype 1 filing with a non-enterprise-collateralized intra-family
loan is supportable; a filing with a sham "loan" that is actually a
gift will fail at-risk review.

## 5. Foreign-document hygiene

### 5.1 Certified translation under 8 CFR § 103.2(b)(3)

The regulation requires that any foreign-language document submitted
in support of a benefit request be accompanied by "a full English
language translation" plus a "certification by the translator … that
the translation is complete and accurate, and that the translator is
competent to translate from the foreign language into English."
**Three things the firm requires beyond the regulatory minimum:**
(i) the translator's full name, signature, and date — typed, not
just signed;
(ii) the translator's contact information sufficient to verify the
certification (email and phone);
(iii) the translator's brief credential statement (e.g., "ATA-certified
Turkish<>English translator since 2014" or "Sworn translator under the
Republic of Türkiye Notary Public Law").

The firm does **not** require notarization of the translator's
certification — the regulation does not require it — but does require
the cover letter to recite the certification standard so the
adjudicator does not second-guess it.

### 5.2 Apostille vs Hague-non-signatory countries

For Hague-Convention countries, an Apostille from the issuing
authority is the complete authentication. The United States is itself
a Convention party, and an Apostille on a foreign public document
satisfies the U.S. presumption of authenticity. Türkiye is a
Convention party (acceded 1985); Apostille from the Kaymakamlık or Vali
office is sufficient.

For non-Hague countries, the consular-legalization chain is required:
**foreign notary → foreign Ministry of Foreign Affairs → U.S. consular
post in the issuing country**. Each step affixes a stamp or seal. The
firm requires the full chain visible on the document; missing any
layer is grounds to reject the document at intake.

### 5.3 Notarization standards — common-law vs civil-law notary

**A civil-law notary is a quasi-judicial officer; a US notary is an
identity-verifier.** This distinction matters because adjudicators
trained in US practice may underweight a Turkish noter signature,
expecting the limited US-style attestation.

**Drop-in notation for Section IV when civil-law notarization
appears:** "The foregoing is sealed by the [city] Noterlik (Turkish
Notary), a civil-law notary office under the Turkish Notary Public
Law (Noterlik Kanunu, Law No. 1512). Unlike the U.S. notary public,
the Turkish noter is a quasi-judicial officer who attests not only
to identity but to the legal validity of the instrument and retains
the original in the noter's archive. The notarization at Exhibit
[E-x] therefore carries presumptive validity beyond identity
verification alone."

### 5.4 Translator credentialing — what to require

Acceptable: ATA-certified, sworn translator under foreign-jurisdiction
oath, in-house bilingual attorney signing under the firm's translation
quality control. Unacceptable: anonymous translation services,
machine-only translations, the client themself translating their own
documents (ethical bar — translator must be disinterested).

## 6. Renewal-only edge cases

### 6.1 Prior approval was thin

**Pattern.** The prior I-129E was approved on a record that the firm,
re-reading at renewal, would not have filed today. Marginal
substantiality, cosmetic business plan, develop-and-direct argued in
two paragraphs.

**Disclosure obligation.** The firm has no affirmative duty to confess
that the prior approval was thin, but does have a duty to file a
renewal that is independently sound. The renewal cover letter does
not relitigate the original approval; it documents the trailing
period and the present state of substantiality, marginality,
develop-and-direct. **A renewal that simply "tracks" a thin original
is itself thin and risks a denial that retroactively highlights the
original's weakness.** Build the renewal as if it were an initial
filing, with the prior approval cited only as evidence of continuous
status.

### 6.2 Business-plan actuals materially diverged from projections

**Pattern.** The original I-129E projected $400K Year-3 revenue; actuals
came in at $180K. Or projections projected break-even at Month 18;
actual break-even is Month 30 and slipping.

**Resolution.** Insert a "Variance Section" in cover-letter Section VI
(Marginality). Reconcile the original projections to actuals line by
line. Explain each material variance with a documented reason (supply
chain disruption, FX volatility, customer concentration loss). Then
present the **forward-looking three-year projection from the present
moment**, supported by a refreshed business plan, and frame the
marginality argument on the forward projection, not the prior one.
Adjudicators accept business reality; what they reject is silence
about the variance.

### 6.3 Beneficiary changed roles mid-cycle

**Pattern.** The Beneficiary was approved as develop-and-direct
investor (Subtype 1) but, mid-cycle, the family relocated and the
Beneficiary's spouse took over operations while the Beneficiary
focused on a parallel project.

**Resolution.** Reconcile honestly. If the Beneficiary still controls
strategy, signing authority, and has not delegated develop-and-direct
in fact, document the day-in-the-life with calendars, signed
contracts, board meeting minutes, and bank-account signatory records.
If develop-and-direct has functionally moved to the spouse, the case
may not be renewable as Subtype 1; consider re-classifying the
spouse as the principal and the original Beneficiary as derivative,
or flag for supervisor review.

### 6.4 Petitioner ownership changed mid-cycle

**Pattern.** The Beneficiary sold a portion of the Petitioner to a
new investor mid-cycle, taking the Beneficiary's stake from 60 % to
40 %.

**Resolution.** **A drop below 50 % treaty-national ownership is not a
renewable Subtype 1 case** — it is either a re-filing under a
different subtype or a non-renewal. The firm's rule: any ownership
change that crosses the 50 % threshold requires supervisor review and
typically a fresh I-129E rather than an extension. A change that
preserves the threshold (e.g., 60 % → 55 %) requires an updated
ownership exhibit and a one-paragraph cover-letter recital, not a
re-filing.
