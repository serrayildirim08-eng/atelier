## § 1 — Regulatory architecture

*Author: Prof. Mariana Suárez-Lopez · Axis: Regulatory*

### 1.1 The statute

The classification is created by **INA § 203(b)(1)(A)**, 8 U.S.C.
§ 1153(b)(1)(A). Three conjunctive elements must be met by the
"priority worker":

1. The alien has **extraordinary ability** in the sciences, arts,
   education, business, or athletics, "demonstrated by sustained
   national or international acclaim," and the achievements have been
   recognized in the field "through extensive documentation."
   (§ 203(b)(1)(A)(i)).
2. The alien **seeks to enter the United States to continue work in
   the area of extraordinary ability**. (§ 203(b)(1)(A)(ii)).
3. The alien's entry will **substantially benefit prospectively the
   United States**. (§ 203(b)(1)(A)(iii)).

The statute requires no labor certification (INA § 212(a)(5)(D)) and
permits self-petitioning (INA § 204(a)(1)(E)). The "sustained acclaim"
language is statutory, not regulatory; it cannot be diluted by
regulation or policy memorandum.

### 1.2 The implementing regulation

The implementing regulation is **8 CFR § 204.5(h)**. Its load-bearing
provisions:

- **8 CFR § 204.5(h)(2)** — defines "extraordinary ability" as "a
  level of expertise indicating that the individual is one of that
  small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of
  endeavor."
- **8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)** — the **evidentiary criteria**. A petitioner
  who has not received a qualifying **one-time achievement** must
  satisfy **at least three** of the ten enumerated criteria
  (§§ (h)(3)(i)–(x)).
- **8 CFR § 204.5(h)(4)** — **comparable evidence** clause: where a
  listed criterion does not readily apply to the occupation, the
  petitioner may submit comparable evidence.
- **8 CFR § 204.5(h)(5)** — requires evidence that the alien intends
  to continue working in the area of extraordinary ability (e.g.,
  letters from employers, contracts, statement of plans).

The ten regulatory criteria at § 204.5(h)(3)(i)–(x):

| # | 8 CFR cite | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | § 204.5(h)(3)(i) | Lesser nationally/internationally recognized prizes or awards |
| (ii) | § 204.5(h)(3)(ii) | Membership in associations requiring outstanding achievement |
| (iii) | § 204.5(h)(3)(iii) | Published material about the alien in professional/major media |
| (iv) | § 204.5(h)(3)(iv) | Participation as a judge of the work of others |
| (v) | § 204.5(h)(3)(v) | Original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business contributions of major significance |
| (vi) | § 204.5(h)(3)(vi) | Authorship of scholarly articles in professional/major-trade publications or major media |
| (vii) | § 204.5(h)(3)(vii) | Display of work at artistic exhibitions or showcases |
| (viii) | § 204.5(h)(3)(viii) | Leading or critical role for organizations of distinguished reputation |
| (ix) | § 204.5(h)(3)(ix) | High salary or remuneration in relation to others in the field |
| (x) | § 204.5(h)(3)(x) | Commercial success in the performing arts |

> **Drafting note.** The firm's intake template uses the Atelier
> 8-criterion intake taxonomy (Awards · Membership · Media · Judge · Original
> Contributions · Authorship · Critical Role · Remuneration). That
> taxonomy collapses (vii) Display and (x) Commercial Success into the
> field-specific track; (i), (ix), and — when triggered — (vii)/(x) are
> outcome criteria, while the rest are practice/recognition criteria.
> The regulatory list of ten remains controlling. See § 4 below for
> per-criterion analysis under the intake order.

### 1.3 One-time achievement

8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3) recognizes a **single dispositive route**: a
"one-time achievement (that is, a major, internationally recognized
award)." The exemplars repeatedly cited in USCIS guidance are:
**Nobel Prize, Pulitzer Prize, Academy Award (Oscar), Olympic Medal**.
A bona fide one-time achievement obviates the need to satisfy any of
the ten criteria. The bar is intentionally narrow; lesser prestigious
awards travel on criterion (i) and require triangulation.

### 1.4 Policy Manual layer

Adjudicative interpretation is housed in the **USCIS Policy Manual,
Volume 6, Part F, Chapter 2** ("Extraordinary Ability"). The chapter
governs:

- The **two-step adjudication framework** (§ 1.5 below) codifying
  *Kazarian*.
- **Per-criterion examples and limiting principles** for each
  § 204.5(h)(3) factor.
- **December 2020 update** introducing the appendix with criterion-by-
  criterion examples.
- **2024 STEM-specific guidance** (PM Vol. 6, Pt. F, Ch. 2,
  Appendix) clarifying that letters from interested government
  agencies and STEM-specific evidence (e.g., peer-review service for
  STEM journals) carry weight where the field's conventions warrant.

The Policy Manual is **interpretive guidance**, not a regulation; it
does not bind a court and does not extend regulatory authority.
Post-*Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, 144 S. Ct. 2244 (2024),
*Chevron* deference is no longer available; the agency's
interpretation receives only *Skidmore* weight to the extent of its
"power to persuade." Practitioners should preserve the distinction
between regulatory text (binding) and PM examples (persuasive). PM
examples remain persuasive under *Skidmore* and should be cited in
cover letters where they corroborate the regulatory text.

### 1.5 The two-step framework (regulatory codification)

Following the Ninth Circuit's decision in *Kazarian v. USCIS*, 596
F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010), USCIS adopted a **bifurcated review**:

- **Step 1 — Evidentiary review.** Does the petitioner satisfy at
  least three of the ten regulatory criteria (or one-time
  achievement)?
- **Step 2 — Final-merits determination.** Considering all the
  evidence in totality, has the petitioner demonstrated sustained
  national or international acclaim and rise to "the very top of the
  field"?

The two steps are **separate and sequential**. USCIS may **not**
import a final-merits judgment ("this evidence isn't impressive
enough") into the count of criteria at Step 1. See PM Vol. 6, Pt. F,
Ch. 2(B). This anti-conflation rule is the single most important
doctrinal feature of EB-1A practice.

### 1.6 Discretion mapping

| Provision | Mandatory / Discretionary |
|---|---|
| INA § 203(b)(1)(A) elements | Mandatory (statutory) |
| 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3) criteria | Mandatory list; satisfaction is a question of fact |
| 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(4) comparable evidence | Mandatory consideration where occupation does not fit a listed criterion |
| Final merits "very top of field" | Discretionary fact-finding within statutory limits |

USCIS retains discretion at Step 2 but **not** the discretion to
reject qualifying evidence at Step 1 on subjective-impressiveness
grounds. The agency carries the burden of articulating its Step-2
rationale; *Kazarian* and the PM forbid silent re-weighting.

## § 2 — The *Kazarian* two-step

*Author: Prof. Rüştü Yılmaz · Axis: AAO + case law*

### 2.1 The decision

*Kazarian v. USCIS*, **596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010)** (amending
*Kazarian v. USCIS*, 580 F.3d 1030 (9th Cir. 2009)). Petitioner was a
theoretical physicist denied EB-1A. The Ninth Circuit reversed in
part, holding that USCIS had **conflated** the evidentiary count of
8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3) criteria with the final-merits assessment of
"sustained acclaim."

The court's holding (paraphrased and bracketed quotation, *Kazarian*,
596 F.3d at 1122): the regulation supplies "a one-time achievement or
… at least three of the listed criteria" as the **threshold for
consideration**, and the agency's "ultimate question" of acclaim must
then be resolved on the totality of evidence — not by re-importing
qualitative impressions into the criterion count.

### 2.2 The two-step framework codified

USCIS adopted the *Kazarian* framework agency-wide via the December
2010 policy memorandum, "Evaluation of Evidence Submitted with Certain
Form I-140 Petitions" (PM-602-0005.1), now embedded in **PM Vol. 6,
Pt. F, Ch. 2**. The framework:

| Step | Question | Type | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Evidentiary | Has petitioner submitted **objective evidence** satisfying at least 3 of 10 criteria (or one-time achievement)? | Categorical | Petitioner failed to meet a threshold criterion |
| 2. Final merits | Does the totality of evidence demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim" and rise to "the very top"? | Holistic | Quantitative threshold met but qualitative acclaim lacking |

The two questions are answered separately, in order. Step 1 is
**existence**; Step 2 is **significance**.

### 2.3 Why the bifurcation matters strategically

1. **It forces USCIS to articulate Step-2 rationale.** A denial that
   recites "this article was published in a low-impact journal" at
   Step 1 is reviewable error. The article either qualifies as
   "scholarly" under (h)(3)(vi) or it does not; impact-factor analysis
   belongs at Step 2.
2. **It opens an appeal lane.** AAO routinely remands or sustains
   petitions where the Service Center conflated steps. Conflation is
   the single most-cited error on AAO sustained appeals from EB-1A
   denials in the trailing 5-year window (see § 3.2 share-of-failure table).
3. **It dictates packet architecture.** The cover letter should
   present each criterion with a "qualifies because…" finding (Step 1
   posture), then a separate consolidated section arguing acclaim and
   "very top" (Step 2 posture). Mixing them invites the same
   conflation the regulation forbids.

### 2.4 Federal-court reception

The *Kazarian* framework has been **adopted by every circuit** to
address it. Notable echoes:

- *Rijal v. USCIS*, 772 F. Supp. 2d 1339 (W.D. Wash. 2011), *aff'd*,
  683 F.3d 1030 (9th Cir. 2012) — applies *Kazarian* two-step;
  declines to disturb USCIS Step-2 determination supported by record.
- *Visinscaia v. Beers*, 4 F. Supp. 3d 126 (D.D.C. 2013) — articulates
  the "two-part inquiry"; emphasizes that comparable evidence under
  (h)(4) does not lower the threshold.
- *Amin v. Mayorkas*, 24 F.4th 383 (5th Cir. 2022) — applies APA
  arbitrary-and-capricious review; sustains denial where Step-2
  reasoning was "reasonable and discernible."

No circuit has rejected *Kazarian*. The threshold question of
deference post-*Loper Bright* (2024) does not displace *Kazarian*
because the framework reflects the **regulation's own structure**,
not an interpretive gloss; it survives *Loper Bright* on regulatory-
text grounds.

## § 3 — AAO + key precedent

*Author: Prof. Rüştü Yılmaz · Axis: AAO + case law*

### 3.1 BIA / agency precedent decisions cited in EB-1A

The EB-1A regulation does not have its own published BIA precedent
fleet (the BIA does not generally hear I-140 employment-based denials;
those run to the AAO). The two **agency precedent decisions** that
adjudicators most often cite in EB-1A reasoning:

- ***Matter of Price*, 20 I&N Dec. 953 (Acting Assoc. Comm'r 1994)**
  — professional golfer; the decision is one of the few
  precedent-level treatments of EB-1A. Holding: a golfer with multiple
  PGA Tour victories and $700k-plus single-season earnings met
  extraordinary ability. *Price* is cited for two propositions:
  (i) prizes won in events open to elite competition can satisfy
  (h)(3)(i); (ii) the "very top" is a small percentage, not the
  literal apex.
- ***Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010)** — though arising
  in the N-470 context, *Chawathe* is the **agency precedent on burden
  of proof**: petitioner must prove eligibility by a **preponderance
  of the evidence**. AAO routinely cites *Chawathe* in EB-1A denials
  to clarify that "more likely than not" — not "clearly and
  convincingly" — is the standard. *Chawathe* is the rebuttal cite
  whenever an RFE imports a higher burden.

Adjudicators also cite **Matter of Caron International, Inc.**, 19
I&N Dec. 791 (Comm'r 1988) for the principle that the agency is not
bound to credit conclusory expert opinion — used (often
inappropriately) to discount EB-1A recommendation letters. *Open
research item:* assemble the firm's defensive cite-package for
rebutting Caron-style discounting (independence + specificity
standard from PM and AAO non-precedent decisions).

### 3.2 AAO non-precedent landscape

The AAO publishes its non-precedent decisions on the USCIS website
(searchable by classification). For EB-1A, the trailing-5-year corpus
(2020-Q1 through 2025-Q1) breaks roughly as follows by criterion-of-
contention (per the firm's empirical scrape, normalized to denial
notices that articulated which criterion(s) failed):

| Criterion | Approximate share of articulated failure points |
|---|---|
| (h)(3)(v) Original contributions of major significance | ~28 % |
| (h)(3)(viii) Leading or critical role | ~22 % |
| (h)(3)(vi) Authorship of scholarly articles | ~14 % |
| (h)(3)(i) Awards | ~10 % |
| (h)(3)(iii) Published material about the alien | ~8 % |
| (h)(3)(ii) Membership | ~7 % |
| (h)(3)(iv) Judging | ~5 % |
| (h)(3)(ix) High remuneration | ~4 % |
| (h)(3)(vii) / (x) Display / commercial success | ~2 % |

Step-2 final-merits-only denials (Step-1 met, Step-2 lost) account
for a meaningful subset — roughly **18–22 %** of articulated denials
in the same window. This is the population that wins on AAO appeal
most often when conflation is shown.

### 3.3 Most-failed criteria — pattern notes

- **(v) Original contributions of major significance.** Failure mode:
  petitioner shows the contribution exists but not that it has been
  **adopted, cited, replicated, or implemented** by independent
  third parties. AAO consistently requires evidence of *impact*, not
  novelty.
- **(viii) Leading or critical role.** Failure mode: petitioner shows
  the role but not the **distinguished reputation** of the
  organization, *or* shows the organization's reputation but not how
  the role was leading/critical (org chart silence; absence of
  testimonial from senior leadership outside the petitioner's
  reporting line).
- **(vi) Authorship.** Failure mode: publications exist but are not
  **scholarly** in the (h)(3)(vi) sense, or appear in venues USCIS
  treats as non-qualifying (predatory journals, conference posters
  without peer review, internal reports).

### 3.4 Recent AAO posture (2023-2025)

- The 2024 PM update on STEM cases (cited at § 1.4) has produced a
  measurable softening on **letters from interested government
  agencies** (NIH, DOE, NSF program officers). AAO sustainings of
  prior denials citing these letters have increased.
- Dance / performing-arts petitions: AAO continues to apply (h)(3)(x)
  commercial success rigorously; box-office data alone is rarely
  sufficient without third-party media coverage.
- Business/entrepreneur petitions: AAO has increased scrutiny of
  founder-CEO "critical role" claims where the organization is
  founded *by* the petitioner; the firm's defensive posture is to
  triangulate distinguished reputation through external indicators
  (funding, press, partnerships) rather than self-attribution.

### 3.5 Circuit splits

There is **no acknowledged circuit split** on the *Kazarian*
framework itself. There is variation on **APA review intensity** of
final-merits determinations: the Fifth Circuit (*Amin*) and D.C.
Circuit are more deferential; the Ninth Circuit reviews more
searchingly. Forum selection in any subsequent federal action will
turn on residence and the AAO record.

## § 4 — Per-criterion analytical playbook

*Author: Karen Whitfield, Esq. · Axis: Practical execution*

This section walks the **Atelier 8-criterion intake order**.
Each entry maps to the underlying 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3) provision,
states what counts, what doesn't, the AAO traps, and the evidence
quality bar.

### 4.1 Awards — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(i)

**What counts.** Lesser nationally or internationally recognized
prizes or awards for excellence in the field. The award must be (a)
for **excellence**, (b) **nationally or internationally recognized**,
(c) in the petitioner's **field**.

**What doesn't.**
- Academic scholarships and study grants (purpose: education, not
  recognition of excellence).
- Local / regional awards without national reach.
- Certificates of participation, employer-internal "employee of the
  year" awards.
- Awards limited to students or trainees (the *competitive pool*
  must include established practitioners).

**AAO traps.**
- **Pool-of-competitors silence.** The record must show the award is
  open to and competed for by accomplished practitioners, not merely
  novices.
- **Conferring-body credibility silence.** Who issued the award?
  Independent industry body vs. promotional outlet — document the
  conferring entity's standing.

**Evidence quality bar.** Award certificate + selection criteria +
prior recipients list (showing accomplished pool) + media coverage of
the award itself. Three-source minimum per award.

### 4.2 Membership — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(ii)

**What counts.** Membership in associations that **require outstanding
achievement** of their members, judged by recognized national or
international experts, in the field.

**What doesn't.**
- Pay-to-join societies (any membership available on payment of dues
  and a basic credential).
- Honorary memberships without disclosed selection criteria.
- "Senior member" or "Fellow" grades that flow automatically from
  years of service without merit review.

**AAO traps.**
- **Selection-criteria silence.** The bylaws or membership
  application materials must show outstanding achievement is required
  *and* judged by experts.
- **Wrong tier.** The petitioner's *grade* of membership must be the
  selective grade, not the entry tier. (E.g., IEEE Senior Member
  qualifies; ordinary IEEE Member does not.)

**Evidence quality bar.** Bylaws excerpt + membership-criteria
publication + letter from association confirming petitioner's grade
and basis for elevation. Independent testimony if available.

### 4.3 Media — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(iii)

**What counts.** Published material **about the alien** in
professional or major trade publications, or other major media,
relating to the alien's work in the field. Must include the **title,
date, and author** of the material; foreign-language items must be
translated.

**What doesn't.**
- Articles authored *by* the petitioner (those route to (vi)
  Authorship, not (iii) Media).
- Listings, mentions, or directory entries.
- Self-published or company-controlled press (e.g., the petitioner's
  own employer's blog, unless the employer qualifies as major media
  in its own right and the article is independent journalism).
- Press releases issued by or on behalf of the petitioner.

**AAO traps.**
- **About vs. by.** AAO frequently rejects media evidence because the
  petitioner is the *author*, not the *subject*.
- **Circulation silence.** The publication's reach (circulation,
  readership, geographic scope) must be in the record.

**Evidence quality bar.** Article copy + circulation/readership data
for the venue + (if foreign) certified translation per 8 CFR
§ 103.2(b)(3) + author byline confirmation.

### 4.4 Judge — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(iv)

**What counts.** Participation, either individually or on a panel, as
a **judge of the work of others** in the same or an allied field. Peer
review, jury duty in competitions, dissertation committee service,
grant review panels.

**What doesn't.**
- Judging within an internal employer process where the petitioner
  evaluates direct reports (that's management, not field-level
  peer review).
- Invitations to judge that were not accepted / completed.
- Editorial positions without documented review activity.

**AAO traps.**
- **Documentation thinness.** The record must show the *act* of
  judging, not just the invitation. Manuscript-review confirmations
  from journal editors, completed-review certificates, panel rosters
  with dates.
- **Field-allied-ness.** If judging in an allied field, defensive
  paragraph explaining the relationship.

**Evidence quality bar.** Editor letter or platform record (Publons /
ORCID review profile) + dated review confirmations + 5-10 review
threshold preferred for sustained judging claim.

### 4.5 Original Contributions — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(v)

**What counts.** Original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic,
or business-related contributions of **major significance** to the
field. The criterion has two prongs: (a) originality; (b) major
significance to the field at large.

**What doesn't.**
- Contributions internal to one employer (no field-level reach).
- Novel work without demonstrated *adoption* (citations, replication,
  industry adoption, regulatory uptake).
- Patents without evidence of commercial implementation or
  field-influencing licensing.
- Conference presentations alone (originality possible, but
  significance not yet established).

**AAO traps.** This is the **most-failed criterion** (~28 % of
articulated AAO failures). Failure mode: petitioner shows the work
exists but cannot show **third-party impact**.
- **Self-attribution loop.** Petitioner's own letters claiming
  significance are discounted; independent testimony is required.
- **Citation count without analysis.** Raw citations don't establish
  significance unless contextualized (h-index relative to field,
  highly-cited threshold, citing-author independence).

**Evidence quality bar.** Independent expert letters (5-7) from
non-collaborators in the field, addressing **specific contribution**
and **specific impact** + objective metrics (citations, adoption,
licensing, regulatory implementation, replication studies) +
intermediate-distance reviewers (not the petitioner's PhD advisor or
direct co-authors).

### 4.6 Authorship — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(vi)

**What counts.** Authorship of **scholarly articles** in the field,
in professional or major trade publications, or other major media.
"Scholarly" means written for learned persons in the field; typically
peer-reviewed but not exclusively so.

**What doesn't.**
- Posters and abstracts (pre-publication; not full articles).
- Conference proceedings without peer review.
- Predatory-journal publications (USCIS maintains an informal list
  of disfavored venues; Beall's List remains a reference).
- Trade-press Op-Eds without analytical depth.
- Internal corporate white papers.

**AAO traps.**
- **Venue scrutiny.** Journal must be *scholarly* — peer-reviewed,
  recognized impact factor (or field-equivalent metric), independent
  editorial board.
- **Authorship-position silence.** If petitioner is a non-first /
  non-corresponding author, defensive paragraph explaining
  contribution percentage and field-conventions on author order.

**Evidence quality bar.** Publication list with venue metadata
(journal name, impact factor or h5-index, publisher, peer-review
confirmation) + sample articles + Google Scholar / ORCID profile +
explanation of authorship conventions where relevant (STEM vs.
humanities vs. arts).

### 4.7 Critical Role — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(viii)

**What counts.** A **leading or critical role** for an organization or
establishment of **distinguished reputation**. Two prongs: (a) the
role is leading or critical; (b) the organization is distinguished.

**What doesn't.**
- Title alone (a "Senior Director" title without role-evidence).
- Role at an unknown or non-distinguished organization (even if the
  role is significant).
- Founder-CEO claims at a startup where distinguished reputation is
  not yet established.
- Time-served claims without role-influence documentation.

**AAO traps.** Second-most-failed criterion (~22 %).
- **Reputation silence.** The organization's *distinguished* status
  must be established with external indicators (industry rankings,
  media coverage of the org, awards to the org, scale, regulatory
  significance).
- **Role-leadership silence.** The petitioner's specific role-
  influence must be shown via reporting structures, decisional
  authority, scope of responsibility — *and* via independent
  testimony from senior leaders **outside the petitioner's reporting
  chain**.

**Evidence quality bar.** Org chart + role description + decisional-
authority memorandum + 3-5 testimonial letters from external
stakeholders (board members, peer-organization leaders, industry
associations) + organization-reputation evidence packet.

### 4.8 Remuneration — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3)(ix)

**What counts.** A **high salary or other significantly high
remuneration** in relation to others in the field.

**What doesn't.**
- High absolute salary without comparison to field benchmarks.
- Equity grants without realized or fair-market valuation.
- One-time bonuses without baseline comparison.
- Benefits / cost-of-living-adjusted figures presented as base.

**AAO traps.**
- **Comparator silence.** The record must establish a *field*
  benchmark — BLS Occupational Employment Statistics, industry salary
  surveys, recruiter compensation reports — and place petitioner
  meaningfully above the percentile (typically 90th+).
- **Geographic-comparator mismatch.** Petitioner's overseas
  compensation must be compared to overseas peers (or converted with
  cost-of-living adjustment); a Turkey-based salary cannot be compared
  to US BLS data without normalization.

**Evidence quality bar.** Pay stubs / employment contracts + tax
returns (showing realized income) + field-benchmark report (BLS,
Robert Half, Glassdoor, industry-specific) + statistical analysis
(percentile rank). For non-US compensation: cost-of-living
normalization paragraph.

### 4.9 Two intake-omitted criteria

The intake template's 8-criterion taxonomy folds (vii) **Display**
and (x) **Commercial Success** into the field-specific stream. These
remain on the regulatory list:

- **(vii) Display.** Display of work at artistic exhibitions or
  showcases. Applies to visual artists, designers, performance
  artists. Evidence: exhibition catalog, curator statement, venue
  reputation, press coverage of the exhibition.
- **(x) Commercial Success.** Commercial successes in the performing
  arts, as shown by box-office receipts, record/cassette/CD/DVD
  sales. Applies to performing artists. Evidence: certified sales
  data, ticketing data from arenas, streaming numbers from major
  platforms with audited data, chart positions.

When a candidate's profile triggers (vii) or (x), the cover letter
adds a ninth (or tenth) criterion section and the diagnostic at § 6
adjusts numerically (3-of-N where N includes the activated
criterion).

### 4.10 Comparable evidence — 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(4)

**Trigger.** Comparable evidence is available only where a listed
criterion **does not readily apply** to the petitioner's occupation
— e.g., a discipline whose practitioners do not publish scholarly
articles, do not compete for prizes, or do not have membership
associations. *Visinscaia v. Beers* confirms (h)(4) does not lower
the threshold; it substitutes a different evidentiary surface for
one that genuinely doesn't fit.

**Structure of a comparable-evidence packet.**

1. *Inapplicability paragraph.* Affirmatively explain why the
   listed criterion does not readily apply to this occupation
   (cite field conventions, structural absence of the criterion,
   not mere thinness of the petitioner's record).
2. *Substitute-evidence proposal.* Identify the comparable
   indicator (e.g., principal-dancer billing in lieu of (vi)
   Authorship; chef Michelin-star inclusion in lieu of (i) Awards
   where the field's awards architecture is non-standard).
3. *Equivalence argument.* Tie the substitute back to the
   regulatory purpose of the original criterion (recognition of
   excellence, peer endorsement, distinguished reputation).
4. *Supporting documentation.* Independent evidence the substitute
   indicator carries the equivalent weight (industry recognition,
   media coverage, expert testimony on field conventions).
5. *Defensive cite.* *Visinscaia*, plus PM Vol. 6, Pt. F, Ch. 2
   discussion of (h)(4).

**Failure modes.** (a) Using (h)(4) as a thin-record workaround
where the listed criterion *does* apply but the evidence is weak
— AAO will reject. (b) Failing to brief inapplicability
explicitly. (c) Substituting evidence whose equivalence to the
original criterion's regulatory purpose is not argued.

## § 5 — Edge cases + defensive paragraphs

*Author: Levent Demir, Esq. · Axis: Edge cases + defensive framework*

### 5.1 Turkish / non-Anglophone candidates

**Translation discipline (8 CFR § 103.2(b)(3)).** Every foreign-
language document must include a complete English translation
**plus** the translator's certification of competence and accuracy.
Defensive practice:

- Use a single translation vendor across the file for consistency.
- Translator certifications go behind each translated document, not
  consolidated.
- For Turkish documents (TÜFE/TEFE indices, Bordro, SGK records,
  YÖK diploma equivalency), include a **defensive paragraph** in the
  cover letter explaining the Turkish-jurisdictional document type.

**Cross-jurisdictional credentials.** Turkish PhD, German Habilitation,
French *agrégation* — none have a US 1:1. Use a credential evaluator
(WES, Educational Credential Evaluators) to map to a US equivalent
**and** include a defensive paragraph explaining the source-country
convention. Drop-in template:

> "Beneficiary holds a [foreign credential] from [institution],
> issued [ISO date]. In [source country] practice, this credential
> requires [specific requirements] and is the standard pathway for
> [career outcome]; it is the substantive equivalent of a [US
> equivalent] (Exhibit X.Y, credential evaluation by [evaluator])."

**Foreign-language scholarly publications.** Articles in Turkish,
German, Japanese, Mandarin, etc. must travel with certified
translations. Defensive paragraph addresses (a) the journal's status
in the source country, (b) the field's use of non-English venues, and
(c) the petitioner's complementary English-language work.

**Per-document mini-templates (Türkiye).**

- *SGK service record (Hizmet Dökümü).* "Exhibit X.Y is the
  beneficiary's official social-security service record issued by
  the Sosyal Güvenlik Kurumu (SGK), the Republic of Türkiye's
  consolidated social-security authority. The record is the
  authoritative government source for employment dates and employer
  identity in Türkiye, used in Turkish litigation and benefit
  adjudication."
- *Bordro (payroll slip).* "Exhibit X.Y is a Bordro — a
  per-period Turkish payroll statement showing gross pay,
  statutory deductions (SGK, income tax, stamp tax), and net pay.
  Bordro is the standard documentary evidence of compensation in
  Türkiye and is generated by the employer's payroll system."
- *YÖK denklik (diploma equivalency).* "Exhibit X.Y is a denklik
  certificate issued by the Yükseköğretim Kurulu (YÖK), the Council
  of Higher Education of Türkiye. YÖK denklik is the authoritative
  Turkish recognition of foreign degrees; here it confirms the
  beneficiary's [degree] is recognized at the [Turkish equivalent]
  level."
- *TÜBİTAK award.* "Exhibit X.Y documents an award conferred by the
  Türkiye Bilimsel ve Teknolojik Araştırma Kurumu (TÜBİTAK), the
  Republic of Türkiye's national scientific and technological
  research council. TÜBİTAK awards are nationally recognized in
  Türkiye and selected by expert panels per published criteria."

### 5.2 STEM vs. arts vs. business — divergent evidence priors

| Field | Strongest criteria | Weakest criteria | Field-specific notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| STEM (academic) | (v) Original contributions; (vi) Authorship; (iv) Judging | (vii) Display; (x) Commercial success | 2024 PM update favors interested-government-agency letters |
| STEM (industry) | (v) Original contributions; (viii) Critical role; (ix) Remuneration | (vi) Authorship (often thin); (iv) Judging | Patents matter only with implementation evidence |
| Arts (fine arts) | (vii) Display; (iii) Media; (i) Awards | (vi) Authorship; (ix) Remuneration | Curator letters > collector testimony |
| Arts (performing) | (x) Commercial success; (iii) Media; (i) Awards | (v) Original contributions; (vi) Authorship | Box-office requires audited data |
| Business / entrepreneur | (viii) Critical role; (ix) Remuneration; (iii) Media | (vi) Authorship; (iv) Judging | Founder claims need external triangulation |
| Athletics | (i) Awards; (x) (analog of) Commercial success; (iii) Media | (vi) Authorship; (iv) Judging | Tour-level competition documentation |

The Atelier 8-criterion intake order works for all six
profiles; only the *evidentiary weight* shifts.

### 5.3 Dual-intent and underlying-status overlap

EB-1A is **not** subject to the dual-intent ambiguity that complicates
F-1 / J-1 / B-1/B-2 transitions. Once the I-140 is filed, the
beneficiary is permitted to seek consular processing or AOS without
the non-immigrant-status compromise issues that affect H-1B and L-1.
Defensive note: a beneficiary in B-1/B-2 status filing AOS
contemporaneously with I-140 raises **preconceived intent** scrutiny;
file the I-140 first and let it pend before filing I-485.

### 5.4 AOS vs. consular processing

| Factor | AOS (I-485) | Consular (DS-260) |
|---|---|---|
| Beneficiary location | In US, eligible to adjust | Outside US or ineligible to adjust |
| EAD/AP availability | Yes (I-765/I-131 concurrent) | No |
| Interview | Often waived for I-485 EB-1A | Required at consulate |
| Timeline | Tied to USCIS workload | Tied to DOS/post workload |
| Risk profile | NTA risk if I-140 denied with no other status | Travel-and-return logistics |
| Best for | Beneficiary with stable underlying NIV | Beneficiary abroad or in NIV gap |

For Turkish nationals processing in Ankara or Istanbul, consular
posts are generally efficient; AOS only when the beneficiary is
already lawfully present and wants the EAD bridge.

### 5.5 Prior-denial recovery

A prior EB-1A denial does not bar refiling. Strategy:

1. **Pull the prior denial notice and AAO decision (if appealed).**
   Identify which criteria were rejected and the articulated
   rationale (Step-1 categorical vs. Step-2 holistic).
2. **Address each prior failure point head-on.** New evidence per
   criterion, plus a **gap-closing paragraph** in the cover letter
   explicitly mapping prior-denial reasoning to new evidence.
3. **Refile with new I-140.** Same priority date is **not**
   recapturable from a denied I-140. (Priority date capture rules
   apply only to revoked-after-approval cases — 8 CFR § 204.5(e).)
4. **Consider sequencing.** If the prior denial was Step-2, build the
   new file with reweighted evidence; if Step-1, new criterion
   coverage is the priority.

### 5.6 Edge-case registry — initial entries (EB-1A)

| Pattern | Defensive posture |
|---|---|
| Beneficiary holds a major international award conferred by a country that the US does not recognize as an issuing authority of distinguished prizes | Treat as (h)(3)(i) lesser award; supplement with field-recognition evidence; do not claim one-time achievement |
| Field-of-extraordinary-ability shift between past acclaim and prospective US work | Defensive paragraph linking the two as a single "area of expertise" per § 203(b)(1)(A)(ii); see PM Vol. 6 Pt. F Ch. 2 |
| Co-authored work where petitioner is mid-list author | Authorship-convention defensive paragraph + co-author non-collaboration letters |
| Prior O-1 approval as evidentiary anchor | Cite as supporting, not dispositive; AAO has explicitly rejected "O-1 approved → EB-1A approved" reasoning |
| Beneficiary's CV gap of 12+ months | Defensive paragraph explaining (parental leave, sabbatical, geographic relocation) — pre-empt "sustained acclaim" challenge |
| Field is so narrow that only 2-3 conferring bodies exist | Comparable evidence under 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(4); document the field's scarcity |

## § 6 — Diagnostic

*Consolidator: 30-point pre-filing checklist.*

### 6.1 Step-1 numeric threshold (10 points)

1. Does the file include a **one-time achievement** under 8 CFR
   § 204.5(h)(3)? If yes, the diagnostic skips to § 6.2.
2. Are at least **3 of 10 regulatory criteria** affirmatively
   pleaded in the cover letter?
3. Is each pleaded criterion supported by **independent, third-party
   evidence** (not solely petitioner's own statements)?
4. For each pleaded criterion, is there a **"qualifies because"
   finding paragraph** in the cover letter, followed by exhibit
   citations?
5. Is the regulatory text of each pleaded criterion **quoted
   verbatim** in the cover letter section heading?
6. Are the **two most-failed criteria** ((v) Original contributions
   and (viii) Critical role), if pleaded, supported by **at least 5
   independent expert letters**?
7. For (v), does the file show **third-party adoption / impact**
   (citations, replication, regulatory uptake, industry adoption) —
   not just novelty?
8. For (viii), is the **organization's distinguished reputation**
   established with external evidence (rankings, media, awards,
   regulatory significance)?
9. For (vi) Authorship, are venues confirmed **scholarly** (peer-
   reviewed, recognized impact metric, independent editorial board)?
10. For (i) Awards, are **selection criteria, conferring body
    credibility, and competitor pool** documented per award?
10b. If (vii) Display or (x) Commercial Success is pleaded, are
    display-/commercial-success-specific evidentiary anchors
    documented (exhibition catalog + venue reputation + press for
    (vii); audited sales / ticketing / chart positions for (x))?

### 6.2 Step-2 final-merits qualitative defense (10 points)

11. Does the cover letter contain a **separate, dedicated final-
    merits section** distinct from the criterion-count?
12. Does the final-merits section affirmatively argue **sustained
    national or international acclaim**?
13. Does the file demonstrate **rise to the very top of the field**
    (small percentage, not literal apex per *Matter of Price*)?
14. Are the **prospective US benefit** elements pleaded per INA
    § 203(b)(1)(A)(iii)?
15. Are the **continued-work** elements pleaded per § 203(b)(1)(A)(ii)
    and 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(5)?
16. Is there an **expert-letter triangulation** — at least 7-10
    letters spanning collaborators, intermediate-distance peers, and
    independent authorities?
17. Are the expert letters **substantively distinct** (not template
    variants) and authored by individuals whose credentials are in
    the file?
18. Does the file include **recent activity** (within trailing 24-36
    months) demonstrating *sustained* (not historical) acclaim?
19. Is the cover letter **pre-armed with *Chawathe*** —
    preponderance-of-the-evidence standard cited up front so the
    burden frame is set before any RFE? (RFE-stage *Chawathe*
    deployment lives on the separate RFE-response checklist.)
20a. Does the **cover letter** state a single, narrowly drawn
    field-of-extraordinary-ability definition?
20b. Do **all expert letters** use the same field definition as the
    cover letter, with no drift across letters or between letters
    and the intake?

### 6.3 Defensive and procedural (10 points)

21. Are all foreign-language documents accompanied by **certified
    translations** per 8 CFR § 103.2(b)(3)?
22. Are **Turkish-jurisdictional** or other non-US documents
    introduced with **defensive paragraphs**?
23. Is the **credential evaluation** present where any non-US degree
    or credential is cited?
24. For non-US compensation claims under (ix), is there a **cost-of-
    living normalization** paragraph?
25. Is the **AOS vs. consular** routing decision documented and
    matched to beneficiary's status?
26. Is the **Form G-28** properly executed and signed?
27. Is the **Form I-140** signed, dated, with correct fee and
    classification box?
28. Is **premium processing** (Form I-907) elected or expressly
    declined with rationale?
29. Has the file been **counter-read by a second attorney** for
    Step-1 / Step-2 conflation hazards?
30. Has the **Supervisor (Serra Yıldırım)** signed off on the cover
    letter and exhibit index before filing?

### 6.4 Failure-mode triage

If fewer than 27 of 30 are affirmative, do not file. Triage:

- **Step-1 deficits (items 1-10).** Add criterion coverage. The
  marginal gain from a thin 4th criterion exceeds the marginal gain
  from a 9th expert letter on a strong criterion.
- **Step-2 deficits (items 11-20).** Add independent triangulation.
  Recommendation letters from individuals at distance from the
  petitioner are higher-value than additional letters from
  collaborators.
- **Defensive deficits (items 21-30).** Procedural; rectify
  immediately. These are unforced errors.

The diagnostic is run **twice**: at Day -30 (file build) and at
Day -3 (pre-filing). The Day -3 run is the gate; Supervisor sign-off
follows.
