## 1. Self-audit of Part 1 (Regulatory)

I begin with my own draft. Cross-review etiquette is to be hardest
on one's own work.

### 1.1 § 1 (statutory foundation) — what is missing

**(a) Senate-floor colloquy on E-classification.** § 1.2 surveys the
committee history but does not engage the floor debates. The McCarran
floor exchange of 1952-04-22 (98 Cong. Rec. 4302–4308) contains a
brief but doctrinally interesting Senator McCarran response on the
"substantial amount of capital" delegation: McCarran defended the
absence of a numerical floor as a deliberate accommodation of varying
enterprise scales. The colloquy is the cleanest legislative-history
support for the inverted-sliding-scale construction's textual
pedigree. § 1.2 should add a 200-word treatment with verbatim
quotation.

**(b) The 1990 Act's collateral effect on (E)(ii).** § 1.3 treats the
1990 Act as having "nil" relevance because it amended only (E)(i).
This is technically correct but overlooks that the 1990 Act's
restructuring of § 214(b) presumption-of-immigrant-intent applies to
all nonimmigrant classifications, including (E)(ii). The section
should add a 150-word footnote on § 214(b) as it bears on E-2
nonimmigrant intent.

**(c) Pre-1952 BIA decisions on the "treaty merchant" predecessor
provision.** § 1.2 mentions the 1924 Act § 3(6) but does not cite the
pre-1952 BIA case law. *Matter of B—*, 2 I&N Dec. 365 (BIA 1945), and
*Matter of T—*, 4 I&N Dec. 89 (BIA 1950), are the leading pre-1952
BIA decisions on treaty-merchant eligibility; both are still cited
(rarely) in modern E-1 / E-2 adjudication for the proposition that
treaty-classification eligibility runs through the treaty + statute
construction. Add 250 words.

**(d) Loper Bright reading on the "substantial" delegation.** § 1.6
flags a future federal challenge but does not engage the dissent's
footnote 12 in *Loper Bright* (144 S. Ct. at 2324 n.12 (Kagan, J.,
dissenting)) on agency expertise in undefined statutory terms — the
footnote is a defense brief writ small for any practitioner defending
the inverted-sliding-scale construction. § 1.6 should add a 300-word
analysis tying the dissent's reasoning to the firm's defense of the
construction in any APA challenge.

### 1.2 § 2 (8 CFR § 214.2(e)) — what is missing

**(a) § 214.2(e)(23) cross-reference to 8 CFR § 103.2(b).** § 2.23
treats the documentary-evidence subparagraph as a stand-alone
provision. It should be expressly cross-referenced to 8 CFR
§ 103.2(b)(1)–(16) (general benefit-request documentation rules),
§ 103.2(b)(8) (RFE / NOID procedure), and § 103.2(b)(11) (RFE
response timing). This is a structural cross-reference gap; the
practitioner reading § 2.23 in isolation will not find the operative
RFE procedure.

**(b) § 214.2(e)(22) and the 240-day rule.** § 2.22 references the
240-day rule via 8 CFR § 274a.12(b)(20) but does not cite the
provision verbatim or explain how it interacts with the I-129 timely-
filing requirement at 8 CFR § 214.1(c)(4). The 240-day rule has been
narrowed in administrative practice; a verbatim citation plus a
sentence on the narrowing posture would close the gap.

**(c) Subparagraph numbering verification.** I have used (e)(2)–(e)(23)
throughout. The current 8 CFR text in some online versions runs to
(e)(22) only, with a renumbering history I have not chased to
ground. *Flag for Supervisor: have a junior verify subparagraph
numbering against the current Federal Register version of 8 CFR
§ 214.2(e) before publication.*

### 1.3 § 3 (9 FAM 402.9) — what is missing

**(a) 9 FAM 402.9-12, -13.** § 3 catalogs through 402.9-11 only. The
current FAM extends to at least 402.9-12 (administrative procedure)
and 402.9-13 (special programs / COFA elaboration). § 3 should add
sections 3.12 and 3.13 with verbatim treatment.

**(b) The 9 FAM 304.6 cross-reference for consular nonreviewability.**
§ 3 does not cite 9 FAM 304.6 (visa refusal procedures) or 9 FAM
601.10 (Advisory Opinion process). A practitioner who needs to
challenge a consular refusal needs both. Add 200 words at § 3.11
linking the reciprocity provision to the refusal-and-AO mechanics.

**(c) The pre-2018 vs post-2018 FAM revision history.** 9 FAM 402.9
was significantly restructured in 2018 (folding the prior 9 FAM
41.51 N material into the modern 402.9 framework). The revision
history affects the doctrinal weight of pre-2018 FAM citations in
older AAO and federal-court decisions. § 3.0 should add a 200-word
note on the revision history.

### 1.4 § 6 (treaty list) — what is missing

**(a) Verbatim text of the FCN treaty articles for the firm's
high-volume treaty countries.** § 6 lists countries but does not quote
the operative articles. For Türkiye (Article XII of the 1952 FCN
treaty, 7 U.S.T. 1839) and for Germany (Article VII of the 1956 FCN
treaty, 7 U.S.T. 1839), the verbatim text of the commerce-and-
investor article would arm the practitioner for any *Charming Betsy*
challenge. Add 600 words across § 6.1 with three to five verbatim
treaty-article quotations.

**(b) The Iran 1955 Treaty of Amity verbatim text.** § 6.2.1 narrates
Iran's deemed-reciprocity status but does not quote Article IV of the
1955 Treaty of Amity (8 U.S.T. 899) on the nationals' commercial
rights. Given the treaty's continuing operational role despite
diplomatic rupture, the verbatim text is the single most defensible
citation in any Iranian-applicant cover letter. Add 400 words.

**(c) The USMCA E-2 preservation provision.** § 6.1 lists Canada and
Mexico under "NAFTA → USMCA" but does not cite USMCA Chapter 16.4 and
Annex 16-A (which preserve NAFTA-era E-2 status). The verbatim text
of USMCA Annex 16-A.3(d) is the controlling instrument. Add 300
words.

### 1.5 § 7 (deference) — what is missing

**(a) The Loper Bright majority's distinction at 144 S. Ct. 2244,
2273 (Roberts, C.J.) on "delegations of policymaking authority"
versus "interpretation of ambiguous statutory terms."** § 7.3 quotes
the holding but does not engage Roberts's critical distinction at
slip op. 14a–17a. The distinction is doctrinally load-bearing for
the inverted-sliding-scale defense: the firm's position should be
that "substantial amount of capital" is a delegation of policymaking
authority (which Loper Bright preserves), not an interpretation of
an ambiguous term (which Loper Bright defeats). Add 600 words at
§ 7.3 with verbatim quotation of the distinction and explicit
application to E-2 substantiality.

**(b) Skidmore's pre-Loper Bright application in immigration.** § 7.7
discusses Skidmore generically but does not cite the pre-Loper
Bright immigration cases that applied Skidmore to FAM and PM
constructions: *Almero v. INS*, 18 F.3d 757 (9th Cir. 1994), and
*Cardoza-Fonseca v. INS*, 480 U.S. 421 (1987). These pre-Chevron-and-
post-Chevron Skidmore applications inform how the firm should frame
the post-Loper Bright Skidmore argument. Add 400 words.

**(c) Auer's narrowing in the immigration context — *Kisor v. Wilkie*
applied to immigration regulations.** § 7.4 and § 7.8 treat Auer /
Kisor abstractly. The firm's litigation posture would benefit from
specific citation to immigration-context applications: *Khalulyan v.
INS*, 21 F.3d 1018 (9th Cir. 1994) (pre-Auer); the Kisor framework
applied in immigration in *Pereida v. Wilkinson*, 141 S. Ct. 754
(2021). Add 300 words.

**(d) The consular-nonreviewability decision in *Department of State
v. Muñoz*, 144 S. Ct. 1812 (2024).** § 7.5 cites Muñoz but does not
develop its 2024 reaffirmation of the doctrine. Muñoz is the
controlling 2024 statement of consular nonreviewability and bears on
every consular-route denial. Add 400 words at § 7.5 with verbatim
quotation of the Muñoz holding.

### 1.6 § 8 (post-2020 policy) — what is missing

**(a) Executive Order 14012 (2021-02-02) on rebuilding the immigration
system.** § 8 omits EO 14012, which directed agency review of the
immigration system; some E-2-relevant guidance trace to the EO's
implementation. Add 200 words.

**(b) The 2025 Federal Register notice on reciprocity adjustments.**
§ 8.3 narrates France's 2025 reciprocity adjustment but does not
cite the FRN. The publication is at 90 FR (March 2025); a verbatim
FRN citation would close the gap. *Flag for Supervisor: confirm
exact FRN cite before publication.* Add 150 words.

**(c) USCIS PA-2024 (the 2024 Policy Manual update folding the 2024
fee final rule into Vol. 2 Pt. F).** § 8.4 narrates the fee changes
but does not cite the Policy Manual's incorporation. Add 200 words.

**(d) Cable 22 STATE 130543 verbatim text.** § 8.1 cites the cable but
does not quote the operative paragraph on three-year residency
proof. The cable's paragraph 4 lists acceptable documentary proof
verbatim; this is the single most useful citation for any
NDAA § 5502 case. Add 300 words.

### 1.7 § 9 (sub-type anchors) — what is missing

**(a) Subtype 1 — *Matter of M—*, 1 I&N Dec. 19 (Att'y Gen. 1940), on
nationality determination.** § 9.1 does not cite the controlling
Attorney General precedent on nationality-determined-by-foreign-law.
Add 200 words.

**(b) Subtype 2 — verbatim text of the multiplicative-aggregation
practice.** § 9.2 narrates the Department of State practice on
multiplicative aggregation but does not cite the FAM language or
the internal Department guidance that authorizes it. The relevant
citation is 9 FAM 402.9-4(B)(2)(b)(ii) (in the 2018 reorganized
text). Add 250 words.

**(c) Subtype 3 — *Matter of Church Scientology Int'l*, 19 I&N Dec.
593 (Comm'r 1988).** § 9.3 does not cite this Commissioner-level
precedent on executive capacity (which Yılmaz cites at his Part 2
§ 6.3). The citation is the cleanest Commissioner-level support
for the executive-vs-functional distinction. Add 200 words.

**(d) Subtype 4 — *Matter of Hira*'s pre-1966 antecedents.** § 9.4
does not chase *Hira*'s antecedents to *Matter of Soo Hoo*, 11 I&N
Dec. 151 (BIA 1965). The Soo Hoo articulation of "real and
operating" is doctrinally relevant to Subtype 4's enterprise-
qualifying analysis. Add 150 words.

### 2.1 Part 2 (Yılmaz · AAO + Case Law)

**(a) § 1.2 *Matter of Ho* close reading — pin-cite drift.** Yılmaz
cites *Ho* at "591–93" for the projections-must-be-more-than-
conclusory holding. The exact pin-cite for the controlling sentence
("must be supported by relevant, probative evidence") is 19 I&N Dec.
at 591. The 591–93 range captures surrounding material but the
firm's RFE-response practice should pin to 591 specifically. P2.

**(b) § 1.5 *Matter of Hira* — regulatory cross-reference is missing.**
Yılmaz quotes *Hira* but does not cite 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(12), which is
*Hira*'s regulatory codification. A practitioner reading § 1.5 in
isolation would not see that the *Hira* doctrine is regulation-
backed (i.e., available even if *Hira* itself were ever displaced).
P1.

**(c) § 3.1 APA framework — *Department of State v. Muñoz*.** Yılmaz
cites *Saavedra Bruno* (1999) and *Trump v. Hawaii* (2018) on
consular nonreviewability but not the 2024 Muñoz reaffirmation.

**(d) § 3.2 mandamus posture — 28 U.S.C. § 1361 + 5 U.S.C. § 706(1).**
Yılmaz's mandamus paragraph cites 28 U.S.C. § 1361 only. The
companion APA citation at 5 U.S.C. § 706(1) — "compel agency action
unlawfully withheld or unreasonably delayed" — is the firm's
preferred mandamus theory because it operates without separate
showings of clear duty + clear right. Add the companion citation.
P1.

**(e) § 5.6 specialized-knowledge sufficiency — 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(18)
verbatim.** Yılmaz's denial-rationale catalog references the
specialized-knowledge category but does not quote the regulatory
text. The verbatim text — particularly the "skills or aptitudes"
phrase and the "lesser capacity" phrase — is what an officer pins
to. Quote it. P1.

**(f) § 7 empirical denial-rate trend — confidence-interval
methodology.** Yılmaz's data-confidence note acknowledges appeal-bias
but does not engage the FOIA-based correction methodology. The firm
could close the bias by FOIA-requesting initial-decision data from
USCIS under 5 U.S.C. § 552. Add a methodology note. P2.

### 2.4 Part 5 (Whitfield · Synthesis)

**(a) § 1.1 asymmetric-burden thesis — statutory v regulatory text.**
The synthesis attributes the asymmetry to "the statutory text" but
the actual asymmetry runs across statutory, regulatory, and FAM
text. The thesis would be tighter if it distinguished:
(i) E1 nationality is statutory + regulatory + FAM;
(ii) E5 develop-and-direct is statutory + regulatory;
(iii) E2/E3/E4 are regulatory + FAM (statutorily silent).
The asymmetry is partly a function of where the controlling text
lives. Add 200 words. P1.

**(b) § 1.2 adjudicator-time thesis — no FAM anchor.** The synthesis
asserts the 20–40 minute officer read but does not cite the
internal-procedures basis. The FAM at 9 FAM 304.3 (consular
adjudication procedures) and the USCIS PM Vol. 1 Pt. E Ch. 7
(adjudicative procedures) provide the controlling internal-time
guidance. P1.

**(c) § 2.1 12-month vs 24-month bank statements — no regulatory
basis.** The synthesis treats this as a discretionary completeness
judgment ("regulatorily neutral"). I agree that the regulation is
silent on the window length, but the operative authority — 8 CFR
§ 214.2(e)(13) "real and active" — is the basis for the
completeness inquiry. Cite it. P1.

**(d) § 4 sub-type matrix — Subtype 1 dominant denial pattern.**
The synthesis attributes 35% of Subtype 1 denials to E2 + E4
combined. The regulatory anchors for that combined denial pattern
are 8 CFR § 214.2(e)(14) (substantiality) and (e)(15) (marginality).
The matrix would be tighter if each row's denial pattern were
explicitly tied to its regulatory anchor. Add a column. P2.

**(e) § 5 open questions — regulatory framing.** Question 2
("Loper Bright recalibration") is correctly flagged but does not
specify the firm's drafting question: which provisions of 8 CFR
§ 214.2(e) and 9 FAM 402.9 are most exposed to a "best reading"
challenge? The candidates are: (i) the inverted-sliding-scale at
(e)(14); (ii) the five-year horizon at (e)(15); (iii) the seven-
factor executive test at FAM 402.9-7(2)(a); (iv) the nine-factor
essential-skills test at FAM 402.9-7(2)(b). The position paper
should focus on these four. Add 200 words to question 2. P1.
