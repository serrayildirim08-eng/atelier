## Why this case ACTUALLY failed (post-OCR; supersedes earlier hypotheses)

After OCR'ing NOID 1 (2024-01-09), NOID 2 (2024-02-07), and the Final Denial Decision (2025-05-10), the true fail signatures are NOT the ones I hypothesized from the RFE response cover letter alone. The actual reasons:

### REAL Fail signature 1 — B-2 status violation + unauthorized employment

USCIS Final Denial verbatim:
> "Performance of some duties for the investment enterprise can be permissible and does not constitute 'employment' if they are incidental to making the investment. However, when you perform the day-to-day functions that are essential to the enterprise's chances of success, this would constitute unauthorized employment. Therefore, it appears that you may have violated your B-2 Status."

Timeline that killed the case:
- **2022-09-23:** Beksac admitted as B-2 Visitor for Pleasure
- **2022-08-19:** Counsel asserts enterprise "fully operational since August 19, 2022" (BEFORE B-2 admission!)
- **2023-03-22:** B-2 expired
- **2023-09-22:** I-129 E-2 filed (6 MONTHS AFTER B-2 expired = 6-month status gap)

The case theory required Beksac to have been operating Flatturbo since 2022-08-19, which means he was running a business on a B-2 (visitor, no work auth). USCIS holds: full-time day-to-day operation = unauthorized employment, regardless of whether the petitioner-of-record is the operator's own LLC.

**Manual addition (proposed § 11.5 — most critical of all gates):**
- `b2_status_violation_signal` (severity 5): petitioner currently on B-2 / B-1 / ESTA AND case theory asserts "fully operational" / "actively doing business" / "performing day-to-day operations" before E-2 work authorization. BLOCK drafting.
- `status_gap_pre_filing` (severity 5): `(filed_date - prior_status_expiration_date) > 0` — i.e., petitioner is OUT OF STATUS at filing. BLOCK; require either consular pivot or extraordinary-circumstances showing under 8 CFR § 248.1(b).

### REAL Fail signature 2 — Material change between RFE response and ITD response (Matter of Izummi)

USCIS Final Denial verbatim:
> "First, in your letter from Counsel it states that you did not engage in business activities until 2023. This is a change from the assertions made in the initial filing that stated that you began your business on August 19, 2022. **Such a change from initial filing to after the ITD was issued constitutes a material change.** Moreover, an application or petition shall be denied where evidence submitted in response to a request for initial evidence does not establish filing eligibility at the time the application or petition was filed."

Cited authorities (case-fatal cites Atelier MUST not provoke):
- *Matter of Bardouille*, 18 I&N Dec. 114 (BIA 1981)
- *Matter of Drigo*, 18 I&N Dec. 223 (BIA 1982)
- *Matter of Katigbak*, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 49 (Reg. Comm'r 1971): "If the petitioner was not already eligible when the petition was filed, subsequent developments cannot retroactively establish eligibility as of the filing date."
- *Matter of Izummi*, 22 I&N Dec. 169 (Assoc. Comm'r, Examinations 1998): "A petitioner may not make material changes to a petition that has already been filed in an effort to make an apparently deficient petition conform to USCIS requirements."
- *Matter of Michelin Tire*, 17 I&N Dec. 248, 249 (Reg. Comm'r 1978).
- *Matter of Soriano*, 19 I&N Dec. 764 (BIA 1988).

**Manual addition (proposed § 11.6):**
- `material_change_in_response_to_uscis` (severity 5): when drafting an RFE/NOID response, the reviewer must compare the new factual narrative against the initial filing's narrative; any material change to a date, ownership history, business activity timeline, or operational status must be flagged for attorney review BEFORE filing the response. Better to acknowledge the deficiency than retroactively rewrite the facts.

### REAL Fail signature 3 — External evidence contradiction (the Yelp smoking gun)

However, **Yelp reviews from your business entered on January 11, 2024 by Johni S., January 12, 2024, by Rashim H., and February 05, 2024 by Eman D, indicate that your enterprise is involved in more business activities than just e-commerce as these reviews indicate that you appear to offer automotive repair services as well**. ### (Superseded) Fail signature 1 — Ownership flip-flop within 2 months

> "Mr. Engin Beksac, the Principal Investor, Founder and President of Flatturbo LLC has founded the company on June 1, 2022, in Santa Ana, CA as 100% owner... Due to difficulties in acquiring a corporate bank account due to Mr. Beksac being abroad at the time, said membership interests have been transferred to another founding Member of the Company, Mr. Andrew Weiss in accordance with the resolutions of Minutes of the Members' meeting held on July 20, 2022... [later] another Transfer agreement has been signed for the return of the 100% ownership of the Company to Mr. Engin Beksac... From August 19, 2022, and onwards, Mr. Engin Beksac has been the sole owner of the company..."

**Pattern**: 2022-06-01 (Beksac founds, 100% owner) → 2022-07-20 (transfer to Weiss) → 2022-08-19 (transfer back to Beksac). Two ownership flips in 2.5 months for "procedural" banking reasons.

**Why USCIS flags this**: ownership changes within months of an E-2 filing imply (a) the original "founder" wasn't actually in control, (b) the petitioner-of-record wasn't the actual investor, and/or (c) the operating intent was non-arm's-length. Even with attested innocent reasoning ("Beksac was abroad and couldn't open a bank account"), the visible chain of title is volatile.

**Manual addition (proposed § 5.5):** add a deterministic gate `ownership_volatility` (severity 4):
- Trigger: ownership of the Petitioner changes ≥ 2 times within the 12 months before I-129 filing
- Action: require a notarized affidavit from each prior member explaining the procedural intent + a banking-rationale letter from the bank that refused to open the corporate account

### Fail signature 2 — Circular SOF chain (loan from a co-petitioner)

> "Mr. Beksac has allocated roughly $240,000.00 to establish the operational foundation of Flatturbo LLC. These funds originate from a blend of his sales of two motorbikes, personal savings, and earnings from his previous consulting engagements and **from a loan payback from Mr. Volkan Tarlaci**."

**Pattern**: SOF includes a "loan payback" from **Volkan Tarlaci** — but **Tarlaci is the Sub-2 beneficiary in the SAME E-2 matter**. Both Beksac and Tarlaci file E-2s under Flatturbo LLC; their funds intermingle pre-filing. This is a circular flow that fails the "lawful, arm's-length, traceable" prongs of § 4.

**Why USCIS flags this**: 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) requires investment funds to be at risk of loss to the Beneficiary; if Tarlaci's "loan repayment" to Beksac is itself part of Tarlaci's E-2 capital chain, the funds become indistinguishable between the two beneficiaries. Either Beksac is funded by Tarlaci, or Tarlaci by Beksac — both cannot independently establish substantial investment.

**Manual addition (proposed § 4.6):** add a deterministic gate `co_petitioner_fund_circularity` (severity 5):
- Trigger: SOF chain references a fund movement (loan, gift, payback) involving a person who is also a petitioner / co-investor in the same matter or a related matter
- Action: BLOCK drafting; require independent SOF for each beneficiary OR formal restructuring as a single petition with one principal + one dependent
- Authority hook: 9 FAM 402.9-6(B) at-risk; 9 FAM 402.9-4(B) ≥ 50% threshold
