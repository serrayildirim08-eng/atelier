# Reviewer Panel — Moodboard

**Status:** v1.0 · 2026-04-29
**Companion:** `spec.md`, `prototype.html`

The reviewer panel sits at the **intersection of two traditions**: the modern editorial annotation interface (Aesop product detail, Kinfolk margin tipi) and the legal annotation tradition (Bluebook redline, Federal Reporter margin notes, appellate brief marginalia). The panel's grammar must read as if a senior associate's red pen has been digitized — not as a "validation pass/fail" SaaS dashboard.

---

## 1. Bluebook + appellate brief marginalia

**Borrow:** the **discipline of authority cite**. Citation glyph (smcp uppercase, narrow tracking) sits in a gutter, never inline-fluid. The body of the finding never wraps the cite; the cite has its own line and bracket.
**Don't borrow:** the dense double-column layout. We want one annotation card per finding, not a packed brief.

## 2. Aesop product detail (web)

**Borrow:** the **two-column annotation rhythm** — main reading column on left, secondary information rail on right with cream-tinted surface. The right rail never competes for attention; it complements.
**Don't borrow:** the e-commerce-specific "Add to bag" CTA pattern. No CTAs in the rail.

## 3. Kinfolk magazine — sidebar essays

**Borrow:** the **sentence-case, generous line-height** in sidebar essays. Section dividers via dinkus (· · ·) instead of underline. Italic for reflection/voice notes. Section headers in display serif but **smaller** than masthead.
**Don't borrow:** the photographic warmth (we have no photos in this panel).

## 4. Monocle — quiet data labels

**Borrow:** the **smcp uppercase mono labels** with restrained tracking. The way Monocle calls things "VOLUME, ISSUE" rather than "Vol. 12 #4" — discipline through abbreviation. We do the same with "MATERIAL", "DISPOSITIVE", "MINOR" lozenges.
**Don't borrow:** Monocle's heavy density. Our findings need air.

## 5. Cabana magazine — ornament-as-meaning

**Borrow:** the **⁂ asterism and § paragraph mark** as functional micro-glyphs. Cabana uses ornament to mark transitions and emphasis without resorting to color. We adopt asterism as the marginalia anchor (`⁂¹` `⁂²`) and § as the missing-argument gutter glyph.
**Don't borrow:** Cabana's full-page floral borders. Our ornament is restrained.

## 6. Apartamento — domesticity and warmth

**Borrow:** the **cream surface as secondary territory**. Apartamento uses warm cream for "this is a side conversation, not the main text." We do the same with the findings rail (cream) vs. the letter pane (paper). Warmth = annotation territory.
**Don't borrow:** the playful, hand-drawn typesetting flourishes.

## 7. Rauschenberg's Inferno illustrations (1958–60)

**Borrow:** the **compositional discipline** of marginalia that interpret rather than decorate the central text. Annotation is a serious dialogue with the source, not a sidebar afterthought.
**Don't borrow:** the painterly chaos. Our visual language is print-discipline.

## 8. The New Yorker — fact-check marginalia (real, internal)

**Borrow:** the **letter_excerpt / facts_value parallel quote pattern** (§3.5). The Yorker fact-check tradition pairs the published claim with the verifiable source side-by-side, in the same monospace, with quiet smcp gutters. Direct ancestor of our inconsistency card.
**Don't borrow:** the production-internal density (we can afford white space).

## 9. Field Notes / Moleskine ruling sheets

**Borrow:** the **hairline ruling, dotted vs solid distinction** for severity. Field Notes' use of solid lines for entries and dotted for "to be filled" is an exact parallel to our `not_applicable` / `data_incomplete` dotted-rule treatment.
**Don't borrow:** the kraft-paper texture. We stay on `paper-grain`.

## 10. Linear issue list — counter-reference

**Don't borrow ANYTHING.** This is the explicit anti-reference. Linear's color-coded severity dots (red P0, orange P1, etc.) are exactly what the DNA forbids. Linear's "Action Required" voice is forbidden. Linear's auto-collapsed-then-expand-on-click drawer is the *opposite* of what we do (high-severity always expanded; low-severity collapsed).
**Lesson:** every time you reach for a Linear pattern, stop and substitute typography weight + ornament density.

## 11. Sentry / Datadog dashboards — counter-reference

**Don't borrow.** Glowing red SQUARES for severity. Stack-trace dumps. "Last seen 12 minutes ago" telemetry. Our panel is the **opposite tone**: contemplative, not reactive. A finding is a *thought*, not an alert.

## 12. Nineteenth-century legal codex — printed footnotes

**Borrow:** the **superscript footnote anchor**. Marginalia anchors use mono superscript glyphs (`⁂¹`, `⁂²`) — same compositional logic as Blackstone's *Commentaries* footnotes: the anchor is in the running text, the body is below or beside, the connection is unambiguous.
**Don't borrow:** the dense ALL-FOOTNOTES-AT-PAGE-BOTTOM treatment; we use a side rail instead.

---

## Density of references

If you only have time to absorb 3 of these 12, choose:
1. **Aesop product detail** — for the two-column annotation rhythm
2. **The New Yorker fact-check pattern** — for the parallel-quote inconsistency card
3. **Cabana asterism** — for the marginalia anchor glyph

If you internalize those three, the rest follow.
